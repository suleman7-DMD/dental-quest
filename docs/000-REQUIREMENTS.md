### PE 640 CLINICAL WORKFLOW REQUIREMENTS (Per Patient):
From the PE 640 course description, every dentulous patient assigned must go through the full clinical sequence, documented properly. This isn't a checkbox — it's a standard of care requirement that affects your perio grade and graduation clearance:

#### Phase 1 — Evaluation/Examination:
- Medical history review, identify dental treatment implications
- Dental history including chief complaint
- Extraoral exam (TMJ and associated structures)
- Intraoral soft tissue exam (mucosa, muscles, lips, floor of mouth, tongue, salivary glands, palate, oropharynx)
- Tooth exam: missing teeth, restorations, caries, mobility, abutment selection, tooth position, occlusion, interdental relationships, parafunctional habits, pulpal status
- Current radiographs appropriate to diagnostic needs
- Plaque and calculus distribution noted and recorded
- Soft tissue exam associated with teeth/replacements, exudate type/presence
- Probing depths, gingival margin location, CAL, BOP — all recorded
- Mucogingival relationships: keratinized tissue deficiencies, frenulum insertions, recession
- Furcation invasions: presence, location, extent
- Additional diagnostic aids as warranted (diagnostic casts, microbial assessments, CBCT, lab tests)
- All findings recorded in chart
- Referrals documented

#### Radiographic Interpretation (student must demonstrate):
- Identify emergency conditions (severe caries, periapical radiolucencies)
- Identify presence/absence of crestal and lateral lamina dura
- Identify abnormal PDL widths
- Identify degree and pattern of bone loss (horizontal vs vertical)
- Identify root proximity problems
- Identify crown/root ratios
- Record unacceptable restorations

#### Occlusal Analysis (student must demonstrate):
- Rationale for occlusal analysis
- Inter/intra-arch relationships (Angle classification, crossbites, rotations, tipping)
- Accurately determine centric relation when indicated
- Guide patient into lateral and protrusive excursions when indicated
- Identify interferences in CR and all excursions when indicated
- Identify need for occlusal appliance when indicated

#### Diagnosis:
- Specific type, degree, and location of periodontal disease
- Acute vs chronic, localized vs generalized
- Slight/moderate/severe gingivitis and/or periodontitis
- Primary or secondary occlusal trauma
- Use current AAP/EFP classification terminology
- Soft tissue and hard tissue diagnosis
- Short and long-term prognosis

#### Treatment Plan:
- Comprehensive, sequenced treatment plan with approximate costs and completion dates
- Present to faculty AND patient
- Patient informed of: disease process, alternatives, potential complications, expected results, their responsibilities, consequences of refusing treatment

#### Phase 2 — OHI (before scaling/SRP):
- Explain plaque origin, composition, significance in lay terms
- Demonstrate proper toothbrush and floss use
- Identify where additional aids needed (floss threader, proxabrush, stimudent)
- Demonstrate use of additional aids
- Disclose and identify remaining plaque
- Record plaque score on SALUD

#### Phase 3 — Initial Therapy (Scaling/Prophy and/or SRP):
- Remove all calculus with proper instruments and working strokes
- Plane and smooth roots where indicated with proper instruments
- Perform scaling/SRP with no tissue damage
- Remove all stain and plaque
- Recontour rough/overhung restorations not being replaced
- Take plaque score of previously scaled quadrants at each subsequent SRP visit
- Modify hygiene technique accordingly

#### Phase 4 — Periodontal Re-evaluation (D0170):
- Required after completion of initial therapy before moving to restorative phase
- Timing: Gingivitis patients = 2–12 weeks post scaling/prophy; Periodontitis patients = 2–8 weeks post first quadrant SRP
- Must include: full perio re-exam, comparison to initial findings, determine outcome of therapy, determine need for further tx, establish recall interval
- Re-evaluation prior to final impression (D0170i): Required and documented before any final impression for fixed or removable prosthetics on dentate patients

**Re-eval student must demonstrate:**
- Record and compare plaque scores; have patient demonstrate OHI techniques
- Report soft tissue changes to instructor
- Describe residual soft/hard tissue problems (pockets, osseous craters, mucogingival problems, mobility)
- Re-probe the case, establish areas needing further Phase I or Phase II therapy
- If Phase II needed, determine if patient is a candidate
- Describe ideal tx plan for remaining problems
- Identify limitations and propose working tx plan
- For impression re-eval: determine if appropriate to take final impression, and if not, what actions are appropriate

#### Phase 5 — Recall/Maintenance:
- Update medical and dental history
- Radiographic review
- Extraoral and intraoral soft tissue exam
- Dental exam
- Periodontal exam
- Review plaque control effectiveness
- Remove microbial flora from sulcular/pocket areas
- Scaling/root planing where indicated
- Polish teeth
- Intervals never longer than 6 months

### PERIODONTAL PATIENT PROGRESS REPORT:
- Must be maintained up-to-date for every assigned patient
- Records each patient through all aspects of perio treatment
- Tracks dates of treatment and patient status
- Course director will periodically request it
- Used to determine compliance with division guidelines for promotion and graduation

### GRADUATION SIGN-OFF (from PE 640 — what they check):
1. Every dentulous patient assigned (transferred or new) has been diagnosed and tx planned per guidelines with proper documentation
2. All diagnosed initial perio therapy completed in proper sequence and timely manner; recall/maintenance schedules established and recorded for all patients
3. All patients who completed initial therapy are re-evaluated in proper sequence; results, treatment status, and need for further tx documented
4. All final impressions for dentate patient prosthetics have been appropriately re-evaluated and documented
5. Periodic re-eval and recall/maintenance visits completed in proper sequence for all assigned patients
6. Prior to participating in any planned surgical treatment, student demonstrates ability to assist in range of surgical procedures
7. Any diagnosed/planned perio surgical therapy referred and/or completed in proper sequence
8. All summative exams completed at or above minimum grade of 75
9. 7 surgical assists completed
10. Perio Mock exam completed
11. OHI zoom/formative and summatives completed

### PERIO GRADING (affects your grade, not just pass/fail):
- **A** = ≥92% on all summatives + ≥70% of patients through full sequence (consult → initial therapy → re-eval → recall)
- **B** = ≥85% on summatives + ≥50% of patients through full sequence
- **C** = ≥75% on summatives + ≥45% of patients through full sequence
- **D** = ≥70% on summatives + <40% through sequence
- **F** = consistently below 70% on summatives + <35% through sequence

This means it's not just about passing summatives — a percentage of your roster must be documented through the ENTIRE clinical workflow.
