Hemophilia A / B

  • A= Factor VIII deficiency (XR, 1:5000 M). B= Factor IX deficiency (XR, 1:30k M, "Christmas")
  • Cat: Disorder of Secondary Hemostasis: Clotting Cascade
  • Clinical: delayed deep bleeding joints/muscles (hemarthrosis); oral bleed after tongue bite, tooth eruption, fren-pull, ext
  • Labs: ↑PTT, nl PT, nl BT, nl plt → factor assay Dx; severity <1% / 1-5% / >5%
  • Dental Tx: factor replacement 50-100%→surgery; DDAVP mild A; TXA/ε-aminocaproic acid rinse (25% q6h 5-7d). Avoid ASA/NSAID/IM inj

Primary Hemostasis (vWD, plt)

  • Cat: Disorder of Primary Hemostasis (plt plug)
  • Mucosal bleeding first sight: gingival, epistaxis, petechiae, ecchymosis, menorrhagia
  • Labs: ↑BT/PFA-100; ± ↑PTT (vWF carries VIII); plt count ↓ (ITP) or nl (vWD)
  • vWD types: 1 (qty↓ 80%), 2 (fxn), 3 (severe); Tx DDAVP type 1, vWF-VIII concentrate type 2/3

Thrombocytopenia & Plasminogen Def

  • Plt <50k delay elective; <20k spontaneous bleed; transfuse <10-20k
  • ITP, TTP, DIC, drug-induced (HIT, quinine)
  • Plasminogen def: ligneous conjunctivitis + ligneous gingivitis (fibrin-rich nodular gingiva)

Iron Deficiency Anemia

  • Most common nutritional deficiency worldwide. Labs: ↓Hgb, ↓MCV, ↓ferritin, ↑TIBC
  • Oral: Atrophic glossitis (27%), Mucosal pallor, Xerostomia and hyposalivation, Oral candidiasis and angular cheilitis, Dysesthesia: burning sensation dysgeusia
  • Plummer-Vinson Syndrome triad = Iron deficiency anemia + Dysphagia + Upper esophageal webs. ↑esoph/pharyng SCC risk
  • Perio in IDA: Standard periodontal therapy ineffective until anemia corrected

Sickle Cell Disease (SCD)

  • HbSS; vaso-occlusive crisis. Triggers: hypoxia, dehydr, cold, stress, acidosis, infection
  • Oral: stepladder trabeculae, hair-on-end skull, delayed eruption, mandibular infarct/pulp necrosis, pallor
  • Anesthesia: Avoid vasoconstrictor-induced ischemia; Infiltration preferred over regional blocks; Ensure adequate anesthesia to minimize stress
  • Pain: SCD patients at risk for undertreatment (opioid tolerance ≠ addiction)
  • Abx ppx for surgical cases (funct asplenia); hydrate pre/post

Aplastic Anemia

  • Pancytopenia, hypocell BM. Life threatening hematologic emergency
  • Oral: petechiae, gingival bleed, candida, HSV, ulcers. Tx: imm suppr, HSCT

Neutropenia / Agranulocytosis

  • ANC tiers: Mild 1000-1500 / Moderate 500-999 / Severe <500
  • Oral lesions first manifestation: deep punched ulcers w/o erythema ring, necrotic perio
  • Dental: CBC with differential day of treatment; Coordinate with hematology; Consider antibiotic prophylaxis ANC <500; defer elective

Cyclic Neutropenia

  • ELANE mut (AD). Usually diagnosed within first year of life
  • 21-day cycles ANC <200 3-6 days; Cycle length 14-36 days 30%
  • Oral triad: recurrent ulcers + gingivitis + perio/early tooth loss. Tx: G-CSF

Leukemia (AML spotlight)

  • AML M4/M5 (monocytic): Gingival enlargement hallmark of AML — diffuse puffy blue-red, leukemic infiltrate
  • Other oral: petechiae, ulcers, spontaneous gingival bleed, chloromas, HSV/candida, LAD
  • CBC triad: anemia + thrombocytopenia + leukocytosis/leukopenia + blasts on smear. Defer elective tx till remission + clearance

ORN

  • Late RT complic; >60 Gy mandible. Mech: hypoxia-hypocell-hypovasc (Marx); newer: RT-induced fibroatrophy
  • Risk: trauma (extraction post-RT), perio, poor OH, ill-fit dentures. Mgmt: atraumatic; PENTO pentoxifyl+tocopherol±clodronate; HBO (controversial, esp. Marx protocol); resection if advanced

Pre-Tx Dental Clearance

  • RT/Chemo: ≥2wk before. IV BP/deno: ≥3wk before
  • Extract hopeless, perio compromised, pericoronitis, non-restorable caries, peri-apical pathology
  • SRP, OHI, caries control, fluoride trays (1.1% NaF) daily ×life after H&N RT

Chronic GVHD (cGVHD)

  • Oral = 2nd most common site. Features: reticular lichenoid (Wickham-like), erythema, ulcers, sclerosis/limited opening, xerostomia, mucocele. ↑oral SCC risk → biopsy persistent

Long-Term Sequelae

  • Xerostomia (irreversible if salivary gland >26 Gy), caries, trismus, dysgeusia, ORN, MRONJ, 2° malignancy, cGVHD, pediatric: growth/dental development arrest (microdontia, blunted roots, agenesis)

Triage / Oral Med Workup

  • ABC → bleeding → airway compromise (Ludwig, angio) → Dx (H&P, imaging, labs, biopsy) → refer if systemic
  • Red flags: unintentional wt loss, night sweats, persistent ulcer >2 wk, hemoptysis, dysphagia, lymphadenopathy, cranial nerve deficit

Lupus (SLE)

  • F>M 9:1, childbearing. Criteria: 4+/11 ACR (malar, discoid, photosens, oral ulcers, arthritis, serositis, renal, neuro, heme, ANA, immuno)
  • Serology: ANA (sens, not spec); Anti-dsDNA 60-80% (specific, nephritis/activity); Anti-Sm 10-30% most specific; anti-Ro/La (Sjögren, neonatal), anti-phospholipid (APS)
  • Oral: palatal ulcers (punched, central atrophy, radiating striae), oral discoid LE, LE cheilitis, xerostomia (2° Sjögren)
  • Tx: HCQ, steroids, MMF/AZA; dental — stress + infxn control; steroid supplementation only if adrenal suppressed + stress indicated

Sarcoidosis

  • Non-caseating granulomas. ↑ACE, ↑Ca (1α-hydroxylase in macrophages). Bilateral hilar LAD. Löfgren (acute: erythema nodosum + LAD + arthralgia + fever, good prognosis)
  • Oral: submucosal nodules, gingival/palatal/labial mass, xerostomia. Heerfordt (uveoparotid fever) = parotid enlarge + uveitis + facial palsy (CN VII) + fever
  • Dx: biopsy non-caseating granuloma; r/o TB/fungi; tx steroids

Amyloidosis

  • Types: AL (primary) — Most common; Light chain deposition BM plasma cells; assoc Multiple myeloma. AA (2° to chronic inflamm: RA, IBD, chronic infxn). ATTR (hereditary / senile — cardiac). Aβ2M (dialysis)
  • AL bleeding: Clotting factor X adsorbed → factor X def → ecchymoses, surgical bleeding
  • TMJ: rarely deposits in TMJ
  • Oral Manifestations: macroglossia (tongue indentations, nodules), submucosal nodules, petechiae, waxy plaques, gingival enlargement, xerostomia. Macroglossia rate: AL amyloidosis 15-20%
  • Pathognomonic sign: Periorbital purpura raccoon eyes pathognomonic (pinch/valsalva-induced)
  • Dx: biopsy (abd fat pad, minor salivary gland — high yield), Congo red stain, Apple-green birefringence pathognomonic under polarized light. SPEP/UPEP + free light chain
  • Tx: chemo ± auto-HSCT (AL); treat underlying (AA); liver Tx / tafamidis (ATTR)

Peutz-Jeghers (PJS)

  • STK11 (LKB1) mutation, AD. Perioral + oral mucosal melanotic macules (lip vermilion, buccal, perioral skin; may fade after puberty on lips but persist in mouth)
  • GI hamartomatous polyps (small bowel) → intussusception, bleeding. ↑ risk GI, breast, pancreatic, ovarian, cervical ca

Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA)

  • Small-vessel necrotizing vasculitis + necrotizing granulomas. Classic triad: upper resp (sinusitis, saddle nose, otitis) + lower resp (nodules, hemoptysis) + renal (pauci-immune RPGN)
  • Serology: PR3-ANCA c-ANCA >85%. MPO-ANCA p-ANCA less
  • Oral: "Strawberry gingivitis" — Pathognomonic; Erythematous to violaceous, Hyperplastic, Petechial stippled gingiva (most often interdental papillae, maxillary anterior); also oral ulcers, palatal necrosis, oro-antral fistula
  • Gingival biopsy rarely shows classic necrotizing granuloma + vasculitis (usually non-specific) → clinical + serology + renal/resp biopsy. Tx: cyclophos/rituxim + steroid

Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS)

  • Primary/idiopathic, ≥3 mo, no oral findings, normal labs. F>>M postmenopause; tip of tongue, lips, palate. Often + dysgeusia + xerostomia subjective
  • First line: Education + Psychological adaptation symptom improvement
  • Dr-Pt: Therapeutic Interaction ***
  • Adjuncts: Nerve Distraction · Alpha-Lipoic Acid (ALA) antiox
  • Topical: Topical Clonazepam Best Evidence (GABA-A agonist, dissolve 1 mg tab 3 min then expectorate, TID)
  • Systemic (refractory): clonazepam PO, gabapentin, TCAs (amitrip / nortrip), SNRIs (duloxetine), capsaicin rinse
  • Secondary causes (always rule out): IDA / B12 / folate / Zn def, candidiasis, hyposalivation, DM, hypothyroid, GERD, allergy/galvanic, para-func

Central Sensitization

  • Wind-up (2° dorsal horn NMDA), ↑ receptor field, chronic nociceptive input → allodynia + hyperalgesia. Basis for BMS, PTTN, TN chronicity. Tx gabapentin/pregab, TCA, SNRI

Dysgeusia & Olfaction

  • ~95% taste disorders olfactory dysfunction (retronasal) rather than gustatory receptor loss
  • Causes: Poor OH Infection Most Common · Medications Very Common (ACEi, metronidazole, clarithro, terbinafine, lithium, metformin, cisplatin, captopril) · Chemotherapy 45-84% · head trauma, post-viral (COVID), RT, Zn/B12 def, Bell's
  • Mechanism: Direct effect taste receptors zinc depletion/chelation · Indirect effect hyposalivation (↓ tastant transport to receptors)
  • Tx: Zinc Supplementation (Zinc sulfate 220mg zinc gluconate 50mg daily) · Olfactory Training — Systematic exposure rose lemon eucalyptus clove, Twice daily 3-6 months
  • Umami pearl: T1R3 SHARED subunit umami sweet (T1R1/T1R3 umami, T1R2/T1R3 sweet). Umami stimulation gustatory-salivary reflex → ↑ salivary flow → ↑ tastant delivery to ALL receptors

Sialorrhea

  • ~90% sialorrhea patients are secondary (not hypersecretion): poor oral-motor (PD, ALS, CP, stroke, ASD), swallow dysfunc, postural, meds (clozapine, pilocarpine)
  • HNC context: HNC patients 16% complain of drooling (post-resection/RT motor/sensory)
  • Tx: postural/speech therapy; anticholinergics (glycopyrrolate, scopolamine patch, atropine 1% SL drops); Botox intra-parotid/SMG; ductal relocation/ligation; RT last-resort

Anesthetic Diagnostic Challenge

  • Infiltrate/block suspected area w/ LA: pain abolished → peripheral (odontogenic, myofasc, PTTN); pain persists → central/referred/psychogenic. Useful in odontalgia vs atypical facial pain

SIB & Psych Referral

  • SIB (self-injurious beh): Lesch-Nyhan, ASD, Rett, De Lange, severe ID. SIB in ASD response to pain elsewhere (otitis, dental, GI) — always rule out pain source before behavior Dx
  • Psych referral when: Medically unexplained symptoms, somatization, functional overlay, mood/anxiety driving perception, catastrophization

AVOID RULES (DENTAL RED-LINES)

  • SCD: avoid vasoconstrictor-induced ischemia; infiltration > regional block; hydrate, O₂ nasal, warm; abx ppx (asplenia) if indicated
  • Hemophilia A/B: avoid ASA/NSAIDs/IM inj; factor 50-100% pre-op; TXA rinse; no bilateral IAN block w/o factor
  • Warfarin: INR <3.5 OK for minor surg; avoid abrupt stop; check INR ≤24 h
  • DOACs: short t½ — hold 24-48 h per CrCl + surgeon; no routine reversal for dental
  • Aspirin (mono): continue for minor dental surg (local hemostasis)
  • Dual antiplat: do not stop w/o cardiology (stent thrombosis risk)
  • Neutropenia ANC<500: abx ppx, defer elective; CBC day-of
  • Thrombocytopenia: defer elective if plt <50k; transfuse if <20k + surgery
  • IV BP / denosumab: clearance ≥3 wk pre-Rx; avoid extractions; if must, atraumatic + abx + PENTO
  • Oral BPs >4 yr: counsel MRONJ risk; prefer RCT over ext
  • Post-RT H&N ≥60 Gy mand: avoid extractions in field; RCT preferred; if ext — atraumatic + PENTO ± HBO
  • Active chemo / nadir D7-14: defer elective; no invasive during thrombocytop/neutrop
  • HSCT: clearance ≥2 wk before; no elective in first 100 d post
  • Acute leukemia induction: palliative only till remission
  • Uncontrolled DM / HbA1c >9: impaired healing — stabilize before elective
  • HTN stage 2 / BP ≥180/110: defer; limit epi 0.04 mg (2 carps 1:100k) CV pt
  • Recent MI <30 d / unstable angina: defer elective, emergency only w/ cardio
  • Pregnancy: elective 2nd trim; avoid tetracycl / metronid 1st trim / NSAID 3rd
  • Lupus on steroids: steroid supplementation only if adrenally suppressed + major surg
