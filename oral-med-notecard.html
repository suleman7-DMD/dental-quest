HEMOPHILIA (2° Hemostasis)

A=Factor VIII deficiency, B=Factor IX deficiency. Both X-linked recessive.
Disorder of Secondary Hemostasis: Clotting Cascade
Characterized by delayed, deep bleeding into joints and muscles (vs plt = immediate mucosal)
Severity: Severe <1% | Mod 1-5% | Mild 5-20% remaining factor
Factor replacement 250-1500 IU, target 50-100%. Avoid regional blocks (hematoma risk)→infiltration preferred
Emicizumab = Hem A ONLY. Bispecific Ab bridges FIXa+FX→mimics VIIIa cofactor function
Severe: 2-5 spontaneous bleeds/mo w/o ppx. Gingival bleeding w/ tooth eruption/brushing
Heme consult for: new pts, mild hemophilia, before major oral surgery

Hemostasis Toolkit

Gelfoam: neutral pH, resorbs 4-6wk, combines w/ thrombin | Surgicel: LOW pH (bacteriostatic), 1-2wk
TXA: 100mg/mL soak q6h × 2-5d | Amicar: 250mg/mL TID | Thrombin 5000 IU topical
Hemodent (AlCl) | Silver nitrate | Cautery | Bone wax | Direct pressure | Suture closed

THROMBOCYTOPENIA (1° Hemostasis)

Plt <150K/uL. Disorder of Primary Hemostasis
▸ >50K: any dental work | 50-30K: multiple ext or 3M | 30-10K: single ext (NOT 3M), transfusion helpful | <10K: DON'T (spont bleeding = emergency)
ANY <50K = extra hemostatic support. AVOID aspirin & NSAIDs
Petechiae ≤2mm | Purpura 3-10mm | Ecchymosis >10mm (flat, non-blanching)
Mucosal bleeding: often first sight of clinically apparent bleeding
Etiologies: ITP (immune), BM failure, meds, liver dz, malignancy

PLASMINOGEN DEF (Ligneous Gingivitis)

Ligneous=woody. Congenital type I. Impaired fibrinolysis→fibrin-rich pseudomembranes on mucous membranes
Marginal gingiva most affected. Thick woody yellow/white/red nodular masses. Recurrent after excision
Conjunctivitis 80% | Respiratory 16% (life-threatening airway obstruction) | Vaginal, nasal, GI less common. Congenital hydrocephalus
Lab: plasminogen activity <5-25% | Genetics: K19E mutation most common | Histo: extensive fibrinous deposits submucosa
Ryplazim (IV glu-plasminogen): reduced manifestations within 4wk. No definitive cure
Standard periodontal therapy ineffective. High recurrence post-excision
Emerging: FFP before + day after procedure | Enoxaparin subQ periop | Minimize trauma

IRON DEFICIENCY ANEMIA

Most common nutritional deficiency worldwide. Fe def causes sx even w/o anemia
OM: Atrophic glossitis (27%), mucosal pallor, xerostomia and hyposalivation, oral candidiasis and angular cheilitis, dysesthesia: burning sensation, dysgeusia
Less common: koilonychia (spoon nails), diffuse alopecia (30%), pica
Pathophys: Fe-dependent enzymes→epithelial regeneration→atrophy. Fe def impairs lymphocyte transformation→Candida
Plummer-Vinson Syndrome: Iron deficiency anemia + dysphagia + upper esophageal webs
90% middle-aged women, mean age 47. 3-15% SCC risk pharynx/esophagus. Tx: Fe supplementation. Esoph dilation if dysphagia persists. Annual upper GI endoscopy

SICKLE CELL DISEASE

Most common AR genetic dz. 100K in US. HbS→sickling under hypoxic conditions→vascular occlusion, hemolytic anemia
Dental: aseptic pulp necrosis (vital-appearing teeth, unexplained discoloration, ↑pulp calcification). Delayed eruption, ext root resorption, enamel hypomineralization
XR: "stepladder" trabecular pattern, ↓bone density, loss of lamina dura
Soft tissue: pallor, candidiasis, glossitis. Skeletal: maxillary overgrowth (BM hyperplasia→midfacial prominence), depressed nasal bridge, malocclusion
Bone: osteomyelitis of jaws (hypoxia+vascular occlusion, hard to distinguish from VOC). Mental nerve neuropathy, orofacial pain
Mgmt: Avoid vasoconstrictor-induced ischemia. Infiltration preferred over regional blocks. Ensure adequate anesthesia to minimize stress (stress→sickling). Hydration. OHI + early intervention
Pain: sickle pain ≠ normal pain. SCD patients are at risk for undertreatment and discrimination in pain management. NSAIDs+opioids. Codeine/hydrocodone reasonable. Acetaminophen if NSAIDs CI

APLASTIC ANEMIA

Pancytopenia (anemia+neutropenia+TCP) + BM hypocellularity. Insidious or abrupt onset
Life threatening hematologic emergency. Oral lesions are often the first manifestation
DUAL THREAT: bleeding (plt) + infxn (neutropenia)
Hemorrhagic: gingival bleeding (plt <25K), petechiae, ecchymoses, epistaxis
Infectious: candidiasis, viral, oral ulceration, NUG. Anemia: pallor, atrophic glossitis, fatigue
Mgmt: defer elective until stabilized, coordinate heme, plt transfusion per TCP guidelines, abx ppx for neutropenic, antifungal/antiviral as needed

NEUTROPENIA / AGRANULOCYTOSIS

ANC <1,500/uL. Mild: 1,000-1,500/uL | Moderate: 500-999/uL | Severe: <500/uL | Agranulocytosis <200 = EMERGENCY→hospitalize
Dental: >1000 safe | 500-1000 coordinate | <500 defer+abx ppx | <200 hospitalize
CBC with differential on day of treatment. Coordinate with hematology for any invasive procedures. Consider antibiotic prophylaxis for ANC <500/uL
Drug-induced most common (chemo, methimazole). Also autoimmune, infxn (EBV/parvo/HIV), nutritional (B12/folate/copper), heme malignancy
OM: oral ulceration 48% (painful irregular borders, deep necrotizing), fungal 20%, viral 15% (HSV most common). NUG/NUP
PE: fever ≥100.4°F/38°C, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly
Agranulocytosis: generalized gingival necrosis, severe mucosal ulceration, fever + sore throat
G-CSF: maintain ANC >250-1000, may give before procedures

Cyclic Neutropenia

Rare AD. ELANE gene. NO malignancy risk (unlike severe congenital). Usually diagnosed within first year of life
Characterized by ~21-day cycles of severe neutropenia (ANC <200/uL at nadir) lasting 3-6 days
Cycle length can range from 14-36 days in ~30% of patients
Completely well between episodes. 10% death untreated (Clostridium/E.coli sepsis, necrotizing enterocolitis)
Oral: >60% ulcers/gingivitis ≥5x/yr. Severe progressive perio disproportionate to age/hygiene. Alveolar bone loss
Dx: serial CBCs q2-3d × 6-8wk. Schedule procedures ANC>1000. G-CSF = mainstay. HSCT for refractory

LEUKEMIA

Most common WBC neoplasm. OM may be presenting feature or indicate relapse
AML: Gingival enlargement/hyperplasia - hallmark of AML (37-80%, soft spongy lobulated ±necrosis). Most oral findings (90%). Hemorrhagic bullae. Hepatosplenomegaly rare
ALL: children <5yr (80%). Oral less common. Hepatosplenomegaly+lymphadenopathy
OMI survival: 14.3% at 21mo. Gingival enlargement resolves with chemo
LIVER CLOT: more common in HEALTHY. DO NOT REMOVE in bleeding d/o. Avoid manipulation/spitting. Tx: topical thrombin

Bleeding D/O Workflow

Periop: CBC diff, PT/PTT/INR, bleeding hx (personal+family), current meds, coordinate heme
Universal: local hemostatic measures ALWAYS. TXA adjunct. Minimize trauma. Avoid ASA/NSAIDs. Prevention through OH
1° (plt/TCP) = immediate superficial mucosal/petechiae. 2° (factor/hemophilia) = delayed deep joints/muscles/hematomas

RT THRESHOLDS

66-70 Gy / 6-7wk = curative H&N | ≥50 Gy mandible = LIFELONG ORN risk | >26 Gy parotid = permanent xerostomia→rampant caries | >45 Gy = ototoxicity
Pre-RT clearance MANDATORY—extract BEFORE RT, not after
Modalities: EBRT (IMRT), brachytherapy, proton, SRS/Gamma Knife (no oral), TBI (all oral tissues)

cGVHD

80% oral. LP-like = diagnostic alone. Distinctive (insufficient): hyposal, mucoceles, atrophy, ulcers, pseudomembranes, sclerosis. Common (acute+chronic): gingivitis, mucositis, erythema
NIH OMS 0-15: erythema+lichenoid+mucoceles (0-3 each) + ulcers (0-6)
Tx: corticosteroids 1st line, calcineurin inhibitors, mTOR, rituximab, TKIs, ECP (cell separation→8-MOP+UVA→reinfusion)
SCC risk: RR 2.78, SIR 15.7, 35yr cumulative 3.47%. ≥15mo = 58.6×. RF: azathioprine, severe cGVHD

SARCOIDOSIS

Noncaseating granulomas, unknown cause. Lungs >90%. Peak 25-35, higher in Black pts. ~50% remission, ~1/3 chronic. Cardiac/neuro→↑mortality
SM: pulm (bilateral hilar adenopathy >90%), skin (lupus pernio, erythema nodosum), eyes (uveitis, sicca 20-25%), cardiac (arrhythmias/CM 10-20%), neuro (CN palsies, neuropathy 10-25%)
OM: nodules/ulcers/swelling (tongue/lips/gingiva/palate), alveolar bone loss/tooth mobility, parotid swelling, gingival enlargement
Oral lesions may be FIRST SIGN→always screen pulmonary
Dx: bx noncaseating (must exclude TB/fungal). CXR: bilateral hilar adenopathy = highly specific. ACE ~60% (normal ≠ r/o). CBC/CMP/Ca. Check CBC if on MTX
Tx: prednisone 20-40mg/d. Topicals NOT proven (unlike lupus!). HCQ for cutaneous/oral. MTX steroid-sparing

Peutz-Jeghers (PJS)

STK11/LKB1 mutation. Triad: mucocutaneous pigmentation + GI hamartomatous polyps + ↑cancer risk. 93% lifetime cancer (GI, breast, pancreas, ovary, lung). Intussusception, GI obstruction/bleeding
Macules 1-5mm dark blue-brown. Perioral (lips, vermilion) + intraoral (buccal, gingiva, palate). Appears infancy, before GI sx. Lip fades after puberty, intraoral persists
DDx: racial (diffuse, symmetric), Addison (generalized, systemic), Laugier-Hunziker (benign, no polyps/cancer), melanotic macule (solitary, lower lip)
Screen: upper/lower endoscopy age 8-10, q2-3yr. Breast MRI, pancreatic, gynecologic. No oral tx needed

Central Sensitization

= ↑responsiveness of central neurons to normal/subthreshold input → hyperalgesia + allodynia persisting BEYOND tissue healing. Top-down (pain inhibition) vs Bottom-up (pain facilitation)

DYSGEUSIA

~95% of taste disorders are olfactory dysfunction. True gustatory loss rare. First eval→EXAMINE NOSE
6 modalities: salty, sweet, sour, bitter, umami + oleogustus (fat)
Poor Oral Hygiene & Infection (Most Common). Medications (Very Common): Mechanism = Direct effect: taste receptors damage or dysfunction, zinc depletion or chelation. Indirect effect: hyposalivation
Drugs: ACEi, ARBs, CCBs, metronidazole, clarithromycin, FQs, antidep, anticonvulsants, chemotherapy (45-84%)
Also: hyposal, nutritional (B12, folate, copper, zinc), post-surg (3M/middle ear chorda tympani), H&N RT
Tx: Zinc Supplementation: Dose: Zinc sulfate 220 mg daily or zinc gluconate 50 mg daily | Clonazepam 0.5mg suck-spit (GABA-A on taste bud receptors) | CHX short-term only (long-term→dysgeusia!)
Olfactory Training (For Olfactory Component): Systematic exposure to strong odors (rose, lemon, eucalyptus, clove). Twice daily for 3-6 months
MSG 750mg/150g food TID: T1R3 is the SHARED subunit for both umami (T1R1/T1R3) AND sweet (T1R2/T1R3) receptors. Umami stimulation enhances gustatory-salivary reflex → increases salivary flow → improves tastant transport to ALL receptors. Chemo downregulates T1R3; MSG suppresses this

Xerostomia w/o Hyposalivation

Neuropathic: altered composition (↑Na/K/Cl/Ca/IgA/amylase) but NORMAL flow. Bidirectional sensory-dryness
Step 1: sialometry → Step 2: neuropathic meds (amitriptyline/nortriptyline) → Step 3: contributing factors (med review, screen anxiety/depression)

SIALORRHEA

Primary (rare): true ↑production. Clozapine, risperidone, lithium, heavy metals, Wilson, GERD. Normal 0.5-1.5 L/day
Secondary (~90% of sialorrhea patients): impaired clearance. CP 22%, Parkinson 74%, ALS 50%, head and neck cancer patients (16%)
Psychogenic: normal + anxiety. Compulsive spitting, "bottles of saliva"
USFR = gold standard (5-min, 0.1 mL/min normal, 0.2 g/min = significant hyposal)
Tx Primary: glycopyrrolate 1mg TID → botulinum (70% response) → surgery (4-duct ligation)
Tx Secondary: conservative (positioning, oromotor therapy) → glycopyrrolate → botulinum
Psychogenic: Do NOT Rx anticholinergics/botulinum. CBT. Treat anxiety

4 categories: Neurodevelopmental (ASD, intellectual disability, Lesch-Nyhan/Rett/Cornelia de Lange/Prader-Willi), Neurologic (coma/decerebrate/seizures/postictal/stroke/TBI — loss of masticatory control→self-inflicted oral trauma), Psychiatric (OCD/BFRBs, delusional infestation, stimulant use, NSSI, factitious), Congenital Pain Insensitivity
