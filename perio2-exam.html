ESSAY TOPIC 1: CROWN LENGTHENING
CL1According to Gargiulo et al. (1961), what is biologic width and what is the approximate total dimension from the alveolar crest to the gingival margin?
A. Zone of enamel at CEJ; 1.0mm
B. Zone of root surface coronal to alveolar crest to which CT and JE are attached; approximately 2.73mm
C. Distance from CEJ to root apex; 10mm
D. Probing depth in health; 3.5mm
E. Width of PDL space; 0.25mm
Correct Answer: B

Gargiulo et al. (1961): biologic width = "zone of root surface coronal to the alveolar crest, to which the CT and JE are attached." Total from crest to margin ≈ 2.73mm (~1.07mm CT + ~0.97mm JE + ~0.69mm sulcus). Varies by individual — why bone sounding matters before CL. Ideal distance from most apical JE cell to bone crest = 1mm (the CT attachment zone).

CL2After removing a temp, you see bleeding and ulceration. Before determining CL is needed, what should you do FIRST?
A. Schedule CL surgery immediately
B. Make a new temp, remove excess cement, reinforce OH, reassess in a few weeks
C. Extract and place implant
D. Prescribe antibiotics, reassess 6 months
E. Place final restoration immediately
Correct Answer: B

Rule out other causes first: make new temp (poor margins?), remove excess cement, reinforce OH, return in weeks. If tissue heals → problem was temp/cement/hygiene. If STILL inflamed → likely biologic width violation → CL indicated. Other causes: bad temp margins, retained cement, food impaction.

CL3Which is an INDICATION for crown lengthening?
A. Poor crown-to-root ratio
B. Tooth decay at or below gingival margin with adequate remaining support
C. Loss of periodontal support on adjacent teeth
D. Furcation at proposed margin
E. Better alternative available
Correct Answer: B

CL indications from slides: tooth decay at/below gingival margin, tooth fracture below margin (with adequate support), excessive wear, insufficient interocclusal space, delayed passive eruption. Choices A/C/D/E are all CONTRAINDICATIONS: poor crown-root ratio, adjacent tooth support loss, furcation location, better alternative, inability to maintain.

CL4When is external gingivectomy appropriate for CL versus flap + bone removal?
A. Gingivectomy always preferred
B. Gingivectomy when sufficient KT AND bone already at appropriate distance; flap + bone when bone too close to planned margin
C. Flap + bone never indicated
D. Gingivectomy for posteriors only
E. Based on patient preference
Correct Answer: B

Gingivectomy: sufficient KT + bone at appropriate distance (e.g., altered passive eruption, drug-induced hyperplasia). Flap + bone: bone too close to planned margin → need to establish biologic width. Coslet 1977 altered passive eruption: 2 types × 2 subtypes (1A, 1B, 2A, 2B). Subtype A (crest at normal distance) → may need only gingivectomy. Subtype B (crest AT CEJ) → needs osseous reduction.

CL5CL healing time: posterior vs anterior?
A. Both 4 weeks
B. Posterior (molars) 6–7 weeks; anterior ~16 weeks for esthetic margin predictability
C. Anterior faster; posterior slower
D. All require 6 months
E. Depends only on age
Correct Answer: B

First molars: 6–7 weeks then restore. Anteriors: >8 weeks, probably ~16 weeks — must know where final gingival margin will settle for esthetics. CL healing overall: 6, 8, 12 weeks or more. Bone rebound: 0.5–1.2mm over 12 months. Gingival crest esthetics: centrals + canines at same level; lateral slightly more coronal.

CL6CL complications and management?
A. No complications exist
B. Bleeding (sutures/diet), pain (NSAIDs), swelling (NSAIDs/ice), root sensitivity (peri packing, stannous fluoride, varnishes, Gluma, Super Seal)
C. Only tooth loss
D. Only permanent numbness
E. Only infection
Correct Answer: B

Four complications: Bleeding → sutures, avoid hard food. Pain → NSAIDs/analgesics. Swelling → NSAIDs/ice. Root sensitivity → peri packing post-op, then stannous fluoride toothpaste, varnishes, Gluma, Super Seal. Root sensitivity common because CL exposes root with open dentinal tubules. Gingivectomy = suprabony defects only, contraindicated for infrabony defects + narrow KT zone.

ESSAY TOPIC 2: GUIDED TISSUE / BONE REGENERATION
GTR1How many tissue types compete at the root surface in GTR, and which wins without a barrier?
A. 2; bone cells win
B. 3; PDL wins
C. 4 (gingival CT, epithelium, bone, PDL); epithelium wins without barrier
D. 5; cementoblasts win
E. Only 1 participates
Correct Answer: C

4 tissue types compete: gingival CT, epithelium, bone cells, PDL cells. Epithelium wins (migrates 0.5mm/day) → forms long JE (repair). GTR membrane excludes epithelium + gingival CT, lets PDL + bone cells repopulate. Each tissue contacting root directly: Gingival CT → resorption. Epithelium → long JE (no true attachment). Bone → ankylosis. PDL → REGENERATION (new cementum with inserting collagen fibers).

GTR2What did the landmark GTR studies prove about each tissue type?
A. All produce identical outcomes
B. Gingival CT → resorption; epithelium → long JE; bone → ankylosis; PDL → regeneration
C. All produce regeneration
D. Only epithelium heals
E. Only bone heals
Correct Answer: B

Karring 1980: roots in bone (no epithelium) → repair only (resorption/ankylosis). Nyman 1980: roots in gingival CT → ankylosis/resorption. Nyman 1982: millipore filter over defect → new cementum with inserting collagen fibers (PDL cells regenerate). Isidor 1986: tight elastic (blocked PDL migration) = NO new attachment; loose elastic (allowed PDL migration) = new attachment. Proves coronal PDL cell migration is ESSENTIAL.

GTR3GTR initial incision and GORE-TEX closure/removal?
A. External bevel; flush with flap; remove at 2 weeks
B. Intrasulcular + remove all pocket epithelium; membrane 2-3mm below flap, interproximal first, verticals last; remove at 6 weeks
C. Semilunar; exposed above flap; 3 months
D. Internal bevel; 5mm below; never remove
E. No specific technique
Correct Answer: B

Incision: intrasulcular + remove ALL pocket epithelium. Closure: membrane 2–3mm below coronal flap aspect, interproximal closure FIRST, verticals LAST. Removal: can leave 3 months max, ideal healing 4–6 weeks, remove at 6 weeks. GORE-TEX = non-resorbable ePTFE, requires second surgery. #1 complication = membrane exposure.

GTR4Define repair, regeneration, new attachment, and reattachment.
A. All identical terms
B. Repair = JE adhesion preventing CT attachment; Regeneration = complete return of architecture/function (Melcher 1969); New attachment = new cementum/fibers on previously diseased root; Reattachment = reunion with root never pathologically detached
C. Only regeneration exists
D. Repair = bone formation
E. New attachment = ankylosis
Correct Answer: B

Repair = "JE cell adhesion with root deprived of its PDL; preventing CT attachment." Regeneration = "biologic process by which architecture and function of lost tissue is completely returned" (Melcher 1969). New attachment = new cementum with inserting fibers on previously DISEASED root. Reattachment = reunion with root NOT pathologically separated. PDL grown into cementum = NEW attachment.

GTR5Bone grafting materials: autograft, allograft, xenograft, alloplast?
A. Only synthetic materials exist
B. Autograft = same individual; Allograft = same species/different individual; Xenograft = different species; Alloplast = synthetic (inert fillers, unlikely to produce true regeneration)
C. Only autograft and allograft exist
D. All are identical
E. Grafting is obsolete
Correct Answer: B

Autograft: best osteogenic = vital cancellous bone with hematopoietic marrow (tuberosity). Allograft: DFDBA — Urist showed demineralization exposes BMP; Schwartz: not all samples osteoinductive. Xenograft: Bio-Oss = anorganic bovine bone (osteoconductive scaffold). Alloplast: "inert biologic fillers, unlikely to result in true regeneration". Osteoconductive = scaffold from existing bone. Osteoinductive = induces bone from undifferentiated cells. BMPs = key to bone morphogenesis (Urist 1965).

GTR6GEM 21S, PRP, EMD, and membrane types?
A. All identical products
B. GEM 21S = PDGF + β-TCP (faster healing, NOT greater volume). PRP = 330-900% platelet concentration. EMD = porcine tooth buds, osteopromotive NOT osteoinductive. Non-resorbable (GORE-TEX): more predictable, needs 2nd surgery. Resorbable: no 2nd surgery, less predictable.
C. Only non-resorbable membranes exist
D. EMD is osteoinductive
E. PRP replaces all other materials
Correct Answer: B

GEM 21S = rhPDGF-BB + β-TCP → improved bone formation, faster healing, NO greater volume. PRP = 330–900% platelet concentration. EMD = from porcine tooth buds, "osteopromotive" (with DFDBA) but NOT osteoinductive. BMPs = truly osteoinductive. GTR/GBR complications: pain/swelling (NSAIDs), membrane exposure (trim 2–3mm), infection (antibiotics + CHX), soft tissue perforation (tension-free closure), recession/sloughing (proper flap design).

ESSAY TOPIC 3: NON-SURGICAL / SURGICAL THERAPY
NSS1How does root planing differ from scaling?
A. Both identical
B. Root planing = remove diseased cementum/endotoxins for smooth surface (nothing to do with calculus); Scaling = remove soft/hard deposits (plaque + calculus) from crown AND root
C. Root planing removes dentin
D. Scaling is only supragingival
E. Root planing is supragingival only
Correct Answer: B

From slides: "Scaling: removal of soft and hard deposits adhering to the tooth surface." "Root Planing: removal of diseased root surfaces to create a smooth non-contaminated root surface." Root plane until smooth, glassy surface. Caffesse 1986: clinicians fail to debride 65% of the time at ≥5mm. Harper & Robinson 1987: SRP reduces microbes for limited period only. All SRP requires anesthesia. Gracey angulation: 60°–85°.

NSS2At what depth is SRP 100% efficient, what's the max reach, and when is surgery most effective?
A. 100% at 2mm; max 4mm; surgery best at 3mm
B. 100% at 3.7mm; max 6.2mm; surgery most effective at 5–6mm vs non-surgical
C. 100% at 5mm; max 8mm; surgery best at 8mm
D. Equally efficient at all depths
E. No data exists
Correct Answer: B

3.7mm = 100% efficiency. 6.2mm = max SRP reach. Surgery most effective at 5–6mm. After SRP, probing ≥6mm = won't improve non-surgically. At 2.5mm sites, SRP causes slight attachment LOSS. Multiple SRP episodes without improvement → transition to surgery. Never start surgery without adequate plaque removal first.

NSS3Non-surgical vs surgical therapy outcomes?
A. Identical outcomes
B. Non-surgical: decreased PD, attachment gain, decreased inflammation/BOP. Surgical ADDS: restoration of physiologic gingival/osseous contours + potential for reattachment/regeneration
C. Surgery has no additional benefit
D. Non-surgical achieves regeneration
E. Neither produces improvement
Correct Answer: B

Surgical objective from slides: "gain access for effective removal of etiologic factors" and "reduce pocket depth, making it more maintainable." Three assumptions when deciding treatment is indicated: (1) disease is active and will progress, (2) treatment will help, (3) patient will be compliant. Patient OH = most important factor for success. 3-month recall for perio patients. OH instruction at EACH visit.

NSS4Local delivery antimicrobials: PerioChip, Periostat, Arestin, Atridox?
A. All identical
B. PerioChip = 2.5mg CHX chip (contraindicated: CHX sensitivity). Periostat = 20mg doxycycline bid (sub-antimicrobial MMP inhibitor). Arestin = minocycline microspheres (14-day duration). Atridox = 42mg doxycycline gel (NOT for children/nursing women).
C. All systemic antibiotics
D. All contain CHX
E. PerioChip = Arestin
Correct Answer: B

PerioChip: 2.5mg CHX gluconate. Periostat: 20mg doxycycline bid — host modulation (inhibits MMPs), NOT antibacterial at this dose. Doxycycline + SRP = increased effect. Arestin: minocycline microspheres, 14-day max. Atridox: 42mg doxycycline polymer gel. CHX is cationic bisguanide; toothpaste is anionic (SLS) — wait 30 minutes. Endo before perio when both present.

ESSAY TOPIC 4: OCCLUSAL TRAUMA
OT1What is trauma from occlusion?
A. Gingival condition from plaque
B. Pathologic lesion in the attachment apparatus from excessive/abnormal forces exceeding the adaptive capacity of tissues
C. Tooth fracture
D. Mucosal damage from appliances
E. Caries from acid
Correct Answer: B

From slides: "A pathologic lesion in the attachment apparatus established by excessive or abnormal biting forces that exceed the adaptive capacity of the tissues." Key: affects attachment apparatus (cementum, PDL, bone) — NOT gingiva. OT does NOT cause attachment loss, pocketing, or recession: "gingival soft tissues coronal to bone are NOT damaged by excessive force."

OT2Primary vs secondary occlusal trauma: diagnosis?
A. Identical presentation
B. Primary: increasing mobility/migration/fremitus on NORMAL periodontium; widened PDL radiographically. Secondary: attachment loss ALREADY present; normal/heavy forces on REDUCED periodontium
C. Primary always has bone loss
D. Both diagnosed by symptoms only
E. Primary: posteriors; Secondary: anteriors
Correct Answer: B

Primary: excessive force, normal support. Clinical: increasing mobility, migration, fremitus. Radiographic: widened PDL, possible angular bone loss. Reversible. Secondary: "must have attachment loss". Normal/heavy forces, reduced periodontium. Usually not reversible. You can have primary OT even WITH attachment loss. Must have increasing mobility over time to diagnose OT.

OT3Treatment for primary vs secondary OT?
A. Both: extraction
B. Primary: reduce forces (occlusal adjustment). Secondary: splinting to compensate for lost support
C. Primary: bone graft; Secondary: RCT
D. Both: antibiotics
E. Primary: splinting; Secondary: adjustment
Correct Answer: B

Primary → reduce force (occlusal equilibration). Once reduced: lesion resolves, PDL width returns, mobility reduces. Secondary → splinting to distribute forces. Splint purpose: patient comfort + dissipate occlusal forces. Treatment may necessitate: equilibration, orthodontics, or prosthetics (splinting).

OT4Glickman & Smulow (1962): perio disease + occlusal trauma?
A. OT replaces plaque as cause
B. OT redirects inflammatory exudate from gingival unit to attachment apparatus, exacerbating progression — but co-destruction still predominantly caused by plaque inflammation, OT contribution is minor
C. OT protects against progression
D. No additional effect
E. OT causes gingivitis alone
Correct Answer: B

Glickman & Smulow 1962: OT redirects inflammatory exudate from gingival unit to attachment apparatus → zone of co-destruction. "Periodontal breakdown with co-destruction is still predominantly caused by plaque inflammation, while that caused by occlusal trauma is minor." Plaque = ignitor. OT = accelerant. "Trauma from occlusion may be an aggravating factor."

OT5Does OT cause attachment loss? Fremitus vs mobility? Mobility grades?
A. Yes, OT causes attachment loss
B. NO — effects limited to attachment apparatus; supracrestal tissues NOT damaged. Fremitus = movement by patient's own forces. Mobility = movement by operator's force. Grades: physiologic ≤0.2mm, Gr1 0.2-1mm, Gr2 1-2mm, Gr3 >2mm. Mobility ≠ disease; treat only if affects function/comfort.
C. OT directly migrates JE
D. OT has no tissue effect
E. All mobility requires treatment
Correct Answer: B

From slides: "Occlusal trauma does not cause (initiate) attachment loss, pocketing, or recession." Fremitus = patient's force. Mobility = operator's force. Grades: Physiologic ≤0.2mm, Grade 1: 0.2–1.0mm, Grade 2: 1.0–2.0mm, Grade 3: >2.0mm ± vertical. "Mobility can indicate disease but alone does not indicate disease." Treat only if disturbs masticatory function or comfort. Posterior bite collapse: missing tooth → super-eruption → drifting → lost support → anterior flaring ("syndrome, NOT disease entity").

B. Thick scalloped

Thick + flat biotype. Thick: probe not visible (>1mm), KT 5.09–6.65mm, GT 1.29–1.79mm. Thin: probe visible (≤1mm), KT 2.75–5.44mm, GT ~0.8mm. Inflammation → pockets in thick tissue, recession in thin tissue. GT <1mm = reduced probability for complete root coverage.

D. Mand molars

Correct Answer: C

Opposite dominant hand canines + premolars. Two major causative factors: toothbrushing + plaque-induced inflammation. NOT amount of attached gingiva (Maynard & Wilson 1979: with good OH, lack of AG does NOT result in recession).

MG5Prevalence of >1mm recession in adults over 30?

E. 78%
Correct Answer: C

58%. Serino 1994: 3mm recession worsened 67%, 4mm worsened 98%. Chambrone & Tatakis 2016: untreated recession with good OH still likely to increase long-term.

MG6Minimum KT for health vs around restorations?
A. 1mm / 3mm
B. 2mm KT (1mm attached + 1mm free) for health; 5mm KT (3mm attached + 2mm free) around restorations
C. 3mm / 5mm
D. 5mm / 8mm
E. No minimum
Correct Answer: B

Lang & Löe 1972: 2mm KT for health. Maynard & Wilson 1979: 5mm KT around restorations. Widening AG helps: enhances plaque control, improves esthetics, reduces inflammation. Indications for gingival augmentation: gingival inflammation, progressive recession, no attached gingiva.

MG7Miller Classification: complete/partial/no root coverage?
A. Complete=I; Partial=II; None=III/IV
B. Complete=I+II; Partial=III; None=IV
C. Complete=I/II/III; None=IV
D. Complete=I; Partial=II/III; None=IV

Miller I+II = complete (no interproximal loss). III = partial. IV = none. 2018 update replaces Miller with Cairo RT1/RT2/RT3: RT1 = no interproximal loss; RT2 = interproximal CAL ≤ facial CAL; RT3 = interproximal CAL > facial CAL.

MG8CTG gold standard for root coverage — what did Wennstrom show about FGG predictability?
A. FGG is gold standard
B. CTG is gold standard over FGG. FGG: greatest success in narrow/shallow defects, predictability 0-90% average 57%. FGG ideal for increasing KT + gingival thickness. CTG = subepithelial CT graft (Langer & Langer 1985).
C. Equal outcomes
D. Neither covers roots
E. CTG only for vestibular depth
Correct Answer: B

CTG = gold standard (Wennstrom). FGG = epithelialized soft tissue graft, rapid/predictable but poor color match. CTG advantages: good color blend, predictable in Miller I/II. Partially covered CTG: leaves epithelium, increases KT (CAN'T be on thin tissue). Completely covered: epithelium removed, for thin tissue. Creeping attachment: ~1mm additional coverage over 1 year with thick graft.

MG9Graft survival factors and FGG technique?
A. Thickness + suture technique
B. Blood supply (don't place above CEJ) + stability (reduce mobility + dead spaces). FGG: graft taken 2mm apically from teeth, 0.75-1.25mm thick, no dead space, sling sutures.
C. OH + smoking status
D. Recipient width + donor quality
E. Tension + color match
Correct Answer: B

Two survival factors: blood supply (no bone above CEJ = no blood) + stability. FGG technique from slides: taken 2mm apically from teeth, 0.75–1.25mm thick, recipient = split thickness at MGJ. CTG donor: double incision (with epithelium) or single incision (without). Palatal anatomy (Reiser 1996): shallow=7mm, average=12mm, high=17mm to greater palatine artery.

MG10CAF criteria (Bernimoulin 1981)?
A. Any recession/thickness
B. Minimum tissue 0.8mm, recession <4mm, Miller I, KT >3mm, minimal tension, flap 2.5mm coronal to CEJ
C. Only Miller III/IV
D. KT >5mm, recession >6mm
E. No criteria exist
Correct Answer: B

Bernimoulin 1981: 0.8mm minimum tissue thickness, <4mm recession, Miller Class I, KT >3mm, minimal/no tension, flap 2.5mm coronal to CEJ. Per Cortellini & Pini Prato 2012: thick residual KT → CAF alone; thin → add CTG/EMD/ADM. Semilunar CAF (Tarnow 1986): no vestibule shortening, requires adequate KT zone/thickness, does NOT increase KT zone.

MG11Apically positioned flap indications?
A. Root coverage + vestibular depth
B. Increase attached gingiva, deepen vestibule, eliminate pocket depth, gummy smile correction

E. Crown lengthening only

4 indications for APF: increase AG, deepen vestibule, eliminate pocket depth, gummy smile. Note: root coverage is NOT an APF indication — APF moves tissue apically. Uses vertical mattress suture (needle through flap + periosteum, knot apically) to secure flap in apical position. APF preserves keratinized tissue by repositioning it.

FUR1Gracey blade width and furcation entrance data?
A. 1.0mm; 40% narrower
B. 0.75mm; 60% of furcation entrances <0.75mm. Mand furcations: buccal 3mm/wide, lingual 4mm/narrow. Max furcations: mesial 3mm, buccal 3.5mm, distal 5mm from CEJ.
C. 0.50mm; 80%
D. 1.5mm; 90%
E. 0.75mm; 30%
Correct Answer: B

New Gracey = 0.75mm; 60% of furcation entrances <0.75mm. Root trunk: 3M>2M>1M (1st molar = shortest, earliest involvement). Highest frequency furcation = upper 1st molar distal. MB root = 85% have >1 canal. Most frequently removed root = DB of max molar.

FUR2Glickman and Lindhe furcation classifications?
A. Measured in mm only
B. Glickman: Gr1=V-notch, Gr2=partial probe, Gr3=probe through + radiographic evidence, Gr4=through + recession + total exposure. Lindhe: Deg1=<1/3 loss, Deg2=>1/3 not through, Deg3=through-and-through. Tarnow vertical: A=0-3mm, B=4-6mm, C=7+mm.
C. Identical systems
D. No clinical definitions
E. Based on tooth type only
Correct Answer: B

Glickman: 4 grades (clinical description). Lindhe: 3 degrees (proportional interradicular bone loss). Tarnow & Fletcher: vertical (bottom of defect to fornix). SRP resolves Grade 1 only, efficiency 4mm, max 6.2mm. Factors influencing furcation regeneration: horizontal defect morphology, furcation anatomy, early healing.

FUR3Root resection success rate and post-op guidelines?
A. 50-65%
B. 85-96% (Hamp/Langer/Carnevale). Healing: 3 months for stable margin (Gargiulo). Wait 3-6 months for final restoration (Eastman/Maynard). Avoid post and core (fracture risk). Retain: max=palatal+distal; mand=distal root.
C. 75-85%
D. 97-100%
E. Success rate unknown
Correct Answer: B

85–96% with proper selection/treatment/restoration. Root resection = removal of all/portion of roots. Root amputation = remove one root, keep crown + remaining roots. Hemisection = surgical separation through furcation. CEPs: 13% of molars have them, 82% with CEPs have furcation involvement. Pulp-to-surface distance in mand furcation: 2.8mm (adequate for odontoplasty). Enamel pearls: 1.1–5.7%, mostly max 2nd molars near CEJ.

DIAGNOSIS, CLASSIFICATION, RISK FACTORS & CLINICAL PEARLS
DX1CAL is measured from:
A. CEJ to bottom of pocket
B. CEJ to gingival margin
C. Bottom of pocket to gingival margin (=probing depth)
D. Alveolar crest to pocket base
E. Margin to crest
Correct Answer: A

CAL = CEJ to base of pocket. Probing depth = margin to base. With recession: CAL = PD + recession. With overgrowth: CAL = PD − overgrowth.

DX2Smoking and periodontal disease (Tomar 2000)?
A. No effect
B. Current = 4x risk; former = 2x; 53% attributable; ≤9/day = 3x, ≥31/day = 6x; ≥11 yrs quit = non-smoker risk. Grade modifier in 2018 classification.
C. Only affects gingivitis
D. Risk normalizes after 1 year
E. Only patients >60
Correct Answer: B

From slides: 4x current, 2x former, 53% attributable. Dose-response. Cessation: ≥11 years = non-smoker risk. Smoking = Grade modifier (≥10 cigs/day → Grade C). Part of PRA hexagon (BOP%, pockets >4mm, missing teeth, bone loss/age, systemic, smoking).

DX32018 staging: which parameter first?
A. RBL first
B. CAL first; RBL backup; tooth loss may shift stage higher. Grading: A=slow (<0.25), B=moderate (0.25-1.0), C=rapid (>1.0). Assume Grade B first. Modifiers: smoking ≥10 cigs→C, HbA1c ≥7%→C.
C. BOP first
D. PD first
E. GI first
Correct Answer: B

From slides: "Initial staging using CAL. If unavailable, use RBL. Tooth loss may shift stage (3→4)." Grading: direct evidence = bone loss over 5 years. Indirect = % bone loss/age. Assume Grade B, seek evidence for A or C.

DX5Löe 1965 proved what? Did NOT prove what?
A. Proved gingivitis→periodontitis
B. Proved plaque→gingivitis (reversible). Did NOT prove gingivitis→periodontitis. Socransky 1984: only 12% of sites lose significant attachment, unpredictable (episodic burst). Eke prevalence: 56% (6-site), NOT 47% (3-site).
C. Proved OT causes perio
D. Proved SRP reverses bone loss
E. Proved antibiotics prevent gingivitis
Correct Answer: B

Fleisher-flagged exam trap. Löe proved plaque → gingivitis only. Socransky 1984: 12% of sites, unpredictable. Eke: 56% is correct (6-site), 47% is wrong (3-site). Exam answer = "none of the above."

DX6Localized vs generalized cutoff?
A. <10% vs >10%
B. <20% vs >20%
C. <30% = localized; >30% = generalized
D. <50% vs >50%
E. <40% vs >40%
Correct Answer: C

From slides: "Localized = 10–30%; Generalized = >30%." Gingivitis: >10% BOP, PD <3mm. Health: <10% BOP. Periodontitis case: interdental CAL at >2 non-adjacent teeth OR buccal/lingual CAL >3mm with PD >3mm at >2 teeth.

DX7Prognosis system (McGuire 1991)?
A. Only good or hopeless exist
B. Good (factors controllable, adequate support) → Fair (≤25% loss, Gr I furcation) → Poor (≤50% loss, Class I+ mobility) → Questionable (>50% loss, Gr II+ furcation) → Hopeless (inadequate attachment, Gr III/IV furcation, Class III mobility)
C. 3 categories only
D. Based solely on age
E. No standardized system
Correct Answer: B

McGuire 1991; McGuire & Nunn 1996. Also Kwok & Caton 2007: Favorable → Unfavorable → Questionable → Hopeless (based on controllability of factors). Prognostic factors: mobility, remaining attachment, PD, bone loss, furcation, CR ratio, endo status, caries, smoking, diabetes, compliance.

DX8Patient: 2-4mm PD, swollen blue gingiva, bone crest 1mm from CEJ. Diagnosis?
A. Gingivitis (bone at normal 1-2mm from CEJ = no bone loss = pseudopocketing from edema)
B. NUP
C. Chronic periodontitis
D. Stage I periodontitis
E. Healthy but reduced periodontium
Correct Answer: A

Bone 1mm from CEJ = normal. No bone loss = cannot diagnose periodontitis. Swollen blue gingiva = chronic venous congestion. 2–4mm PDs = pseudopocketing from inflammatory edema. "Healthy but reduced periodontium" = prior attachment loss + PD ≤3mm + no BOP.

DX9Most important factor for long-term perio treatment success?
A. Operator skill
B. Recall compliance
C. Patient's oral hygiene practices
D. Surgical technique
E. Graft material
Correct Answer: C

From slides: "Professional cleaning every 3 MONTHS at least partly compensated for negative effects of variations in self-performed plaque control." Perio = 3-month recall. Non-perio = 6 months. OH instruction at EACH visit. Never start surgery if no adequate plaque removal.

DX10BOP is a sign of what?
A. Periodontitis specifically
B. Gingival inflammation (gingivitis), NOT a definitive sign of periodontitis — need CAL/bone loss to diagnose periodontitis
C. Normal healthy tissue
D. Occlusal trauma
E. Root fracture
Correct Answer: B

BOP = inflammation sign. Most reliable objective feature of gingivitis. But BOP alone ≠ periodontitis — need CAL + bone loss evidence. Probing affected by: force applied and degree of inflammation (flat/tapered probes penetrate more than round).

DX11Posterior bite collapse sequence?
A. Caries → extraction → implant
B. Missing tooth → super-eruption → loss of contact/drifting → loss of posterior support → anterior flaring. 'A syndrome, NOT a disease entity.'
C. Ortho → intrusion → stable
D. Bruxism → wear → open bite
E. A disease requiring antibiotics
Correct Answer: B

From slides: Missing tooth → Super-erupted tooth → Loss of Contact/Drifting → Loss of Posterior Support → Flaring of Anterior Teeth. "A syndrome with many clinical findings and is NOT a disease entity."

DX125 conditions that CANNOT be ascribed to periodontitis when evaluating CAL?
A. Plaque-induced attachment loss at multiple sites
B. Gingival recession of traumatic origin; cervical caries; CAL on distal of 2nd molar from 3rd molar issues; endo lesion draining through marginal periodontium; vertical root fracture
C. Generalized CAL with bone loss + BOP
D. Interdental CAL at >2 non-adjacent teeth
E. Buccal CAL >3mm with PD >3mm at >2 teeth
Correct Answer: B

From slides — 5 exclusions: (1) traumatic recession, (2) cervical caries, (3) CAL distal to 2nd molar from 3rd molar, (4) endo lesion draining marginally, (5) vertical root fracture. If isolated deep probing has one of these explanations, it's NOT periodontitis. Walk the probe for vertical fracture detection.

DX13Chronic periodontitis and age? Aggressive perio features?
A. Age directly causes perio
B. Chronic perio is age-DEPENDENT (accumulation of cases), but age itself does NOT make patient high-risk. Aggressive perio: rapid attachment loss, otherwise healthy, microbial deposits inconsistent with severity, elevated A.a. and P.g., treat with amoxicillin + metronidazole or azithromycin (250mg/day × 5 days after 500mg load).
C. Only occurs after 65
D. Age has no relationship
E. Prevalence decreases with age
Correct Answer: B

Chronic perio: age-dependent from accumulation, NOT age-caused. Aggressive perio (now Stage III Grade C): rapid loss, healthy otherwise, biofilm inconsistent with destruction. Treatment: SRP + amoxicillin + metronidazole (tandem/serially) or azithromycin 250mg/day × 5 days after 500mg loading dose. "Chronic apical periodontitis" = purely endo term (primary endo lesion), nothing to do with perio.

DX14Electric vs manual toothbrush?
A. Electric has greater efficacy
B. SAME efficacy, but electric reaches efficacy faster = better effectiveness overall
C. Manual superior
D. Electric only for disabilities
E. No difference
Correct Answer: B

From slides on plaque control: same efficacy (same endpoint) but electric reaches it faster = better effectiveness. Bass technique: 45° toward sulcus, short vibratory motion. Proxabrush: for open embrasures (not tight contacts). End-tuft brush: for distal of last molars. Unwaxed floss = same efficacy as waxed.
