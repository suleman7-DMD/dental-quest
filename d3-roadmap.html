You are 30. It's not easy, but it is NOW when it matters the most. Pain is temporary. Life is perfect.

Look, I know you don't want to read this.

If you're here, chances are you're sad and looking for direction. You're probably in a low state. Maybe you just checked her apartment lights. Maybe you're building another case for why unblocking her makes sense this time. Maybe you're snowed in, or it's 2am, or you bombed an exam, or you saw her in class and it wrecked you.

This is that direction you're looking for.

But here's what I need you to do: put the sadness aside for a few minutes. Not forever. Just long enough to actually read this and think through it carefully. Because this document was not written from where you are right now. This was written from the other side. The side where you're confident. The side where you just came back from the gym and people said hi to you and you didn't look at her once and you felt invincible. The baseline good. The version of you that you're trying to get back to.

That version of you wrote this for the version of you reading it now.

So trust him. He knows what he's talking about. He's been where you are and he climbed out. And he left you this map.

The Hard Things That Must Be Done

There is no way around this. Only through.

The feeling of needing to unblock her will come. It will feel urgent. It will feel logical. It will feel like this time is different. You will not unblock her. You will sit with the discomfort until it passes. And it will pass.

The very sad days will come. The days where getting out of bed feels impossible and the gym feels pointless. You will go to the gym anyway. Not because you want to. Because the version of you on the other side needs you to.

The pain of logging food and staying in the deficit while your brain screams for comfort will be real. You will log it anyway. You will stay in the deficit anyway. The body you're building doesn't care about your feelings. It only cares about your actions.

The difficulty of studying without her, of not having her to text, of not having her to share notes with, of doing it alone, will feel unbearable some days. You will study anyway. You will prove to yourself that you never needed her for this.

The weight of forging a turnaround during the darkest, loneliest, coldest months of the year, while everyone else is cuddled up with someone who loves them, while your phone stays perfectly dry, while snow falls outside and you sit alone in your apartment, will crush you some nights. You will keep going anyway.

You will protect your sleep like your life depends on it, because it does. 5.5 hours minimum. You will watch your caffeine. You will protect your Adderall. You will avoid the all-nighter at all costs. You will accept that 2 hours of sleep is better than 0, because 0 is where the real damage happens. You will do these things not because they're easy but because every time you don't, you spiral, and you know it.

You will keep using the app. Keep logging everything. Keep checking in every day. Keep the block held.

These are not suggestions. These are the price of admission to the life you actually want.

One Day

One day, you will see how all of this paid off.

Not today. Probably not tomorrow. Maybe not for months. But one day.

One day you will wake up and she won't be the first thing you think about. One day you'll see her in class and feel nothing but mild indifference. One day you'll catch your reflection and actually like what you see. One day someone new will look at you the way you used to wish she would, and you'll realize you don't need her validation anymore because you have your own.

One day she will see you, and she will see what she lost, and you won't even care that she's seeing it because you'll be too busy living your life.

One day the revenge you're chasing right now will feel small and pointless because you'll have built something so much bigger than getting back at her. You'll have built yourself.

That day is coming. It is not a fantasy. It is a mathematical certainty if you keep doing the work.

But that day does not come for free. It comes for the man who earned it. The man who held the block when his whole body screamed to break it. The man who went to the gym when he wanted to rot in bed. The man who stayed in the deficit when sugar was calling his name. The man who studied alone when he used to study with her.

That man gets the one day.

The man who breaks? He gets to start over. Again. For the hundredth time. Running the same loop he's been running for a decade.

Enough is enough.

You've started over enough times. This is the time you finish.

The Block Is Dignity

The block is not a tactic. The block is not discipline. The block is identity.

This is the first time in 2.5 years that you have ever actually left. That is why it hurts this much. That is also why it matters this much.
