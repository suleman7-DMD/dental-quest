Quiz Review Pearls
  • Hx + meds always — open question first, then specific, no leading bias
  • Serpiginous = white snakelike borders (geographic tongue)
  • Measure every lesion; describe each independently
  • Clotrimazole (Mycelex) = sugar → NOT for diabetic patients
Saliva Functions

Lubrication / mech protection · taste (sensory) · speech / communication · dental protection (remin, pH buffer, pellicle) · digestion (bolus, carb/lipid, esophageal acid neutralization) · antimicrobial (Ig, microbiome) · cleansing & clearance · wound healing · hormone synthesis · diagnostic biomarker reservoir

Gland Types
  • Parotid = serous (thin, watery, fast-flowing)
  • Submandibular + sublingual = mixed serous + mucous; affected first in hyposalivation
  • Why dry mouth = ropey/sticky: water loss >> mucin loss → mucins concentrate, altered, less lubricating
  • New gland: tubarial glands (Valstar, 2021)

Causes

Hyposalivation 40 – 60%

Anxiety, polypharmacy, dehydration, uncontrolled DM, ESRD, radiation / surgery, Parkinson disease, GVHD, irAEs, Sjögren, sarcoidosis, GPA, IgG4-RD, HCV, HIV.

Xerostomia 20 – 42%

Medications (anticholinergic, antidepressant, antihypertensive, urological), oral dysesthesia / BMS, trigeminal neuropathy.

Both 16 – 27%

Chairside Workup
1. History

Meds, radiation, autoimmune dz, duration, other dry sites (eyes / nose / throat), dysphagia.

3. Objective measurement

Unstim + stim flow rates; refer if borderline / abnormal.

Pt c/o dry mouth + normal pooling on exam → measure flow to distinguish true hyposalivation from xerostomia alone.

Radiation-Induced Hyposalivation

Dose

  • H&N rad total: 65 – 70 Gy
  • Parotid threshold: > 25 Gy
  • Submandibular threshold: > 39 Gy
  • Dose-dependent acinar destruction; irreversible past threshold

Caries Pattern

Ipsilateral, cervical, facial → circumferential, smooth surfaces of mandibular anterior dentition, maxillary molars.

Fluoride / Caries Protocol

  • 1.1% NaF (5000 ppm) or SnF₂ gel — daily brush-on or in trays
  • 1.1% NaF dentifrice daily
  • Fluoride varnish 3× / year
  • Ca-PO₄ artificial saliva for remineralization
  • Recall q 3 – 4 mo, brush 2 – 4×/day, CHX rinses
Consequences (hyposalivation only — not xerostomia)

Root / cervical caries (classic pattern), recurrent decay, erythematous candidiasis, dysphagia, mucosal trauma, denture difficulty.

Treatment

Avoid

Caffeine, alcohol (drinks & mouthwash), Vaseline, Aquaphor.

OTC

  • Hydration (± pineapple), bedside humidifier
  • Sugar-free lemon candy
  • Xylimelts, Xerostrips, Biotene
  • Lanolin HPA for lips

M-Receptor Cheat Sheet

  • M1 — CNS / cognition
  • M2 — heart (↓ HR)
  • M3 — exocrine: saliva / tears / sweat; bronchoconstrict; GI motility; bladder
  • M4 / M5 — CNS
Sjögren Syndrome

Autoimmune destruction of salivary + lacrimal glands.

  • Middle-aged female; F : M = 9 : 1; prevalence 0.5 – 1%
  • Classic triad: hyposalivation + xerophthalmia + bilateral parotid enlargement
  • 1° SS = alone   ·   2° SS = with RA, SLE, thyroid disease, etc.
Must exclude before dx: H&N radiation, active HCV, sarcoidosis, IgG4-RD, recent anticholinergic use (false positives).

Workup — what the dentist orders

Anti-SSA (Ro), anti-SSB (La), ANA, RF → refer to OM / OMFS for minor salivary gland biopsy (lower lip).

Up to 30% are seronegative but have positive biopsy / Schirmer — negative antibodies do not rule out Sjögren.

Management

↑ recall frequency, high-potency fluoride, OHI counseling, refer OM + rheumatology, sialagogues, OTC saliva, hydration, finish workup.

Lymphoma Risk (critical)

  • 5 – 10% lifetime NHL risk
  • MALT lymphoma — most common type
  • Highest lymphoma risk among autoimmune diseases — 7 – 19× general population
Red flags → urgent rheum / heme referral:
  • Persistent unilateral gland enlargement
  • Lymphadenopathy, purpura
  • Constitutional symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss)
Sialolithiasis

Pathognomonic: mealtime pain + swelling, unilateral.

  • Submandibular 80 – 90% (long tortuous Wharton duct, against gravity, mucous + Ca²⁺-rich saliva)
  • Imaging ladder: occlusal radiograph (1st line, submand) → panoramic → ultrasound (catches non-radiopaque) → CT if surgical planning
Not all stones are radiopaque — negative XR does not exclude.

Conservative management (1st line)

Hydrate 2 L/day, gland massage, warm moist compress, OTC saliva substitutes, NSAIDs / acetaminophen, muscarinic agonist.

Refer for sialendoscopy

If: stone > 2 mm in proximal duct, failed conservative care 1 – 2 wk, or recurrent. 85 – 92% gland preservation rate.

Acute Bacterial Sialadenitis

Painful swollen unilateral gland + fever + purulence at duct orifice + erythema + firm / tender on palpation.

  • #1 organism: S. aureus; also Strep, anaerobes
  • Tx: amoxicillin-clavulanate OR clindamycin (cover Staph) + aggressive hydration
  • Culture purulent material; image to rule out stone / mass
Urgent ED / ENT / OMFS if: sepsis, immunocompromised, trismus / severe swelling, airway concern.
Mucocele
  • Lower lip 60 – 70% — #1 site
  • Fluctuant, bluish, painless swelling
  • Trauma history — lip bite, sharp canine

Pathophysiology

Trauma → minor salivary gland duct rupture → mucin extravasation into connective tissue (extravasation phenomenon).
