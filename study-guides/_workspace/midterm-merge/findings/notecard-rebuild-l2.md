## SECTION 1: HYPERSENSITIVITY TYPES

### HIGH
- **Type I:** IgE-mediated; timing minutes–hours; dental triggers: latex, antibiotics, chlorhexidine. *Mnemonic: "Type 1 ends in E → IgE" — prof repeated 3× in lecture.*
- **Type IV:** T-cell mediated (delayed); timing 24–72 hours; triggers: metals (nickel, chromium, cobalt), acrylates, amalgam, flavorings
- **Pseudoallergic:** Non-immune mast cell activation; variable timing; bisulfites (LA preservatives); most "LA allergies" are pseudoallergic — NOT true IgE allergy

### MEDIUM
- **Patch test is gold standard for Type IV BUT:** Positive patch test = sensitization history, NOT necessarily the active current trigger. Requires temporal correlation. Source: prof-flag-tier-1 trap F12.
- **CHX allergy substitution:** If CHX allergy → povidone-iodine/Betadine; if shellfish allergy → iodine contraindicated (cannot use Betadine)

### LOW
- Type IV dental presentations: lichenoid reactions at amalgam sites, denture acrylic stomatitis (palatal erythema), contact cheilitis (scaling/crusting of vermilion border)

## SECTION 2: RECURRENT APHTHOUS STOMATITIS (RAS) — TIER 1

### HIGH
- **Location = NONKERATINIZED MUCOSA ONLY:** labial/buccal mucosa, ventral tongue, soft palate. This is the single most critical differentiator from HSV.
- **RAS vs HSV key differentiator:** Keratinized = HSV; Nonkeratinized = RAS. HSV also: fever, vesicles before ulcers, lymphadenopathy, prodrome (tingling). RAS: none of those.
- **Erythematous halo = RAS on differential — "EASY EXAM POINTS":** Yellow-white fibrinopurulent membrane surrounded by red halo. See it → put RAS on list. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **Age >40 + new-onset RAS → MANDATORY SYSTEMIC WORKUP:** HIV, celiac disease, IBD, hematologic malignancy. Key question: "Did you have these as a child?" No childhood history + age >40 = workup. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **Herpetiform RAS = morphologic term ONLY, NOT caused by HSV.** Name is a red herring. Immune-mediated. Up to 100 ulcers, may coalesce, female predominance. Source: prof-flag-tier-1.
- **"When RAS isn't RAS":** Oral ulcers + fever + fatigue + weight loss + not responding to acyclovir → markedly reduced CD4 count → top DDx = HIV/AIDS
- **Biopsy indications:** (1) Single ulcer >4 weeks, (2) Atypical presentation, (3) New onset after age 40 — liberal biopsying policy

### MEDIUM
- **RAS variants table:**
  - Minor: 3–10 mm, heals 7–14 days, no scarring, 80% of cases
  - Major: 1–3 cm, heals 2–6 weeks, may scar, 10–15%; sites: labial/soft palate/tonsillar fauces
  - Herpetiform: 1–3 mm, 5–10%, up to 100 ulcers, female predominance, NOT HSV
- **Complex aphthosis:** ≥3 concurrent ulcers, almost constant, severe pain → 60% have underlying trigger → full lab workup: CBC, iron, B12, folate, Vit D, celiac screening
- **Medication-induced RAS:** NSAIDs, beta-blockers, bisphosphonates, nicorandil
- **Systemic associations:** Celiac, IBD, Behcet, cyclic neutropenia, PFAPA, HIV
- **CD8+ T cells target nonkeratinized mucosa** (pathomechanism)
- **Silver nitrate cautery — AVOID:** risk of massive necrosis and argyria

### LOW
- Most common oral ulcerative condition; ~20% population (range 5–66%)
- 80% first ulcer before age 30; 40% positive family history
- Nutritional screen (only when clinically suspected): B12, folate, iron/ferritin, Vit D, Zinc — supplement ONLY AS NEEDED
- Refractory: refer to oral medicine (thalidomide, colchicine, dapsone, apremilast — specialist use)

## SECTION 3: CORTICOSTEROID PHARMACOTHERAPY (RAS/OLP/MMP — applies to ALL conditions)

### HIGH
- **Dexamethasone elixir 0.5 mg/5 mL — THE drug to memorize.** Universal first-line oral medicine rinse referenced for EVERY disease in this lecture. Dose: soak 5 minutes (min 2 min), up to QID. Soak & expectorate (or swallow per Rx). Source: alg-drug-hero, prof-emphasis.
- **Prof mnemonic:** "My Windex" — like the father in My Big Fat Greek Wedding, she puts it on everything.
- **Corticosteroid potency ladder (memorize — TRAP on exam):**
  1. **Clobetasol propionate 0.05% gel** — Group 1, super-high potency — first-line for moderate-severe
  2. **Fluocinonide 0.05% gel** — Group 2, high potency — pre-numb patient first (gel = alcohol = burns)
  3. **Dexamethasone elixir 0.5 mg/5 mL** — Not classified — ONLY oral rinse formulation
  4. **Triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% paste** — Group 4, WEAKEST — trap: "dental" in name does NOT make it best
- **TRAP: Triamcinolone acetonide "dental" paste = WEAKEST** of the three. "Dental" in name → students think it's best → it's NOT. Source: prof-flag-tier-1.
- **Gel = alcohol = BURNS:** Pre-numb patient with lidocaine or benzocaine BEFORE applying fluocinonide gel. Apply gel under gauze for 20 min for max penetration.
- **Candidiasis risk with prolonged steroids:** Combine dexamethasone solution with nystatin solution in equal parts if candidiasis risk.
- **Off-label note:** Fluocinonide and clobetasol drug inserts say "do not use intraorally" — used off-label; warn patients; pharmacist may flag the Rx.

### HIGH — NEVER RULE
- **NEVER apply topical corticosteroids to the vermilion lip — irreversible fat pad atrophy (dimpling).** Use tacrolimus 0.1% ointment on vermilion instead. Oral mucosa = OK for topical steroids. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.

### HIGH — SLS/TOOTHPASTE TRAP
- **DO NOT recommend Sensodyne for soft tissue disease.** Sensodyne = most irritating toothpaste for mucosal tissues. Good for dentin hypersensitivity, NOT soft tissue. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **Recommend SLS-free instead:** Cleure, Boka, CloSYS, Tom's (original), Verve, or **Doctor Bob** (single one to remember — unflavored, SLS-free, dye-free, gluten-free). Source: alg-catG + prof-emphasis.

## SECTION 4: GEOGRAPHIC TONGUE (ERYTHEMA MIGRANS / BENIGN MIGRATORY GLOSSITIS) — TIER 1

### HIGH
- **3 D's of Geographic Tongue (mnemonic):**
  - **Depapillated** — loss of filiform papillae → red smooth atrophic patches
  - **Demarcated** — clear circumferential serpiginous (winding/snake-like) white border
  - **Dynamic** — migrates location over days to weeks (PATHOGNOMONIC — rules out malignancy)
- **SERPIGINOUS** = key exam vocabulary word. From "serpent" — snake-shaped winding border. Prof had class say it aloud. Source: prof-emphasis.
- **MIGRATORY = PATHOGNOMONIC → rules out malignancy.** If lesion moves location over days, it cannot be cancer. Most clinically reassuring feature. Source: callout-danger.
- **TRAP 1:** White serpiginous border ≠ candidiasis. Candida scrapes off with gauze. Geographic tongue border does NOT scrape off and does NOT respond to antifungals. Source: prof-flag-tier-1.
- **TRAP 2:** Ectopic geographic tongue: GT can appear on buccal mucosa, floor of mouth, soft palate — still called erythema migrans. NOT tongue-restricted. Source: prof-flag-tier-1.
- **DDx critical item — Erythroplakia:** FIXED (does not migrate) + high malignant potential → URGENT BIOPSY. Must not miss.

### MEDIUM
- **Management:**
  - Asymptomatic: reassurance only — benign condition, no treatment needed
  - Symptomatic: children's toothpaste (unflavored); avoid mint/spicy/acidic; **diphenhydramine 12.5 mg/5 mL (children's Benadryl)** soak TID-QID; fluocinonide/clobetasol gel; tacrolimus 0.1%
- **Diphenhydramine 12.5 mg/5 mL dual role:** anti-inflammatory AND topical anesthetic on mucosal surfaces. Source: pearl-callout.
- **DDx table distinguishers:**
  - Candidiasis: scrapes off with gauze, responds to antifungals
  - OLP: Wickham striae, bilateral buccal pattern
  - Erythroplakia: FIXED, high malignant potential → urgent biopsy
  - Leukoplakia: fixed white patch, does not shift

### LOW
- Affects ~3% of population; familial predisposition
- Associations: fissured tongue (strong), psoriasis (documented), atopy (elevated IgE ≥200 U/mL)

## SECTION 5: DRUG-RELATED GINGIVAL HYPERPLASIA (DRGH/DIGH) — TIER 1 / BOARDS FAVORITE

### HIGH
- **Three drug classes (ALL must be memorized — Boards love this):**
  1. **Anticonvulsants: Phenytoin = #1 most important** — ~50% of users; boards #1
  2. **Immunosuppressants: Cyclosporine = #1** — 25–70% of users; worst when combined with nifedipine; transplant patients
  3. **Calcium Channel Blockers: Nifedipine, Amlodipine** — 6–25%; verapamil = lower risk
- **Onset: 1–3 months after starting medication** — classic exam timeline. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **Distribution:** Begins at **interdental papillae** → spreads anteriorly and facially. **Edentulous areas SPARED.** Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **Management Step 1 = METICULOUS PLAQUE CONTROL = #1 intervention.** Must optimize oral hygiene BEFORE any other step. Surgery without prior hygiene = ~35% recurrence within 18 months. Source: callout-danger.
- **TRAP (F11):** Do NOT go straight to gingivectomy. Surgery without correcting the causative drug + optimizing hygiene = tissue grows right back. Source: prof-flag-tier-1.
- **Management ladder:**
  1. Plaque control (meticulous)
  2. Antimicrobial rinse: chlorhexidine 0.12% (CHX allergy → Betadine; shellfish allergy = iodine contraindicated)
  3. **Coordinate with prescribing physician** — suggest drug substitution (nifedipine → felodipine; CCB → ACE-I/ARB). NEVER change unilaterally. Regression takes 6 months–1 year.
  4. Gingivectomy — LAST RESORT only after hygiene optimized + drug addressed

### MEDIUM
- Cyclosporine + nifedipine = synergistic effect — transplant patients at highest risk for severe overgrowth
- Verapamil has lower CCB risk than nifedipine/amlodipine
- Risk factors: poor plaque control, male sex (3×), young age, higher drug dose, smoking

## SECTION 6: TRANSIENT LINGUAL PAPILLITIS (TLP) — TIER 1

### HIGH
- **TLP = "lie bumps"** — painful erythematous/whitish papules on fungiform papillae of tip/dorsolateral tongue. Resolves spontaneously in 2–15 days (mean ~7 days).
- **First-line management = REASSURANCE.** Self-limited. Benign condition. Source: prof-emphasis.

### MEDIUM
- Variants: Localized (single papule, adult, most common), Generalized/Eruptive (multiple, may have fever/lymphadenopathy/hypersalivation, ~7 days), Papulokeratotic (hyperkeratotic, less common)
- Symptomatic relief: cold foods/ice chips, topical anesthetics (benzocaine), topical corticosteroids
- Proposed triggers: trauma, stress, food allergies, possible viral URI, GI, hormonal

## SECTION 7: IMMUNOBULLOUS FRAMEWORK (Cross-cutting)

### HIGH
- **Level of cleavage determines disease identity:**
  - PV (Pemphigus Vulgaris): INTRAEPITHELIAL (above BMZ, suprabasal) — desmosomes targeted — flaccid blister
  - MMP/Pemphigoid: SUBEPITHELIAL (at BMZ) — BP180, BP230, laminin 332 targeted — tense blister
  - EBA: Sub-lamina densa (below BMZ) — type VII collagen targeted
  - OLP: Band-like lymphocytic infiltrate at BMZ — no true blistering, erosions
- **Pegasus/Hades mnemonic:**
  - "PemphiGus = Pegasus — flies HIGH BETWEEN the clouds (cells). Intraepithelial, UP HIGH."
  - "PemphigoiD = Hades — lives DEEP underground. Sub-epithelial, DOWN DEEP."

## SECTION 8: ORAL LICHEN PLANUS (OLP) — TIER 1

### HIGH
- **OLP = AUTO-INFLAMMATORY, NOT autoimmune.** Chronic T-cell mediated auto-inflammatory disorder. Use "auto-inflammatory" on this exam. Matters for patient counseling. Source: alg-catG + prof-emphasis.
- **OPMD controversy (Category G — likely exam target):** Prof's updated position: OLP may NOT be a true OPMD. Cancers attributed to OLP transformation were likely dysplasia misdiagnosed as OLP. Ref: Wu (2019), HNJP. On this prof's exam: "OLP is OPMD" = nuanced/controversial answer, NOT a clear yes. Source: alg-catG.
- **Pathognomonic location: Bilateral posterior buccal mucosa + ventral tongue with Wickham striae.** Bilateral = reassuring for OLP. Unilateral or atypical site = BIOPSY NOW. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **Wickham Striae** = lacy white reticulations of OLP. Defines reticular form. Clinical/histopathologic signature finding.
- **4 P's of cutaneous LP:** Purple, Polygonal, Pruritic, Papules on **flexor surfaces (wrists and ankles).** 50% of cutaneous LP patients have oral lesions. **Examine wrists and ankles in dental chair to confirm OLP diagnosis.** Source: prof-mnemonic + prof-emphasis.
- **NEVER apply topical corticosteroids to vermilion lip** — irreversible fat pad atrophy. Use tacrolimus 0.1% on lip instead. Source: callout-danger.
- **Unilateral + high-risk site + progressive = BIOPSY NOW.** Rule out dysplasia. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.

### MEDIUM
- **Reticular OLP:** Most common form. Bilateral symmetric lacy white lines (Wickham striae). Asymptomatic, waxes/wanes. Treatment: none needed — annual monitoring.
- **Erosive OLP:** Atrophic areas ± ulceration; may present as desquamative gingivitis. Painful. Treatment: clobetasol 0.05% BID-TID + dexamethasone soak QID. Follow-up every 3–6 months.
- **Management drugs to memorize:** Clobetasol 0.05% gel (BID-TID) + Dexamethasone 0.5 mg/5 mL soak (5 min, up to QID). Tacrolimus 0.1% ointment BID for vermilion only.
- **OLP treatment-associated candidiasis:** Monitor with prolonged steroid use. Add nystatin if risk.
- **Classic histopathology:** Interface mucositis + band-like lymphocytic infiltrate at BMZ + saw-tooth rete ridges + Civatte bodies (not deeply tested per prof)

### LOW
- Prevalence 0.1–2.2%; F:M = 3:2; middle-aged adults. Thyroid disease association.

## SECTION 9: MUCOUS MEMBRANE PEMPHIGOID (MMP) — TIER 1

### HIGH
- **MMP = Autoimmune SUBEPITHELIAL blistering disease.** Autoantibodies target BMZ proteins: **BP180, BP230, type VII collagen, laminin 332.** Source: key structural table.
- **Most common oral presentation = DESQUAMATIVE GINGIVITIS.** Oral involvement in ~85% of MMP. Erythematous, eroded, peeling gingiva. Source: callout-danger.
- **REFER ALL MMP → OPHTHALMOLOGY, EVEN IF ASYMPTOMATIC.** Ocular involvement in 61–70% of patients. Early symptoms: burning, dryness, foreign body sensation. Conjunctival scarring (symblepharon) = IRREVERSIBLE → BLINDNESS if missed. Source: callout-danger (bold bordered box) + prof-emphasis.
- **Mnemonic:** "Pemphigoid has two i's → two eyes. This disease involves the eyes."
- **Anti-Laminin 332 (+) → ORDER CANCER SCREEN IMMEDIATELY.** 25% of anti-laminin 332-positive patients ALREADY have an occult adenocarcinoma. Life-saving diagnostic step. Source: alg-catG + prof-emphasis.
- **Nikolsky sign = NEGATIVE in MMP** — subepithelial blister is tense; harder to elicit. Contrast: PV = positive.
- **DIF Pattern: Linear IgG, IgA, and/or C3 at basement membrane zone.** Contrast: PV = chicken wire intercellular pattern. Same antibodies, different LOCATION. Source: callout + prof-emphasis.
- **BIOPSY PROTOCOL — 2 SPECIMENS REQUIRED (TRAP F15):**
  - Perilesional tissue (normal-appearing tissue adjacent to blister)
  - Specimen 1: H&E histology → **10% formalin**
  - Specimen 2: DIF immunofluorescence → **Michel's solution** (formalin DESTROYS immunoglobulins needed for DIF)
  - Putting all tissue in formalin = destroys DIF = cannot diagnose. Source: alg-biopsy-trap + prof-flag-tier-1.
- **"Don't Lose Your Sushi"** — MMP epithelium extremely fragile; detaches easily. Warn assistant: NO close suctioning near biopsy site. Epithelium will flap off into suction. Source: pearl-callout.
- **Blister type: TENSE** — intact, dome-shaped bullae (vs. PV = flaccid)

### MEDIUM
- **Management by risk:**
  - Low risk (oral ± skin): Clobetasol 0.05% gel BID-TID + Dexamethasone 0.5 mg/5 mL soak QID — MEMORIZE
  - High risk (ocular, nasal, laryngeal, esophageal, genital): Systemic therapy — refer to oral med/derm/ophthalmology team
- Nasal involvement: 20–40%. Laryngeal: 5–10%.
- Incidence ~2/million/year; mean age 60–65; slight female predominance.
- IIF (indirect immunofluorescence on blood) less accurate than DIF — skip; DIF is primary.

## SECTION 10: PEMPHIGUS VULGARIS (PV) — TIER 1

### HIGH
- **>50% PRESENT WITH ORAL LESIONS FIRST** — precede skin by months to over a year. Dentist often FIRST clinician to see this potentially fatal disease. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **Prof mantra: "Oral lesions first to show, last to go."**
- **Nikolsky Sign = POSITIVE in PV** — intraepithelial cleavage; lateral pressure on normal mucosa creates blister easily. Source: callout-danger.
- **Pathomechanism:** Autoantibodies attack desmoglein (Dsg3, Dsg1) → ACANTHOLYSIS (epithelial cells lose cohesion/fall apart). Intraepithelial/suprabasal cleavage — above BMZ.
- **DSG antibody–phenotype table (TRAP F13):**
  - Anti-DSG3 only → Mucosal-dominant PV — **oral only** (DSG1 compensates in skin)
  - Anti-DSG3 + Anti-DSG1 → Mucocutaneous PV — **oral + skin** (both targets attacked)
  - Anti-DSG1 only → Pemphigus foliaceus — **skin only, NO oral** (DSG3 compensates in mucosa)
  - DSG3 = oral; DSG1 = skin; both = oral + skin. Don't blur these. Source: prof-flag-tier-1 (Trap F13).
- **DIF Pattern: "Chicken wire" / fishnet / intercellular netting of IgG/C3 between keratinocytes in spinous layer.** Pathognomonic for PV. Source: callout + prof-emphasis.
- **Pegasus mnemonic:** "PemPHIGus sounds like Pegasus. Pegasus flies BETWEEN the clouds. Cells = clouds. Acantholysis = space between clouds. INTRAEPITHELIAL (suprabasal)."
- **BIOPSY BEFORE ANY TREATMENT — CRITICAL RULE.** Topical steroids suppress autoantibodies needed for DIF diagnosis. Protocol: (1) PERILESIONAL biopsy — 2 specimens (formalin H&E + Michel's DIF); (2) Begin topical steroids on non-biopsy areas immediately; (3) Wait **5–7 days** before applying steroids to biopsy site. Source: alg-biopsy-trap + prof-emphasis.
- **PV = ALL surfaces (keratinized AND nonkeratinized)** — unlike RAS which is nonkeratinized only.
- **PDAI Severity Classification:**
  - <15: Mild → topical corticosteroids only
  - 15–45: Moderate → dermatology consult STAT; rituximab + prednisone 0.5 mg/kg
  - **>45: SEVERE — MEDICAL EMERGENCY → Emergency Department (ED) immediately**
- **PV is rare but fatal if untreated.** Source: prof-emphasis.

### MEDIUM
- Blister type: **FLACCID** — ruptures immediately on formation (vs. MMP = tense)
- DSG compensation explanation: Mucosa expresses mostly DSG3 → anti-DSG3 hits mucosa unopposed. Skin expresses both DSG1 + DSG3 → DSG1 compensates → delays skin involvement. Reference: Stanley JR, Amagai M (2006) NEJM.
- Both DIF protocols: same two-vial rule as MMP (formalin H&E + Michel's for DIF)

## SECTION 11: ERYTHEMA MULTIFORME (EM) / RIME — TIER 1

### HIGH
- **EM/RIME and SJS/TEN are SEPARATE DISEASES — NOT a spectrum.** Old teaching (EM → SJS → TEN = severity continuum) = WRONG per this professor. Different causes, different prognoses, different management. RIME = preferred modern term. Source: alg-sep-entities + prof-emphasis.
- **THE 90% RULE (core distinguisher):**
  - **INFECTION (90%)** → EM/RIME → good prognosis, self-limiting
  - **DRUG (90%)** → SJS/TEN → potentially FATAL
  Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **EM vs RIME — 5-row comparison:**
  - Defining feature: EM = raised 3-ring "iris" target lesions; RIME = prominent mucositis (oral dominant)
  - Skin: EM = prominent, acral distribution; RIME = minimal or absent
  - Mucosa: EM = variable (minor=none/1 site; major=≥2 sites); RIME = always prominent (oral + ocular + genital)
  - Trigger: EM = HSV ~70%; RIME = Mycoplasma + other respiratory pathogens
  - Typical patient: EM = young adults; RIME = children/adolescents
- **CHILDREN: topical lidocaine → seizures** (absorbed through inflamed mucosa). Use benzocaine or diphenhydramine instead. Source: callout + prof-emphasis.
- **Recurrent EM prophylaxis (HSV-triggered):** Valacyclovir 500 mg BID for ≥6 months, then taper. Source: pearl-callout.

### MEDIUM
- EM management: Lidocaine 2% gel before meals; magic mouthwash q4h; dexamethasone soak; sucralfate suspension
- EM: target lesions are raised 3-ring "iris" type — classic clinical identifier
- RIME oral lesion: severe hemorrhagic lip crusting + oral erosions + conjunctivitis ± genital ulcers with minimal skin involvement

## SECTION 12: STEVENS-JOHNSON SYNDROME (SJS) / TOXIC EPIDERMAL NECROLYSIS (TEN) — TIER 1 EMERGENCY

### HIGH
- **EMERGENCY — POTENTIALLY FATAL. Drug-triggered in ~85–90% of cases.**
- **BSA Classification (MEMORIZE):**
  - SJS: **<10% BSA** — mortality 1–5%
  - SJS/TEN Overlap: **10–30% BSA**
  - TEN: **>30% BSA** — mortality **25–50%**
- **#1 DRUG TO MEMORIZE FOR SJS/TEN: ALLOPURINOL** — highest absolute risk per new user of ANY drug for SJS/TEN. Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.
- **High-risk drugs (Boards favorite):**
  - Allopurinol (#1 highest absolute risk per new user)
  - Sulfonamides / TMP-SMX
  - Beta-lactams, fluoroquinolones
  - Anticonvulsants: carbamazepine, phenytoin, lamotrigine, phenobarbital
  - COX-2 NSAIDs (especially etoricoxib)
- **Carbamazepine + Han Chinese patients = HLA-B*1502 SCREENING REQUIRED.** Dentists prescribe carbamazepine for trigeminal neuralgia. Han Chinese carry HLA-B*1502 at dramatically elevated rates → greatly increased SJS risk. MUST screen before prescribing. Source: alg-catG + prof-emphasis.
- **SJS/TEN onset timeline: 1–3 weeks after starting a new medication.** Always take medication history. Source: pearl-callout.
- **SCORTEN Mortality Score:**
  - 7 factors × 1 point each: Age ≥40, HR >120 bpm, malignancy present, BSA detachment >10%, BUN >28 mg/dL, glucose >252 mg/dL, bicarbonate <20 mEq/L
  - Score 0–1 = 3% mortality; 2 = 12%; 3 = 35%; 4 = 58%; ≥5 = **90% mortality**
  Source: prof-emphasis.
- **Dentist's 4-step role:**
  1. RECOGNIZE: new drug + flu-like prodrome + oral erosions + skin macules → suspect SJS/TEN
  2. STOP THE SUSPECTED DRUG IMMEDIATELY
  3. Refer to ED / burn unit urgently (Call 911 if severe)
  4. Document ALL current medications and start dates
  Source: callout-danger + prof-emphasis.

### MEDIUM
- Perioral hemorrhagic crusting = clinical hallmark of SJS
- BSA assessment methods: Wallace Rule of Nines; Palmar Method (patient's palm = 1% TBSA)
- 50% develop permanent ocular sequelae
- Inpatient supportive care: magic mouthwash + lidocaine 2%, CHX 0.12% rinse, petroleum jelly to lips frequently, gentle saline irrigation, avoid aggressive debridement

## SECTION 13: PARANEOPLASTIC PEMPHIGUS / PAMS (PNP/PAMS) — TIER 2/3

### HIGH
- **ALWAYS ASSOCIATED WITH UNDERLYING NEOPLASM (known or occult)** → trigger full cancer workup in any patient with PNP/PAMS pattern. Source: callout-danger.
- **ORAL LESIONS FIRST in ~45% of patients** — often precedes cancer diagnosis. Dentist may be first to catch an occult malignancy. Source: callout-danger.
- **MORTALITY 60–90% — most deaths within first year.** Source: callout-danger.
- **Intractable stomatitis that does NOT respond to standard therapy → think PNP/PAMS → cancer workup.** Source: callout-danger.
- **Bronchiolitis obliterans = major cause of death** — autoantibodies attack respiratory epithelium → progressive, often irreversible airway destruction. Source: callout-danger.

### MEDIUM
- **Associated malignancies (Boards favorite):**
  - Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL): ~40% (#1)
  - Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL): ~20%
  - Castleman Disease: ~18% (best prognosis — surgically removable)
- Five mucocutaneous phenotypes: Pemphigus-like, Pemphigoid-like, EM-like, GVHD-like, LP-like
- Rituximab less effective for PNP than for regular PV
- Better prognosis: Castleman disease (surgically removable), lichenoid pattern, oral-only involvement
- Ocular: cicatrizing conjunctivitis, symblepharon
- Supportive oral care: dexamethasone + clobetasol + magic mouthwash + lidocaine 2% gel + CHX 0.12% + lanolin/petroleum jelly for hemorrhagic crusting + nutritional evaluation

## SECTION 15: DRUG ALLERGY / CONTACT STOMATITIS RAPID-REFERENCE

### HIGH
- **Latex allergy → Type I (IgE-mediated)** — latex-free protocol required in office
- **CHX allergy → substitute povidone-iodine/Betadine; shellfish allergy = iodine contraindicated**
- **"LA allergy" = almost always pseudoallergic (bisulfite preservative reaction), NOT true IgE allergy.** Bisulfites = the preservative in dental local anesthetics.
- **Plasma Cell Gingivitis (PCG) trigger = cinnamon / cinnamaldehyde.** Ask about ALL cinnamon-containing products (toothpaste, mouthwash, gum, candy, foods). Eliminate → expect improvement. 70% of OFG patients improve in 12 weeks on cinnamon/benzoate-free diet. Source: prof-mnemonic.
- **Type IV triggers in dental practice:** Metals (nickel, chromium, cobalt), acrylates, amalgam, flavorings → lichenoid reactions, denture stomatitis, contact cheilitis, PCG

### MEDIUM
- **Drug–Disease master associations:**
  - Phenytoin → DRGH (#1) + SJS/TEN
  - Cyclosporine → DRGH (worst with nifedipine)
  - Nifedipine / Amlodipine → DRGH
  - Carbamazepine → SJS/TEN (screen HLA-B*1502 in Han Chinese); also DRGH (lower risk)
  - Allopurinol → SJS/TEN (#1 absolute risk)
  - Sulfonamides / TMP-SMX → SJS/TEN (classic boards)
  - COX-2 NSAIDs (etoricoxib) → SJS/TEN highest-risk NSAID
  - Cinnamon / cinnamaldehyde → PCG
  - Beta-blockers, bisphosphonates, nicorandil, NSAIDs → medication-induced RAS

## SECTION 16: IBS vs IBD TRAP + SYSTEMIC ASSOCIATIONS

### MEDIUM
- **Systemic associations with RAS:** Celiac disease, IBD, Behcet's syndrome, cyclic neutropenia, PFAPA, HIV
- **New-onset RAS after age 40 without childhood history** must trigger workup for all of the above
- **IBD (Crohn's, UC) can cause RAS** — part of the mandatory workup
- Note: IBS is NOT associated with RAS (IBS is functional, IBD is inflammatory)
