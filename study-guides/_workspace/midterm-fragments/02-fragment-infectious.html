L1 Meta-Rules — Things That Apply Across Diseases

Cross-cutting rules from highlighted slides · the "always / never" master list
DPH-REPORTABLE PATHOGENS (L1)
  • Tuberculosis — mandatory MA Department of Public Health report.
  • Syphilis (1°, 2°, 3°) — mandatory.
  • Measles (Rubeola) — mandatory; immediate phone notification.
  • Sully annotation: "Syphilis + TB = both mandatory reportable" — flagged on the TB intro slide.

BIOPSY TIMING RULES
  • TB: NEVER biopsy first — PPE + ID referral + airborne workup FIRST. (Sully: "protect yourself first.")

  • Topical lidocaine in kids — AVOID (seizure risk).
  • Topical benzocaine in kids — AVOID (methemoglobinemia).

Section 02 · Bacterial

Bacterial Infections of the Oral & Perioral Region

Impetigo (the "honey-crust" pediatric case) and Syphilis — the great imitator across primary, secondary, and tertiary stages.

Impetigo High Yield

Most common bacterial skin infection in kids 2–5 yo · classic case: 7-year-old, perinasal honey crusts

Henderson's case (Slide intro)

🧑🏻‍⚕️
CASE · 7yo
Healthy 7-year-old presents with bilateral honey-colored crusts at the nares and on the maxillary lip (each ~1.0 × 0.5 cm) on an erythematous base. No systemic symptoms. Recent runny nose. Lives in warm humid climate.

Pathogen & epidemiology

  • Staphylococcus aureus and/or β-hemolytic Streptococcus (group A strep).
  • Most common bacterial skin infection in children 2–5 years old (highlighted) — the case is 7yo but the textbook age is 2–5.
  • Risk triad (highlighted): broken skin + runny nose + warm moist climate.

Lesion appearance — pathognomonic

PATHOGNOMONIC

Honey-colored crusts — described as "cornflakes glued to the surface." Multifocal, on an erythematous base. Most often perinasal/perioral.

Differential: Impetigo vs HSV

Impetigo

  • Multifocal CRUSTS ("cornflakes glued")
  • Honey color
  • Perinasal/perioral
  • Bacterial

HSV (e.g., RHL)

  • Grouped VESICLES on erythematous base
  • Vesicles rupture, then crust
  • Vermillion border / nasal tip
  • Viral — prodromal tingling

Treatment

Topical (limited disease)

Mupirocin 2% TID × 5 days — exfoliate the crusts first with a warm cloth, then apply.

Systemic (extensive disease) — oral × 7 days
Weight cutoffAugmentin dose
< 40 kgAmoxicillin 25 mg/kg/day + clavulanate 125 mg, divided BID
≥ 40 kgAmoxicillin 500 mg + clavulanate 125 mg
DOSING RULE

Augmentin cutoff is WEIGHT, not age. 40 kg is the line — not "child vs adult."

Syphilis 1° (Primary Chancre) High Yield

Case: 28-year-old, painless indurated lateral tongue ulcer · "the great imitator"
🧑🏼‍⚕️
CASE · 28yo
28-year-old presents with a 1.0 × 0.3 cm painless ulcer on the lateral tongue. Indurated, clean base, regional cervical lymphadenopathy. No prodrome. Recent unprotected oral sex.

Pathogen & pathology

  • Treponema pallidum — spirochete bacterium. On biopsy: "cursed corkscrews" (Henderson mnemonic, highlighted).
  • DPH REPORTABLE (mandatory MA Department of Public Health report).

Chancre timeline & sites

  • Onset: 3–90 days post-exposure (highlighted).
  • Site distribution (highlighted): 85% genital · 10% anal · 4% oral · 1% other.
  • Heals spontaneously in 3–8 weeks even untreated — but the patient is NOT cured (organism disseminates).

Oral chancre — the four hallmark features

CHANCRE = "THE GREAT IMITATOR"
  • Painless (key differentiator from aphthous and TUG).
  • Indurated borders (firm to palpation).
  • Clean base (no necrotic exudate).
  • Regional lymphadenopathy (firm, non-tender).

Differential diagnosis

  • SCC — raised, rolled, induced borders. (Sully annotation: "induced border = SCC; clean + indurated = chancre.")
  • Aphthous ulcer — painful, non-keratinized, no induration.
  • Traumatic ulcerative granuloma (TUG) — painful, non-indurated.
  • Mpox — vesiculopustular, often perioral.

Diagnosis — biopsy beats serology early

TestSensitivityWhen to use
IHC (immunohistochemistry)64–94%First-line on biopsy
Silver stain (Warthin-Starry)0–41%Older method, much less sensitive
Serology (RPR/VDRL)NEGATIVE up to 6 weeksUse AFTER 6 wks; NOT reliable early

Serology can be NEGATIVE for the first 6 weeks. If you suspect chancre and serology is negative — DON'T rule it out. Biopsy with IHC trumps early serology.

Treatment

Benzathine Penicillin G — 2.4 million units IM × 1 dose. Refer to PCP / ID. Chancre heals on its own in 3–8 wks but treatment is required to prevent dissemination.

Syphilis 2° (Secondary / Disseminated) High Yield

4–10 weeks after chancre · oral triad + systemic rash · HIGHLY infectious

Timing

Onset: 4–10 weeks after primary infection (after the chancre has resolved). Patient often thinks the chancre "healed" — now systemic.

Systemic symptoms

  • Painless generalized lymphadenopathy
  • Maculopapular rash — classically involves palms and soles
  • Musculoskeletal pain, sore throat, low-grade fever, malaise

Oral triad (highlighted) — ALL three are exam targets

Split Papules

  • Ulcerated mass at the commissure
  • Looks like a fissured "split"

Mucous Patches

  • White, slightly elevated patches
  • Converge into a serpentine pattern on buccal/tongue

Condyloma Lata

  • Look like papillomas — but NOT HPV
  • Broad, moist, exophytic lesions

Oral lesions of secondary syphilis are HIGHLY INFECTIOUS. Serpentine mucous patches on buccal/tongue shed spirochetes — gloves + droplet precautions.

Diagnosis & treatment

By this stage serology IS reliable (RPR/VDRL + treponemal test). Tx: Benzathine Penicillin G 2.4 MU IM × 1 — same as primary.

Syphilis 3° (Tertiary / Late) Medium Yield

1–30 year latency · only 30% of untreated 2° progress · gumma + tabes dorsalis

Latency & progression

  • Latency: 1–30 years after secondary (highlighted).
  • 30% of untreated secondary cases progress to tertiary (highlighted).

Oral findings

PATHOGNOMONIC
  • Gumma — destructive granulomatous mass on the palate or tongue. Can perforate the hard palate.
  • Interstitial glossitis — atrophic, fissured tongue from endarteritis → chronic ischemia.

Systemic findings

  • Cardiovascular: aortitis, ascending aortic aneurysm, aortic regurgitation, CHF.
  • Neurosyphilis: tabes dorsalis (degeneration of the posterior columns → sensory ataxia, lightning pains), general paresis, dementia, Argyll Robertson pupils.

Reporting: still mandatory MA DPH report at any stage.

Quick Check — Bacterial

A 28-year-old has a 1.0 cm painless, indurated, clean-based ulcer on the lateral tongue with regional cervical LAD. RPR is negative. What's the next best step?

  1. Reassure the patient that syphilis is ruled out by the negative RPR.
  2. Biopsy with immunohistochemistry — serology can be negative for the first 6 weeks.
  3. Start empiric Augmentin 500/125 mg orally.
  4. Repeat the RPR in 6 months.
Primary syphilis serology has a 6-week window of false-negativity. Biopsy with IHC has 64–94% sensitivity vs silver stain at 0–41%. The lesion clinically fits a chancre — biopsy now.
Section 03 · TB & Candida

Tuberculosis & Candidiasis

Two infections you'll be tested on for opposite reasons: TB for "don't biopsy first," Candida for the 6-subtype table and "-azole" drug list.

Tuberculosis (Oral / Secondary) High Yield

Case: 54yo on methotrexate · large posterior tongue ulcer + 2-month cough · "DON'T BIOPSY FIRST"
👨🏻
CASE · 54yo
54-year-old with rheumatoid arthritis on methotrexate. Right posterior lateral tongue: 2.0 × 1.5 cm deep, papillary, irregular ulcer. Two months of productive cough. No BCG vaccination history. Recently traveled.

Pathogen & epidemiology

  • Mycobacterium tuberculosis — airborne acid-fast bacillus.
  • Oral TB is RARE — and ALWAYS secondary to pulmonary disease.
  • Most common oral site: tongue >>> everything else (highlighted).

Lesion description

  • Chronic, painful, irregular ulcer (often deep, undermined edges).
  • Constitutional symptoms: weight loss, night sweats, productive cough, hemoptysis, low-grade fever.
  • Risk factors: immunosuppression (MTX, biologics, HIV), foreign-born, congregate living, healthcare workers.

DO NOT BIOPSY FIRST. Workup + airborne PPE BEFORE any tissue manipulation. Refer ID. (Sully: "protect yourself first".) An aerosol-generating biopsy on undiagnosed pulmonary TB exposes the entire clinic.

Diagnostic workup

  • NAAT — Xpert MTB/RIF assay on sputum (highlighted — first-line nucleic acid amplification test).
  • Chest X-ray / CT (cavitary upper lobe disease, miliary pattern).
  • IGRA (QuantiFERON-TB Gold) — preferred over PPD in BCG-vaccinated.
  • Tongue biopsy only after the systemic workup is underway and PPE is in place.

Treatment — DOT (Directly Observed Therapy)

Phase 1 — 8 weeks (highlighted)

PIRE: Pyrazinamide + Isoniazid + Rifampin + Ethambutol

Phase 2 — 16 weeks (highlighted)

IR: Isoniazid + Rifampin

Total ~24 weeks of DOT. Oral TB self-resolves with systemic treatment — the tongue lesion does not need separate intervention.

DPH REPORTABLE

TB is mandatorily reportable to the MA Department of Public Health. (Sully annotation on the slide: "Syphilis + TB = both mandatory reportable.")

Candidiasis — 6 Subtypes High Yield

Case: 42yo asthmatic, inhaler without spacer/rinse, recent Z-pack · multifocal palatal plaques that wipe off
👩🏻
CASE · 42yo
42-year-old asthmatic. Uses inhaled corticosteroid without a spacer and does not rinse. Just finished a Z-pack for sinusitis. Multifocal white plaques on the soft palate, uvula, and tonsillar pillar that wipe off with gauze.

Background

  • Carriers: 30–50% of the population harbor Candida albicans as commensal flora.
  • Three factors determine infection vs colonization: (1) host immune status, (2) oral environment, (3) C. albicans strain.
  • Triggers: antibiotics, steroids (inhaled or systemic), hyposalivation, immunosuppression, dentures, vertical-dimension loss.

The 6 subtypes (Jabra-Rizk 2016 reference)

Erythematous Red macules; burning; hyposalivation = unique trigger Posterior hard palate, buccal, dorsal tongue Antibiotics, hyposalivation, immunosuppression
Median rhomboid glossitis (central papillary atrophy) Red, atrophic, asymptomatic; "kissing lesion" mirror on palate Midline posterior dorsal tongue Idiopathic, immunosuppression

"WIPE-OFF" RULE

Pseudomembranous (thrush) wipes off — reveals a red base. Hyperplastic does NOT wipe off — biopsy to rule out dysplasia.

For inhaled-corticosteroid candida: (1) rinse the mouth after every inhaler use, (2) use a spacer. Both are highlighted on the slide. The case 42yo did neither.

Quick Check — TB & Candida

Which Candida subtype is the LEAST likely to be a true infection of the mucosa, despite often culturing positive?

  1. Pseudomembranous
  2. Denture stomatitis
  3. Median rhomboid glossitis
  4. Hyperplastic

Garden Variety HSV — Primary vs Recurrent High Yield

Slide 38 comparison table · both columns are highlighted = both are exam fodder

PrHGS (Primary Herpetic Gingivostomatitis)

  • Age: 6 months – 5 years
  • Prodrome: cervical LAD + chills + fever + nausea + anorexia + irritability
  • Lesion: pinhead vesicles → coalesce → ulcer
  • Location: KERATINIZED tissue (attached gingiva, hard palate)
  • Duration: 5 days – 2 weeks
  • Adults: pharyngotonsillitis; rarely Bell palsy / vestibular neuritis

RHL (Recurrent Herpes Labialis)

  • Prevalence: 15–45% of US = "cold sore" / "fever blister"
  • Prodrome: itching / tingling / warmth / erythema 6–24 hrs before vesicles
  • Vesicles rupture and crust within 2 days
  • Location: vermillion border (most common), nasal, perioral
  • HIGHLY transmissible at vesicle rupture — avoid manipulation until scabbed
PrHGS vs APHTHOUS — THE LOCATION RULE

PrHGS → KERATINIZED tissue (attached gingiva, hard palate, dorsal tongue). Aphthous → NON-keratinized tissue (buccal, labial, soft palate, ventral tongue, FOM). This is the single most reliable differentiator on a clinical exam.

Nasal HSV Case — 34-year-old High Yield

"Itchy stinging sore on nose" + recent stress + COVID + cervical LAD
🧑🏻
CASE · 34yo
34-year-old presents with a 0.8 × 0.3 cm focal cluster of pinhead vesicles on the right alar rim. Itchy / stinging. Recent severe breakup + recent COVID infection. Cervical lymphadenopathy.

Differential

  • HSV (most likely) — grouped vesicles, prodromal tingling, immunosuppression trigger.
  • Impetigo — honey crusts, multifocal, no prodrome.
  • VZV (zoster) — dermatomal, nasal tip = Hutchinson sign → HZO emergency.
  • Superficial abrasion — trauma history, no vesicles.

Treatment

Valacyclovir 1 g PO BID × 5 days for nasal/cutaneous HSV.

Mandatory ophthalmology referral if the lesion is on the nasal tip — rule out Hutchinson sign / HZO (covered in next card).

Hutchinson Sign & HZO — OCULAR EMERGENCY High Yield

Nasal-tip VZV → V1/nasociliary → cornea → potential blindness

Hutchinson sign = a VZV (or HSV) vesicular lesion on the nasal tip. The nasal tip is innervated by the nasociliary branch of the ophthalmic division (V1) of the trigeminal nerve, which also supplies the cornea. A nasal-tip lesion predicts HZO (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) — corneal ulceration → scarring → perforation → BLINDNESS.

What to do

  • Mandatory same-day ophthalmology referral — ambulance-level urgency if symptomatic.
  • Start systemic antivirals empirically: Valacyclovir 1 g PO TID for HZO (per ophthalmology guidance).
  • NEVER manage corneal involvement in the dental chair.

Why this matters: A patient walks in with what looks like a "cold sore on the nose" — if it's on the nasal tip, you cannot just hand them Abreva and send them home. The same dermatomal nerve serves the eye. Miss this, the patient loses an eye.

Quick Check — Herpes

A 4-year-old presents with painful pinhead vesicles on the attached gingiva and hard palate, fever, and cervical LAD. The most likely diagnosis is:

  1. Major aphthous ulcer
  2. Primary herpetic gingivostomatitis (PrHGS)
  3. Recurrent herpes labialis
  4. Hand-foot-and-mouth disease
PrHGS hits 6 mo–5 yr olds, prefers KERATINIZED tissue (attached gingiva, hard palate), and presents with prodromal fever + cervical LAD. Aphthous prefers NON-keratinized tissue and lacks the systemic prodrome.
Section 05 · CMV & Measles

CMV & Measles — The Pathognomonic Pair

"Owl-eye" nucleus on biopsy and Koplik spots on the buccal — two findings that should trigger an instant diagnosis on a vignette.

CMV (HHV-5) Oral Ulcer High Yield

Case: 36yo HIV+ off ART · 4-quadrant ulcers · failed acyclovir — THE clue for CMV over HSV
👩🏻
CASE · 36yo HIV+
36-year-old female, HIV+, off ART. Four-quadrant ulcers: palate 2.0 × 1.7 cm, R lateral tongue 2.5 × 2.0 cm, R upper lip 1.2 × 1.0 cm, R lower lip 0.8 × 0.6 cm + R vestibule 0.4 × 0.2 cm. Failed 10 days of acyclovir. 10 lb weight loss. Low-grade fevers.

Differential

  • CMV — immunosuppressed, acyclovir failure, deep ulcers.
  • TB — constitutional symptoms, irregular ulcer.
  • Deep fungal (histo, blasto, crypto) — immunosuppressed.
  • Granulomatous disease (sarcoid, GPA).

Workup

  • Biopsy × 2 sites + CBC + CD4 count + HIV viral load.
  • Histology: "Owl-eye" intranuclear inclusions — pathognomonic (highlighted).
  • Serology: ELISA — IgG + IgM; viral culture; PCR.
PATHOGNOMONIC HISTOLOGY

"Owl-eye" nucleus on biopsy = CMV. The intranuclear inclusion + halo + thickened nuclear membrane resembles an owl's face. If you see this, you don't need anything else.

Acyclovir does NOT work on CMV. A patient who "failed" acyclovir for what looked like HSV ulcers is the case-vignette clue for CMV. Switch to ganciclovir/valganciclovir.

Management

  • ID referral.
  • Valganciclovir 900 mg PO BID (duration not stated on the slide; per ID).
  • Supportive: topical anesthetic (adults), nutritional support, acetaminophen.
  • In immunocompetent hosts: NSAIDs are typically sufficient (CMV ulcers are usually self-limited).

Epidemiology & sequelae (highlighted facts)

  • Prevalence: 83% of the population — older age, women, lower SES, foreign-born.
  • #1 cause of nonhereditary sensorineural hearing loss (highlighted) — congenital CMV.
  • Clinically evident in neonates and the immunosuppressed; often subclinical otherwise.
  • PrEP / education for at-risk populations.

Measles (Rubeola) High Yield

Case: 6yo unvaccinated · Koplik spots = pathognomonic · "do not treat in dental clinic"
🧑🏻
CASE · 6yo unvaccinated
6-year-old, unvaccinated. Fever 103°F + cough + rhinorrhea + conjunctivitis + photophobia + maculopapular rash. Blue-white pinpoint macules on the right buccal mucosa, gingiva, and lip. Coated tongue.

Pathognomonic — Koplik spots

VERBATIM (highlighted)

"Koplik spots: pathognomonic bluish-white plaques on buccal mucosa (70% of patients)." "Appear 1–2 days BEFORE the rash — key early diagnostic sign."

The 3 C's (verbatim)

  • Cough
  • Coryza (profuse rhinorrhea)
  • Conjunctivitis

Plus photophobia + cephalocaudal maculopapular rash (head → trunk → extremities).

Infectious window (highlighted)

4 days BEFORE rash onset → 4 days AFTER rash onset. The patient is most contagious during the prodrome — before you even know it's measles.

Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)

  • MMR vaccine within 72 hours of exposure (highlighted), OR
  • Immunoglobulin (Ig) within 6 days of exposure (highlighted).

Vitamin A (hospitalized children context)

Vitamin A 200,000 IU for children ≥ 12 months old (highlighted). Reduces measles mortality and morbidity.

Complications

  • Encephalitis: 1 in 1,000 (highlighted).
  • Pneumonia, otitis media, diarrhea.
  • Subacute sclerosing panencephalitis (SSPE) — rare, late, fatal.

DDx for Koplik spots (don't get fooled)

  • Candidiasis (pseudomembranous wipes off; Koplik does not).
  • Fordyce granules (asymptomatic, no fever, no prodrome).
  • Pyostomatitis vegetans (IBD-associated).

DO NOT TREAT in the dental clinic. Active measles requires airborne isolation — N-95 + negative-pressure room. Direct the patient to the ED. DPH REPORTABLE (immediate phone notification).

Quick Check — CMV & Measles

A 36-year-old HIV+ patient (off ART) presents with deep, painful, multi-quadrant ulcers that have failed 10 days of acyclovir. The next best step is:

  1. Increase acyclovir to 800 mg PO 5×/day × 7 days.
  2. Switch to oral fluconazole.
  3. Biopsy × 2 sites looking for "owl-eye" inclusions; refer ID; consider valganciclovir.
  4. Reassure that HSV will resolve with continued acyclovir.
Acyclovir failure in an immunosuppressed patient is the case-vignette clue for CMV. Acyclovir does not cover CMV. Biopsy → "owl-eye" pathognomonic; valganciclovir 900 mg PO BID is the correct antiviral.
Section 06 · Mumps & EBV

Mumps & EBV Mucocutaneous Ulcer

The 20yo college parotitis case (with the trap that 10–30% have NO parotid swelling) and the 46yo transplant ulcer that requires EBER ISH to distinguish from lymphoma.

Mumps (Endemic Parotitis) High Yield

Case: 20yo college student · bilateral parotid swelling · the "no-parotitis" trap
🧑🏻‍🏫
CASE · 20yo
20-year-old college student. 3 days of low-grade fever (100.8°F), malaise, headache, myalgia, then sudden bilateral parotid swelling. Pain on sour foods. Trismus. MMR × 2 documented. Dorm contacts. One tender, swollen testicle.

Lesion description

Bilateral symmetric facial swelling at the parotids, descending below the angle of mandible to submental region. Mild generalized erythema. Sour foods provoke pain (sialagogue effect on inflamed gland).

Differential

  • Mumps
  • Sialolithiasis
  • Sialadenitis (HIV / EBV / influenza)
  • Sjögren syndrome

The big traps (highlighted)

"Parotitis is classic BUT 10–30% have NO parotid swelling."

"Serious complications can occur WITHOUT parotitis."

"Mumps is a systemic infection — can affect CNS (~50%), pancreas, reproductive, auditory."

Diagnosis

  • Gold standard: RT-PCR from buccal/oral swab.
  • IgM serology caveat (highlighted): "IgM serology can help BUT vaccinated patients may not mount an IgM response." So IgM negative does NOT rule out mumps in someone with prior vaccination — do the PCR.

Transmission timeline (highlighted)

  • Contagious: 1–2 days before symptoms → 14 days after clinical resolution.
  • Saliva virus shedding: 7 days before to 9 days after symptom onset.
  • Incubation: 16–18 days (range 12–25).

Complications

  • Orchitis: 3–10% of postpubertal males.
  • Subfertility: 13%.
  • CNS involvement: ~50% (most subclinical) — including without parotitis.
  • Hearing loss / encephalitis: now < 1% in vaccinated era (was a major morbidity prevaccine).

Management

  • Analgesics / antipyretics · bed rest · avoid sour foods (sialagogues) · warm/cool compresses.
  • 3rd MMR dose during outbreak for close contacts: 61–88% additional protection (highlighted).
  • 2-dose MMR schedule is 88% effective — immunity wanes; outbreak boosters are real.

Bottom line for the test: the case patient is fully vaccinated AND has clinical mumps. The slide highlights this exact paradox — vaccine immunity wanes, IgM may not rise, and 10–30% don't even get parotid swelling. PCR is the answer.

EBV Mucocutaneous Ulcer (MCU) High Yield

Case: 46yo post-renal transplant on tacrolimus + MMF · sharply circumscribed ulcer · EBER ISH required
🧑🏻
CASE · 46yo transplant
46-year-old post-renal transplant on tacrolimus + mycophenolate mofetil × 3 years. Right buccal vestibule extending to gingiva #18–20: necrotic ulcer 2.0 × 2.0 cm with mild erythematous halo. Rapidly enlarging over 4–6 weeks. B-symptoms (fever, night sweats, 15 lb weight loss). Cervical LAD.

Differential

  • EBV MCU
  • CMV ulcer
  • Neutropenic ulcer (drug-induced)
  • SCC (always on the DDx for a non-healing ulcer)

Workup

  • Biopsy + EBER in-situ hybridization (EBER ISH) — required (highlighted).
  • CBC.
  • MMF (mycophenolate) drug levels.

Henderson framing (verbatim, highlighted)

VERBATIM

"Localized, self-limited lymphoproliferative disorder with excellent prognosis."

"~45% regress spontaneously."

"NO disease-related deaths."

Who gets it (highlighted)

  • 91% have immunosuppression.
  • Canonical iatrogenic triggers: methotrexate, azathioprine, cyclosporine. (The case used tacrolimus + MMF — same idea.)
  • Age-related type: median age ~77 years.

Where it is

  • Most common site: oropharynx 62% (also skin and GI).
  • Appearance: "sharply circumscribed, shallow ulcer" (highlighted).
  • Mimics: malignancy (lymphoma, SCC), ANUG, traumatic ulcer.

How to distinguish from lymphoma

EBER ISH + 3 LAB CRITERIA

Biopsy + EBER in-situ hybridization is required. To distinguish from EBV-driven lymphoma: normal LDH + normal IL-2R + EBV DNA < 1,000 copies/mL.

Management

  • Conservative: observe for spontaneous regression OR reduce immunosuppression (with the transplant team).
  • Chemotherapy is rarely needed.
  • Re-biopsy any recurrence — rule out true lymphoma transformation.

Quick Check — Mumps & EBV

A 46yo on tacrolimus has a 2 cm sharply circumscribed shallow ulcer. Biopsy shows atypical large lymphoid cells. The single best confirmatory test is:

  1. PET/CT to stage lymphoma.
  2. EBER in-situ hybridization on the biopsy specimen.
  3. Serum EBV IgM.
  4. Empiric R-CHOP chemotherapy.
EBER ISH is required to call EBV MCU. Combined with normal LDH, normal IL-2R, and EBV DNA < 1,000 copies/mL, it distinguishes a self-limited MCU (45% spontaneously regress, no deaths) from true lymphoma. Without EBER ISH, you can't make the call — the histology overlaps.

AVOID Rules / Dental Red-Lines

"Never do this" master list for L1
  • TB suspected: NEVER biopsy first — workup + airborne PPE + ID referral first.
  • Measles: NEVER treat in dental clinic — N-95 + negative-pressure room mandatory.
  • Nasal-tip HSV/VZV (Hutchinson sign): mandatory ophthalmology referral — HZO/blindness risk.

  • Augmentin dosing: use WEIGHT not age — cutoff is 40 kg, not "child vs adult."

  • Inhaled-steroid candida: rinse + spacer — both required, both highlighted.

"Oral = First Sign" — The Dentist's Diagnostic Window

When the mouth gets you to the diagnosis before the medical workup catches up
  • Koplik spots appear 1–2 days BEFORE the measles rash — you may diagnose measles before the pediatrician.
  • Chancre on lateral tongue — primary syphilis often presents to dentistry first (4% of chancres are oral).
  • Mucous patches + split papules — secondary syphilis before the patient knows they have a systemic illness.
  • Oral CMV ulcer in HIV+ off ART — reactivation marker; failed acyclovir is the case clue.
  • EBV MCU — marker of immunosuppression in transplant / iatrogenic / age-related populations.
  • Keratinized vesicle-ulcers in a 6-mo-to-5-yr child — PrHGS, often the family's first encounter with HSV.
  • White plaques that wipe off after inhaler use or antibiotic course — pseudomembranous candida (early diagnostic clue for inhaler misuse).
  • Large irregular painful tongue ulcer + night sweats + cough — TB, not SCC (TB has no raised, induced borders).

Impetigo

  • Staph aureus / β-hemolytic strep. Most common bacterial skin infxn in 2–5 yo (case was 7yo)
  • Risk triad: Broken skin + Runny nose + Warm moist climate
  • Lesion: Honey-colored crusts = "Cornflakes glued to surface"; multifocal on erythematous base
  • vs Herpes: Impetigo = multifocal CRUSTS  |  Herpes = grouped VESICLES — both on erythematous base
  • Topical (limited dz): Mupirocin 2% TID × 5 days (exfoliate first w/ warm cloth)
  • Systemic (extensive): anti-staph/strep oral × 7 d  —  Augmentin: <40 kg = Amox 25 mg/kg/d + 125 mg clav ÷ 2 doses
  • ≥40 kg: Amox 500 mg + 125 mg clav  |  Cutoff = WEIGHT, not age

Syphilis 1° (Primary)

  • Pathogen: T. pallidum spirochete ("Cursed corkscrews"). DPH REPORTABLE
  • Chancre onset: 3–90 days post-exposure. Sites: 85% genital / 10% anal / 4% oral
  • Oral chancre: painless, indurated, clean base, regional LAD → "THE GREAT IMITATOR"
  • DDx: SCC, aphthous, TUG, Mpox (Sully: induced border=SCC; clean+indurated=chancre)
  • Biopsy: IHC sens 64–94% vs silver stain 0–41%
  • Serology NEG up to 6 weeks → biopsy trumps serology early
  • Tx: IM Pen G × 1 dose (refer to PCP). Heals spontaneously 3–8 wks

Syphilis 2° (Secondary / Disseminated)

  • 4–10 wks post-infection (after chancre resolves). Systemic: painless LAD + rash + MSK pain + sore throat
  • Oral triad: Split papules (commissure ulcerated mass)  |  Mucous patches (white elevated, converge serpentine)  |  Condyloma lata (look like papillomas)
  • ⚠ Oral lesions HIGHLY INFECTIOUS — serpentine patches on buccal/tongue

Syphilis 3° (Tertiary)

  • Latency 1–30 years; 30% of untreated 2° progress
  • Oral: Gumma (destructive granulomatous mass — palate/tongue) + Interstitial glossitis
  • Systemic: Cardiac (arteritis, aneurysm, CHF) + CNS (Tabes dorsalis posterior column, paresis, dementia)

Tuberculosis (Oral / Secondary)

  • Pathogen: M. tuberculosis airborne. Oral TB = rare; Tongue >>> most common oral site; ALWAYS secondary to pulmonary
  • Lesion: chronic painful IRREGULAR ulcer + constitutional sx (wt loss, night sweats, cough)
  • ⚠ DO NOT BIOPSY FIRST — workup + airborne PPE first (refer ID). Sully: "protect yourself first"
  • Test: NAAT = Xpert MTB/RIF assay. Biopsy AFTER workup
  • DOT Regimen: Phase 1 8 wk = Pyrazinamide + Isoniazid + Rifampin + Ethambutol (PIRE)
  • Phase 2 16 wk = Isoniazid + Rifampin. Oral TB self-resolves with systemic tx
  • ⚠ DPH REPORTABLE (Sully: "Syphilis + TB = both mandatory reportable")

Primary Herpetic Gingivostomatitis (PrHGS)

  • Age: 6 months – 5 years (NO dedicated pediatric case; in Garden Variety table)
  • Prodrome: cervical LAD + chills + fever + nausea + anorexia + irritability
  • Lesion: Pinhead vesicles → coalesce → ulcer; location: KERATINIZED tissue
  • KEY: PrHGS = KERATINIZED  |  Aphthous = NON-keratinized
  • Duration: 5 days – 2 weeks
  • Adults: pharyngotonsillitis; rarely Bell Palsy (facial nerve) / vestibular neuritis

Recurrent Herpes Labialis (RHL)

  • Prevalence: 15–45% of US. = cold sore = fever blister
  • Prodrome: itching / tingling / warmth / erythema — 6–24 hrs
  • Vesicles rupture + crust within 2 days
  • ⚠ HIGHLY transmissible at vesicle rupture — avoid manipulation until scabbed

Hutchinson Sign / HZO — EMERGENCY

  • Hutchinson sign = VZV lesion on nasal tip → V1/nasociliary nerve → corneal risk
  • HZO complications: Corneal ulceration → scarring → perforation → BLINDNESS
  • ⚠ Nasal tip HSV/VZV → MANDATORY ophthalmology referral. Ambulance-level urgency.

CMV (HHV5) Oral Ulcer

  • Context: HIV+ off ART. Case: 36yo female — large necrotic ulcers, failed 10-day acyclovir
  • ⚠ Acyclovir DOES NOT WORK on CMV — case clue for CMV over HSV
  • Biopsy: "Owl eye" nucleus (pathognomonic histology)
  • Tests: ELISA → IgG + IgM + viral culture + PCR + CD4/viral load
  • Tx: Valganciclovir 900 mg PO BID (duration not stated on slide). Immunocompetent: NSAIDs
  • Prevalence: 83% of population (older age, women, low SES, foreign birthplace)
  • #1 cause nonhereditary sensorineural hearing loss

Measles (Rubeola)

  • Transmission: airborne. Case: 6yo unvaccinated — fever 103°F + cough + rhinorrhea + conjunctivitis
  • Oral: Koplik spots = PATHOGNOMONIC — bluish-white pinpoint plaques on BUCCAL MUCOSA, 70% of pts
  • Appear 1–2 days BEFORE rash — key early diagnostic sign
  • 3 C's: Cough + Coryza (rhinorrhea) + Conjunctivitis
  • Rash: head → spreads down (cephalocaudal). Photophobia present
  • Infectious window: 4 days BEFORE to 4 days AFTER rash onset
  • PEP: MMR within 72 hrs OR Ig within 6 days
  • Vit A: 200,000 IU for children ≥12 months (hospitalized children)
  • Complication: encephalitis 1/1,000
  • ⚠ DO NOT TREAT IN DENTAL CLINIC — N-95 + negative pressure room mandatory. DPH REPORTABLE.

Mumps (Endemic Parotitis)

  • Case: 20yo college student — bilateral parotid swelling, fever, sour food pain, trismus, orchitis
  • 10–30% have NO parotid swelling (counterintuitive!)
  • Serious complications (orchitis, meningitis, encephalitis) can occur WITHOUT parotitis
  • CNS affected: ~50% (even without parotitis)
  • Gold standard Dx: RT-PCR from buccal/oral swab
  • IgM caveat: may FAIL in vaccinated patients despite active infection
  • Contagious: 1–2 days before sx → 14 days after clinical resolution; saliva virus: 7d before to 9d after sx onset
  • Incubation: 16–18 days (range 12–25)
  • Orchitis: 3–10% postpubertal males / Subfertility: 13%
  • Hearing loss / encephalitis: now <1% (prevaccine era = major)
  • 3rd MMR dose in outbreak: 61–88% additional protection
  • Management: avoid SOUR FOODS (sialagogue); analgesics/antipyretics; bed rest; warm/cool compresses

EBV Mucocutaneous Ulcer (MCU)

  • Case: 46yo post-renal transplant (tacrolimus + mycophenolate × 3 yrs) — rapidly enlarging ulcer, B-symptoms
  • Frame: "Localized, self-limited lymphoproliferative disorder with excellent prognosis"
  • ~45% regress spontaneously / NO disease-related deaths
  • 91% have immunosuppression: canonical 3 = methotrexate, azathioprine, cyclosporine (case used TAC + MMF)
  • Age-related type: median age ~77 years. Most common site: Oropharynx 62%
  • Appearance: sharply circumscribed, shallow ulcer. Mimics: malignancy, ANUG, traumatic ulcer
  • Dx: Biopsy + EBER in situ hybridization (EBER ISH) — required
  • Distinguish from lymphoma: Normal LDH + normal IL-2R + EBV DNA <1,000 copies/mL
  • Management: observe for regression OR reduce immunosuppression; chemo rarely needed; re-biopsy recurrences
