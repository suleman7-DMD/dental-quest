01 — Opening Quiz Case: 75-year-old, fire engine red gingiva S3

🎙️ Prof opened the lecture with this case and explicitly flagged it as exam material: "This is something that could show up on your exam." She said the verbatim phrase "generalized erythema and edema of the maxillary and mandibular facial gingiva" is expected exam phrasing.
⚠️ TIER 1 — EXAM TARGETED: This case tests plasma cell gingivitis vs OLP vs periodontal disease. The answer is avoidance of the trigger — NOT dexamethasone, NOT magic mouthwash, NOT SRP.

Case: 75-year-old male — new patient

Chief complaint: Generalized swollen, painful, bleeding gingiva × 6 months. Difficulty eating. Sores inside mouth and lips bleeding daily. S3

Med Hx: Prostate adenocarcinoma (brachytherapy 2015), HTN (amlodipine, lisinopril), anemia, inflammatory polyarthritis, GERD, Hepatitis C (treated), bladder calculus, IBS, epilepsy, overactive bladder, depression, PTSD.

Exam: Generalized erythema and edema of the maxillary and mandibular facial gingiva. Fire engine red throughout. No Wickham striae. No white reticular pattern. Uses Sensodyne toothpaste and cinnamon gum daily.

Answer Breakdown — Confirmed Exam Material

  1. Dexamethasone elixir — WRONG (OLP treatment, not contact hypersensitivity)
  2. Continue Sensodyne — WRONG (toothpaste is not the trigger here; gum/benzoates are)
  3. Scaling and root planing — WRONG (this is not periodontal disease)
  4. Avoidance of cinnamon and benzoate-containing products ← CORRECT
  5. Magic mouthwash — WRONG (treats pain, not the contact mechanism)
⚠️ DISTRACTOR TRAP: Dexamethasone is the reflexive "steroid for gingival inflammation" reflex. The differentiator: plasma cell gingivitis has fire engine red diffuse erythema with NO Wickham striae. OLP = bilateral symmetric + reticular white pattern. "Fire engine red" + cinnamon = plasma cell gingivitis. "Magic mouthwash is BS" for contact hypersensitivity. S3
⚠️ IBS ≠ IBD TRAP: IBS (irritable bowel syndrome) = functional GI disorder = NO oral manifestations. IBD (Crohn's/UC) = inflammatory = oral facial granulomatosis. This patient has IBS — it is a red herring in the case. Prof said this explicitly.

Verbatim phrasing expected on exam: "Generalized erythema and edema of the maxillary and mandibular facial gingiva." Say maxilla/mandible — not "jaw."

Tier 3 — Heck disease treatment detail (awareness only)

Heck disease management: imiquimod 5% cream applied 3×/week × 2–3 months (off-label oral use). TLR-7 agonist mechanism. May regress spontaneously — watchful waiting is also acceptable. No malignant potential — biopsy only if diagnosis uncertain.

04 — Leukoplakia, Erythroplakia & PVL S15 S17 S19 S24 S25 S26

⚠️ CRITICAL EXAM TRAP: Carcinoma in Situ IS cancer — NOT a pre-cancer. "Some people think that carcinoma in situ is a pre-cancer. It's not, it's cancer. It's just the second before it invades. It is cancer." S15

Leukoplakia — Definition S15

🎙️ WHO definition: "A predominantly white plaque of questionable risk having excluded other known diseases or disorders that carry no increased risk for cancer." Translation: "white patch I can't wipe off, can't blame on anything else." It is a diagnosis of exclusion. Must rule out: frictional keratosis, candidiasis, lichen planus, white sponge nevus.

Requires biopsy for definitive diagnosis. Clinical judgment alone has sensitivity 60% and specificity 62% for detecting cancer — you cannot reliably tell malignant from benign by eye alone. S15

Erythroplakia — The Dangerous One S19

🎙️ WHO definition: "A bright red velvety plaque that cannot be characterized clinically or pathologically as any other recognizable condition." Erythroplakia has TWO separate statistics: ~50% MT rate (eventual transformation) AND ~75–90% already show severe dysplasia, CIS, or invasive SCC at the very first biopsy (histology at presentation). Median time to cancer: 3.7 months. Do not watch and wait.
⚠️ ERYTHROPLAKIA TWO-STAT TRAP: The exam will separate these two numbers. "~50% MT rate" = eventual transformation probability. "~75–90% already severe dysplasia/CIS/SCC at first biopsy" = histology at presentation. Both are testable. Median time to cancer = 3.7 months (vs leukoplakia = 25 months). S19

Proliferative Verrucous Leukoplakia (PVL) S24 S25 S26

🎙️ Prof described PVL as having "the highest malignant transformation rate of any OPMD — 48–50%, approaching 100% over time." Female > male (F:M = 2:1), often non-smokers, gingiva most common site. "Ring around the collar" circumferential marginal gingival involvement is ominous. There is no curative treatment — only serial excision and lifelong surveillance.
⚠️ PVL RISK FACTORS FOR MT (triage): Non-homogeneous appearance → 4.2–6.5× higher risk · Lateral tongue leukoplakia → 12.7% MT vs 6.6% overall · FOM → 2.1× higher risk · Size >200mm² → 2–4× higher risk · Female sex paradox: higher MT despite lower overall incidence S17

Numbers to memorize: Hyperkeratosis only ~3% MT · Severe dysplasia >40% MT · 5-yr MT: no dysplasia 2.2% → mild 2.6–11.9% → mod 4.1–8.7% → severe 29.2–32.2% · 39.6% of progressors had NO dysplasia on first biopsy · Erythroplakia TWO stats: ~50% MT rate; ~75–90% already severe dysplasia/CIS/SCC at biopsy · Erythroplakia median to cancer 3.7 mo vs leukoplakia 25 mo · PVL 48–50% MT, 9.3%/yr, 8× higher · OSF 5.1% MT, ~36 mo · OLP/OLL ~1.4% · Clinical sensitivity 60%, specificity 62%

05 — Biopsy Technique & Site Selection S29 S30

🎙️ Prof gave specific biopsy rules that are directly exam-testable. Mnemonic she provided: "Red over white, edge over center, deep for verrucous, multiple for big."

Site Selection Rules S29

  • If there is a red component: biopsy the red. Mixed red/white lesions — the dysplasia/SCC is in the red. S17
  • Sample the thickest, most indurated, or most ulcerated zone if the lesion is non-homogeneous. S29
  • Avoid the necrotic center of an ulcer — sample the edge where viable epithelium meets ulcerated tissue. S29
  • Multiple incisional biopsies for large or non-homogeneous lesions — one site may miss the worst dysplasia. S29
  • Biopsy depth: 4–6 mm minimum. DOI ≥5 mm = already T2 staging. Superficial 2mm punch = artificially low DOI. S29
⚠️ DOI >4 mm = AUTOMATIC NECK DISSECTION: "If it's more than 4 millimeters, the patient automatically gets a neck dissection. So you need to go deep." DOI ≥5 mm → T2 staging. Biopsy technique has direct surgical staging consequences — punch to 4–6 mm depth. S29
⚠️ BRUSH BIOPSY IS NOT APPROPRIATE: "This is why brush biopsies don't work — it's not going to get the organization or the architecture. It will only get the cytology. I do not recommend that for anyone. No brush biopsies." Brush biopsy = cytology only — cannot assess architectural changes that define dysplasia. A negative brush biopsy does NOT rule out dysplasia. S29

Tumor seeding from biopsy is a myth. Incidence: ~0.00012% FNA, ~0.0011% core biopsy. Population registry study: no association between incisional biopsy and 5-yr survival in Stage I/II OSCC. Do NOT withhold biopsy out of fear of seeding. S29

Scalpel first, laser second: "Cut first with your scalpel, use the laser secondary for your bleeders. Give me a clean margin that doesn't have fried tissue on the ends because I can't tell you if it's cancer or not." Never use laser/cautery as the primary cutting instrument for a diagnostic biopsy. S30

⚠️ Verrucous lesion special case: A superficial biopsy of verrucous carcinoma will read as benign hyperkeratosis or "verrucous hyperplasia." The diagnosis depends on seeing the deep pushing border (not infiltrative). Biopsy must extend to the deep submucosa. S29
