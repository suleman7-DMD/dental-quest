## NOTECARD-WORTHY DISTILLATION (the "easy to forget" granular details)

These are the items that should populate the `Notecard-Worthy Highlights` section near the end of the HTML guide (PART 7). They are facts that the student "may know the concept but the associated critical details are easy to forget":

### Numbers that are easy to forget
- RAS variants: Minor 80% (3-10 mm, 7-14d) / Major 10-15% (1-3 cm, 2-6 wks, scarring) / Herpetiform 5-10% (1-3 mm, up to 100 ulcers, NOT HSV)
- OLP prevalence 0.1-2.2%, F:M 3:2
- MMP oral 85%, conjunctivae 61-70%
- PV PDAI: <15 mild / 15-45 moderate / >45 severe
- SJS <10% BSA / overlap 10-30% / TEN >30% BSA
- SJS mortality 1-5%, TEN 25-50%, PAMS 60-90%
- PAMS oral first 45%; NHL 40%, CLL 20%, Castleman 18%, solid tumors 16%
- Drug Gingival Hyperplasia: anticonvulsants ~50%, immunosuppressants 25-70%, CCBs 6-25%
- BMS COVID-related: pooled 2.9%, range 4-15%

### Pathognomonic anchors (one-liner each)
- Wickham striae bilateral posterior buccal = OLP
- Migratory nature = Geographic tongue
- Linear IgG/IgA/C3 at BMZ = MMP (subepithelial)
- "Chicken wire" intercellular IgG = PV (intraepithelial)
- Target lesions (3 rings) = EM
- Bilateral hilar adenopathy = sarcoid (NOT in this lecture but cross-ref)

### Drug lists that are pure memorization
- SJS/TEN top offenders (Board Favorite): TMP-SMX, β-lactams, FQs, AEDs (carbamazepine/phenytoin/lamotrigine/phenobarbital), allopurinol, NSAIDs
- Drug Gingival Hyperplasia: Phenytoin / Cyclosporine / Nifedipine-amlodipine
- PAMS associations (Board Favorite): NHL, CLL, Castleman, thymoma, solid

### Side-by-side "easy to confuse" pairs
- Type I (IgE, minutes, latex/penicillin/CHX) vs Type IV (T-cell, 24-72h, metals/acrylates/amalgam/flavorings)
- MMP (subepithelial, Nikolsky negative, linear IgG/IgA/C3) vs PV (intraepithelial, Nikolsky positive, chicken-wire IgG)
- EM (raised targets, HSV, skin prominent) vs RIME (mucosa prominent, Mycoplasma, skin minimal) vs SJS/TEN (drugs, flat macules→sloughing, BSA-tiered, mortality)
- True OLP (bilateral, posterior buccal, no atypia, waxes/wanes) vs Dysplasia w/ lichenoid (unilateral, high-risk site, bulbous rete pegs, progressive)
- DSG3 (mucosa, lower epi, anti-DSG3 → oral lesions) vs DSG1 (skin, upper epi, anti-DSG1 → skin only)
- RAS (nonkeratinized) vs HSV (keratinized)

### Foundational concept anchors
- Hypersensitivity I = IgE / IV = T-cell
- Subepithelial blister = Nikolsky negative; Intraepithelial = Nikolsky positive
- Patch testing = gold standard for Type IV
- Michel solution = transport medium for DIF specimens
- Corticosteroid potency: Group 1 (clobetasol/halobetasol 0.05%) > Group 2 (fluocinonide/betamethasone dipropionate 0.05%) > Group 4 (triamcinolone/betamethasone valerate 0.1%) > Group 6 (desonide). Dexamethasone elixir 0.5 mg/5 mL = unique systemic-mimic delivery.
