RAS (most common oral ulcer)
20% prev (5–66%); 39–50% lifetime; 80% before 30; 40% +FHx; children w/ affected parents = 90% chance. NOT HSV. CD8+ T-cell, ↑inflam cytokines, ↓IL-10. Genetic polymorphisms ↑ susceptibility.

vs HSV
RAS: no fever, no vesicles, nonkerat; HSV: fever, vesicles, keratinized (hard palate/ging).
Assoc
Celiac, IBD, Behçet, cyclic neutropenia, PFAPA, FMF, progesterone stomatitis, HIV, ↓B12/folate/iron/VitD/Zn, meds (NSAID/BB/bisphos/nicorandil).
Nutritional: screen + supplement only as needed
• New onset >40 → workup
• Complex aphthosis (≥3, near-constant) → 60% systemic
• No bx for typical recurrent ulcers w/ clear hx
• Bx if: single >4 wk, atypical, new >40
Tx mild
SLS-free TP. triamcinolone paste 0.1% QID; fluocinonide 0.05% gel under gauze 20 min 5×/d; dex 0.5 mg/5 mL 5 min QID; laser therapy (32.2% reduction vs placebo)
Tx mod–severe
clobetasol 0.05% gel; intralesional triam; prednisolone 15 mg/5 mL 5 mL hold 5 min then swallow BID
Refractory
azathioprine, colchicine, dapsone, thalidomide; TNF-α (adalimumab/etanercept/infliximab); apremilast (Behçet FDA); probiotics as adjunct (reduce pain w/ steroids)
AVOID silver nitrate (necrosis, argyria)
Workup: CBC, iron, B12, folate, VitD, celiac
Patient education: breaking the cycle may increase disease-free intervals

GEOGRAPHIC TONGUE — “3 D’s”
aka Erythema Migrans / Benign Migratory Glossitis
Depapillated + Demarcated (white serpiginous borders) + Dynamic (migrates).
3% prev; <30 yo; 12% FHx. Etiology: immune-mediated inflammatory.
Assoc: fissured tongue, psoriasis, atopy (asthma/eczema/hay fever/IgE ≥200), psych stress, GI, reactive arthritis
Mostly asx; if sx: burning, spicy/acidic/hot food sensitivity
Migration = pathognomonic (rules out malignancy)
DDx
Geographic: migrates, white serpiginous, benign · Candidiasis: white patches scrape off, antifungal-responsive · Leukoplakia: fixed, adherent, dysplasia on bx · Erythroplakia: fixed red, ↑malignant potential, urgent bx · OLP: bilateral reticular, doesn’t migrate, may erode · Median Rhomboid Glossitis: fixed rhomboid dorsal midline, candidal
Tx
Reassure; avoid triggers, avoid alcohol/mint/Sensodyne; kid’s TP; viscous lido 2%; diphenhydramine 12.5 mg/5 mL 5 min TID–QID; fluocinonide/clobetasol gel; tacrolimus 0.1% oint; acetaminophen
DRUG-INDUCED GINGIVAL HYPERPLASIA
Onset 1–3 mo. Begins interdental papillae → spreads. Anterior/facial worst. Severe: covers crowns, interferes w/ speech/mastication. Edentulous spared (unless dentures/implants).
Class%Drug
Anticonvulsant~50phenytoin
Immunosuppressant25–70cyclosporine
CCB6–25nifedipine, amlodipine
• Lower risk CCB: verapamil, felodipine; ACE/ARB safer
• Cyclosporine + nifedipine = synergistic
• RF: plaque, M (3×), <40 yo (children/adolescents highest risk), higher dose, smoking
Tx
1. Plaque control = most important; professional prophylaxis/SRP; CHX 0.12% BID–TID 5 min
2. Azithromycin 500 mg QD ×3 d OR metronidazole 250 mg TID ×7 d (esp cyclosporine); folic acid 0.5 mg QD prevention
3. Drug substitution (nifedipine → felodipine or → ACE/ARB) — regress 6–12 mo
4. Gingivectomy (scalpel/laser) — 35% recur in 18 mo

TRANSIENT LINGUAL PAPILLITIS (“lie bumps”)
Self-limited inflam of fungiform papillae. Tip/dorsolat tongue. 2–15 d (mean 7).
Triggers: trauma, stress, food, ?virus, GI, hormone, URI, contact hypersensitivity to foods, drinks, oral hygiene products
Variants: Localized (1–few, F > M, hrs–days) / Generalized eruptive (multi + fever/LAD/hypersalivation, ~7 d) / Papulokeratotic (multifocal asx w/ keratotic cap, chronic = friction hyperkeratosis?)
Tx: reassure; benzocaine; top steroid; cold

ORAL LICHEN PLANUS (OLP)
Chronic T-cell mucocutaneous, auto-inflam. Prev 0.1–2.2%; F:M 3:2; middle age.
Pathognomonic: bilateral posterior buccal muc + vent tongue + Wickham striae.

MUCOUS MEMBRANE PEMPHIGOID (MMP)
Autoimmune subepithelial blistering. Abs to BMZ (BP180, BP230, type VII collagen, laminin 332).
Incidence 2/million/yr; age 60–65; F slight
Nikolsky NEGATIVE (subepithelial = harder to shear). Not done routinely orally (traumatic, low yield).
Sites
• Oral 85%: desquamative gingivitis, erosions, intact bullae rare; may heal w/o scarring
• Conjunctiva 61–70%: burning/dryness/FB sensation → symblepharon, trichiasis → BLINDNESS
• Skin 25–30%: tense bullae
• Nasal 20–40%: crusting, epistaxis
• Laryngeal 5–10% (airway risk)
Dx
Bx perilesional; H&E (formalin) + DIF (Michel solution). DIF = linear IgG/IgA/C3 at BMZ. IIF salt-split skin; ELISA BP180/BP230. Anti-laminin 332 → 25% have malignancy (adenoCA).
“PemphigoiD lives DEEP”
Risk
Low = oral ± skin only / High = ANY other mucosa
Tx
• Oral only: clobetasol gel, dex rinse ± dapsone 50–100 mg/d; escalate: add prednisone 0.5 mg/kg/d → azathioprine or mycophenolate
• Ocular/high-risk: prednisone 1–1.5 mg/kg + dapsone or MMF; cyclophosphamide/rituximab/IVIG refractory

REFER ALL TO OPHTHO even if asx — scarring irreversible
PEMPHIGUS VULGARIS (PV)
Autoimmune intraepithelial blistering — anti-desmoglein. Ragged painful erosions on all oral surfaces; intact blisters rare (rupture immediately).
Nikolsky POSITIVE
>50% present orally first — dentist often sees first
Oral can precede skin by mo–1+ yr
Skin (variable): flaccid bullae rupture quickly
Bx perilesional + Michel’s
DIF: “chicken wire” intercellular IgG
Early diagnosis = easier treatment = lives saved
Serology (DSG1/DSG3 ELISA) — “Pegasus flies through clouds”
TypeAnti-DsgSites
Mucosal-dominant PVDsg3 onlyOral only
Mucocutaneous PVDsg3 + Dsg1Oral + skin
FoliaceusDsg1 onlySkin only, NO oral
PDAI severity
• Mild <15 (oral only): get OM on board; clobetasol 0.05% gel under gauze 20 min BID–TID; dex 0.5 mg/5 mL 5 min BID–TID
• Mod 15–45: rituximab + prednisone 0.5 mg/kg — derm STAT
• Severe >45: rituximab + prednisone 1–1.5 mg/kg — ED time

Bx BEFORE tx; apply non-bx areas immediately, bx area 5–7 d after

• Abx: sulfonamides (TMP-SMX), β-lactams, fluoroquinolones
• Anticonvulsants: carbamazepine, phenytoin, lamotrigine, phenobarbital

• Onset 1–3 wk after new med
Clinical
Prodrome (fever/malaise/sore throat) → eryth macules trunk → flaccid bullae → “scalded” + diffuse oral erosions + hemorrhagic lip crust + conjunctivitis (50% perm scarring). Nikolsky +.
SCORTEN
(1 pt each): age ≥40 / malignancy / HR >120 / BSA >10% / BUN >28 / glucose >252 / bicarb <20
0–1 = 3% / 2 = 12% / 3 = 35% / 4 = 58% / ≥5 = 90%
Dentist role
Recognize → STOP DRUG → refer ED/burn unit → document meds + start dates. Do NOT manage in dental office.
Supportive: magic mouthwash, lido 2%, CHX 0.12%, petroleum jelly to lips (frequent, prevent adhesions), gentle supragingival cleaning, saline irrigation to soften crusts. AVOID: aggressive debridement, sulfonamides
PNP / PAMS
Rare (~500 reported), always w/ neoplasm (known or occult). PAMS = preferred term (affects multiple organs, not just skin). Mortality 60–90%, most die in 1st yr. Oral first sign in 45% — may precede cancer dx. Oral lesions are first to show and last to go.
5 phenotypes
Lichen planus-like, pemphigus-like (classic), pemphigoid-like, EM-like, GVHD-like
Assoc malignancy
NHL ~40%, CLL ~20%, Castleman ~18% (Asia, benign but triggers PNP), thymoma, solid tumors ~16%
Features
• Oral ~100%: severe intractable stomatitis, painful tongue ulcers (more common than PV), lip erosions to vermilion + hemorrhagic crusting (SJS-like), pharyngeal/laryngeal/esoph
• Other mucosal: genital (erosions/ulcers), nasal (crusting/erosions), GI tract
• Skin: polymorphous (mimics PV/BP/EM/LP/GVHD); palmar/plantar bullae uncommon in PV = distinguishing
• Ocular 41–70%: cicatrizing conjunctivitis; symblepharon, fornix shortening, vision impairment
• Resp = MAJOR COD: bronchiolitis obliterans (autoabs vs resp epithelium; progressive dyspnea, cough; 6% Eur / 20% Japan / 70% peds, more w/ Castleman) — often irreversible
3 Causes of Death
Underlying malignancy • bronchiolitis obliterans • sepsis from immunosuppression
2021 Criteria
Need all 3 major OR 2 maj + 2 min:
Major: mucosal lesions ± cutaneous / neoplasm / anti-plakin Abs
Minor: acantholysis ± lichenoid histo / DIF intercellular ± BMZ
Prognosis
Rituximab less effective than for PV. Better prog: Castleman (resectable), lichenoid pattern, oral-only initial.
Dentist role
Recognize → refer OM/derm/onc → trigger cancer workup (PNP may be 1st sign of occult malignancy)
Supportive: dex 0.5 mg/5 mL, clobetasol/fluocinonide 0.05%, magic mouthwash, lido 2%, CHX 0.12%, lanolin or petroleum jelly, nutrition referral. Avoid crunchy / spicy / acidic / hot foods.
