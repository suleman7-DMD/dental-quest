EPI · Burden & Prognosis
US: 36.6k oral cancers/yr, 12.2k deaths. >50% present with mets at diagnosis. Tongue #1 site (42%). Incidence ↑1.8%/yr overall, F ↑2.3%/yr (rising faster in women, esp. tongue). 7th most common ca worldwide.

HPV BENIGN LESIONS · No Malignant Transformation
Squamous papilloma
HPV 6/11. Pedunculated cauliflower. Soft palate > lateral tongue. M>F. Solitary. Recur ~2%. Tx: simple excision.
Condyloma acuminatum
HPV 6/11 (75% HPV+ — highest HPV positivity of the 4). Sessile multiple pink/white. STD; in children consider abuse vs perinatal vs autoinoculation. Tx: excision ± imiquimod.
Verruca vulgaris
HPV 2/4 (cutaneous types). Sessile keratotic. Children. Autoinoculation from hand warts. Tx: excision/cryo.
Heck disease (multifocal epithelial hyperplasia, MEH)
HPV 13/32 — NOT in Gardasil 9 vaccine coverage. Native American / Inuit predominance. Children & teens. Multiple soft pink papules (lower lip / buccal / tongue). Self-resolves. Tx: imiquimod 5% topical 3×/wk × 2–3 mo or CO2 laser.
LEUKOPLAKIA · Clinical Term for Dysplasia
White plaque that cannot be wiped off. Dx of exclusion. DDx: friction keratosis, candida (scrapes off), lichen planus, white sponge nevus, other identifiable white lesions. All persistent leukoplakia → biopsy.

The 39.6% paradox
39.6% of oral cancers arise from leukoplakia WITHOUT dysplasia on initial bx → absence of dysplasia does NOT rule out future malignancy. Re-bx and surveillance still indicated.
ERYTHROPLAKIA · Red & Velvety = Worst OPMD
Red velvety plaque; may sit at slightly lower level than surrounding mucosa. FOM, lateral tongue, soft palate. 75–90% are already CIS or invasive SCC at biopsy. MT rate 50%. Median time to cancer 3.7 mo (vs leukoplakia 25 mo). URGENT referral — biopsy / excise NOW.
DDx (must rule out): atrophic glossitis, median rhomboid glossitis, plasma cell gingivitis, erosive lichen planus.

MT risk multipliers
Non-homogeneous (mixed red/white) leukoplakia 4.2–6.5× baseline → biopsy the RED component. Site: lateral / ventral tongue, FOM highest (tongue LP 12.7% MT). Size >2 cm: 2–4×. Female sex paradox (higher MT despite less exposure). Any dysplasia on bx: 2.75×.
PVL · Proliferative Verrucous Leukoplakia
F > M (2:1), classically non-smoker, gingiva-predominant. "Ring around the collar" = circumferential gingival involvement = ominous sign. MT 48–50% (some series 70–100%) — 8× higher MT than conventional leukoplakia.
Diagnostic criteria (Cerero-Lapiedra 2010)
Major: ≥2 oral sites · enlargement or new lesions during follow-up · recurrence after treatment
Minor: total size ≥3 cm · female · non-smoker · disease course >5 years
📸 Slide images: two views of tongue PVL — (L) diffuse white verrucous/pebbly plaque across dorsal & lateral surface; (R) same region showing progressive thickening with mixed white/pink areas suggesting early malignant transformation.
BIOPSY · When & How
  • All persistent leukoplakia → biopsy (clinical exam alone sens 60%, spec 62%).
  • Erythroplakia → biopsy / excise NOW.
  • Persists >2–3 wk after irritant removal → biopsy.
  • Site choice: red > white, nodular > flat, ulcer. For ulcer → biopsy the margin + adjacent intact epithelium (centre is necrotic).
  • Always include adjacent normal tissue.
  • Tumor seeding from biopsy = MYTH — do not delay biopsy. FNA: ~0.00012%; core bx: ~0.0011%. No association between incisional bx and 5-yr survival in Stage I/II OSCC.
  • Punch 4–6 mm; scalpel/incisional if equivocal, need depth of invasion (DOI), or verrucous suspected.
  • No survival impact from incisional biopsy.
Procedural sequence (slide)
Confirm patient healthy → explain → consent → time-out → topical → anesthetize → confirm anesthesia → proceed. Hemostasis: gauze, suture, silver nitrate, cautery.

ORAL SUBMUCOUS FIBROSIS (OSF)
Areca / betel quid chewing (South / Southeast Asian). Clinical: trismus, burning, blanching of buccal mucosa, palpable fibrous bands. MT 5.1%, median 36 months.
Mechanism (4 pathways — slide-highlighted)
(1) Epithelial atrophy → deeper carcinogen penetration · (2) Hypoxia → HIF-1α/2α upregulation · (3) Oxidative stress → DNA damage · (4) Mechanical stress → EMT activation.
Tx: cessation, physiotherapy / mouth-opening exercises, intralesional steroid; surgical release for advanced trismus.
ORAL LICHEN PLANUS (OLP)
Chronic relapsing T-cell mediated mucocutaneous disease. Bilateral and symmetric. Wickham striae (lacy white reticulation). Distribution: buccal > tongue > gingiva. Desquamative gingivitis presentation common. Clinical forms: white reticular ± atrophic / erosive / ulcerative. MT 0.2–1.4%.
ORAL LICHENOID LESION (OLL)
Lacks classic OLP features — often unilateral / asymmetric, atypical distribution.
3 subtypes by trigger
  • Contact lichenoid — dental restoration / amalgam contact.
  • Drug-induced — NSAIDs, antihypertensives, antimalarials, etc.
  • GVHD-associated — post-allogeneic HSCT.
MT 1.2–1.4%; with dysplasia 5.1%. Workup: remove the trigger; if persists 6–8 wk → biopsy. Ask pathologist: "Was architectural dysplasia assessed?"

ORAL SQUAMOUS CELL CARCINOMA (SCC)
>90% of oral malignancies. 7th most common cancer worldwide. US: 58.5k cases + 12.2k deaths/yr. Median age 66 yr. M:F 2–4:1.
📸 Slide images (75-yr-old case, 2024): (1) advanced lateral tongue SCC — large exophytic mass with ulcerated hemorrhagic surface; (2) earlier stage showing diffuse erythroplakic change across dorsal/lateral surfaces; (3) protruded tongue with large erythroleukoplakic patch, mixed red-white, irregular margins, surface necrosis.
SCC RISK FACTORS
  • Tobacco + alcohol = 70–75% of cases (multiplicative effect).
  • Betel / areca: OR 9.9.
  • Prior cancer: 16% ↑ risk of second primary.
  • HSCT recipients: SIR 7–16.
  • FDR with HNC: OR 1.7–3.8.
  • Fanconi anemia: OR 40.6.
  • HPV — oral cavity ~2–4% (DON'T routinely test); oropharynx 60–70% (DO test — p16 IHC).
  • HPV+ OPSCC 5-yr OS: ~81% vs ~40%; 3-yr OS: 82% vs 57% for HPV−.
  • HPV+ OSCC (oral cavity) = NO survival benefit.
  • Diet: Mediterranean ↓ 43%; Western ↑ 62%.
LIP SCC
25–30% of all oral cancers. Lower lip: 3× UV exposure vs upper. Vermilion border lacks melanin, thin stratum corneum → minimal protection. Actinic cheilitis (AC) → SCC MT 10–30%. 90% of AC patients never used lip sun protection. F < M (lipstick hypothesis — lipstick = de facto sunscreen, p=0.0008). Lip protection >1×/day halves lip cancer risk; physical + chemical protection ↓ AC by 33–36%. Prevention: SPF 30+ broad-spectrum lip balm q2h, brimmed hat, avoid sun 10a–2p.
📸 Slide images: three lip photos — (L) diffuse erythema, crusting, atrophy of lower lip vermilion with blurred border (actinic cheilitis); (R-top) focal crusted ulcer on lower lip; (R-bottom) larger crusted/ulcerated lesion with irregular margins (established lip SCC).
CLINICAL PRESENTATION
Site distribution: lateral / ventral tongue 42% > FOM 16% > soft palate.
Early: erythroleukoplakia, asymptomatic.
Advanced: ulcer with rolled / indurated borders OR exophytic mass.
~50% of SCCs are NON-ulcerated at the time of biopsy — do not let absence of an ulcer reassure you.
Red flags → biopsy: persistent >2 wk, erythroplakia, induration, fixation to underlying tissue, bleeding.

Referral packet — what to include
Lesion description (size, color, morphology, induration), clinical photo, exact location, duration, evolution, risk factors (T+A, betel, HPV, immune status, FHx), prior biopsy report if any, current medications, allergies.
Oncology workup
H&P + scope, biopsy / FNA, CT/MRI primary + neck, chest CT, ± PET-CT, dental evaluation: Panorex + pre-RT extractions (preserve mandibular dentition before radiation).
HPV+ OPSCC · Patient Profile
Younger (~54 yr), male, non-smoker, cystic neck nodes, small or occult primary (often base of tongue / palatine tonsil). Dx: p16 IHC positive (only valid marker in oropharynx). Better prognosis than HPV-negative OPSCC.
GARDASIL 9 · HPV Vaccine
Coverage: HPV 6 / 11 / 16 / 18 / 31 / 33 / 45 / 52 / 58 (9-valent).
Schedule
  • Ages 9–14: 2 doses (0, 6–12 mo).
  • Ages 15–26: 3 doses (0, 1–2, 6 mo).
  • Ages 27–45: shared decision-making.
Efficacy (slide-highlighted — corrected pair)
88% reduction in oral HPV infections (overall).
80% reduction in HPV-16 specifically (the OPSCC-driving type).
Emerging concept
Adjuvant Gardasil 9 after HPV-related diagnosis — investigated as a post-treatment adjunct (not yet standard of care).
VERRUCOUS CARCINOMA
Low-grade SCC variant — 2–3% of oral cancers. Slow exophytic cauliflower. Virtually never metastasizes. Demographics: 67–70 yr, slight male predominance (female if snuff dipping). Buccal mucosa 59% > vestibule > alveolar ridge. Sources: smokeless tobacco, betel, or arises from PVL in non-smokers.
Behavior: 0% nodal metastasis, 0% distant metastasis. 5-yr OS 78–89%. Local recurrence 7–38%.
Treatment: Wide local excision (WLE) ALONE — NO neck dissection, NO RT (no benefit).
Pearl — deep / excisional biopsy required; superficial bx misses the diagnosis (looks like benign hyperplasia on shallow specimens).
ORAL MELANOMA
Rare and deadly. <1% of all melanomas; ~0.5% of oral cancers. 5-yr survival 20–34% (vs cutaneous melanoma ~90%). UV is NOT a risk factor.
Demographics: 60–70 yr, M:F 1.5:1.
Distribution: hard palate 50% > maxillary gingiva 30%.
Appearance: brown / black macule or nodule. 15% are amelanotic — worse prognosis (delayed dx).
Molecular: c-KIT mutations common; BRAF rare → poor response to BRAF-targeted therapy used in cutaneous melanoma.
Late diagnosis + rich vascular / lymphatic supply → early metastasis.
Treatment: WLE; immunotherapy less effective than in cutaneous melanoma; neck dissection only if cN+.
Pearl — biopsy ANY new or changing pigmented lesion of palate / gingiva in an older adult; do not assume racial pigmentation.
