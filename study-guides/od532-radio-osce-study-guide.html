Oral Radiology — Three Radiographic Mimics

Three classic features the OSCE will hand you: cervical burnout at the premolar necks, the dental papilla at the apex of a developing molar, and the lamina dura outlining a recent extraction socket.

Opening Anatomy Quiz

Three radiographs, three arrows, three structures. Read each radiograph from top → middle → bottom and match it to the answer below.

Three Classic Radiographic Mimics

Top · Middle · Bottom
  1. Top image — Cervical Burnout Anatomy / Optical artifact

    The arrows in the top image point to diffuse radiolucencies at the mesial and distal necks of the premolars. This is a radiographic artifact or optical illusion caused by the normal anatomical concavities at the cementoenamel junction (CEJ). Because there is less tooth mass in this gap between the highly dense enamel crown and the level of the alveolar crest, the X-ray beam penetrates more easily, creating a radiolucent band that can sometimes be misidentified as root caries.

  2. Middle image — Dental Papilla Anatomy / Developing tooth

    The arrow in the middle image points to the radiolucent area at the apex of the developing molar. Because this tooth is still undergoing root formation (open apex), the radiolucency represents the connective tissue of the dental papilla, which will eventually form the dentin and pulp of the root. It is contained within the developing bony crypt.

  3. Bottom image — Lamina Dura Anatomy / Post-extraction socket

    The arrow in the bottom image points to the distinct radiopaque line outlining the empty tooth socket of a recently extracted tooth. This is the lamina dura (the radiographic term for the alveolar bone proper or cribriform plate). Following an extraction, the residual lamina dura remains visible until the socket heals and the bone fully remodels.
