LECTURE 2 — Infections (1-18)

1. Impetigo

Clinical Appearance

Honey-colored (golden-yellow) crusts on an erythematous base around the nose and mouth — “cornflakes glued to the surface.” Bilateral perinasal distribution on SKIN (not mucosa). Occurs in children 2-5 years old.

What Causes It (Simply)

S. aureus + beta-hemolytic streptococci invade broken skin. The “Biodome formula”: broken skin + runny nose + warm moist environment = perfect bacterial breeding ground. The bacteria multiply under the crust, which is why you must exfoliate before applying antibiotics.

Differentials

  • Recurrent HSV-1 — vesicle clusters, not broad honey crusts
  • Angular Cheilitis — limited to commissures only
  • Contact Dermatitis — erythema/scaling, no golden crusts, responds to removing irritant

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The golden honey crusts are pathognomonic — nothing else looks like this. HSV has vesicle clusters (tiny blisters), impetigo has broad golden crusts. Also: impetigo is on SKIN around the nose, HSV is on the vermilion border/keratinized mucosa. If you see “honey” or “cornflakes” in a question, it’s impetigo.

2. Primary Syphilis (Chancre)

Clinical Appearance

Single, round/oval painless ulcer with a clean base, indurated (firm) rolled borders, and a white border ring. Looks “too clean.” Button-like firmness on palpation. Regional painless lymphadenopathy. Lateral tongue is the classic oral site.

What Causes It (Simply)

Treponema pallidum (spirochete, “cursed corkscrews”) enters through mucosa during sexual contact. The chancre is the body’s local immune response at the inoculation site. It self-heals in 3-8 weeks, but the disease silently progresses to secondary syphilis without treatment. Only IM Penicillin G cures it.

Differentials

  • OSCC (SCC) — also lateral tongue, rolled borders, but SCC never self-heals, has necrotic granular base
  • Oral TB — TB is PAINFUL with papillary/irregular borders; syphilis is PAINLESS with clean base
  • Traumatic ulcer — has identifiable cause (sharp cusp), soft, heals in 2 weeks after removing cause
  • Aphthous ulcer — aphthous is PAINFUL, shallow, yellow fibrin base, on NONkeratinized tissue

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The magic word is PAINLESS. Syphilis chancre is the only common oral ulcer that is painless + indurated + clean base. If the question says “painless ulcer on lateral tongue,” think syphilis first. But you MUST biopsy to rule out SCC (which can also be painless early). Serology can be negative for 6 weeks — don’t rely on it early.

3. Secondary Syphilis

Clinical Appearance

Three oral findings: (1) Split papules at lip commissures (cracking/splitting), (2) Mucous patches — serpentine/snail-track white patches on mucosa, (3) Condyloma lata — flat moist gray-white plaques. Plus diffuse maculopapular rash on palms/soles, painless LAD, sore throat, fever, weight loss.

What Causes It (Simply)

4-10 weeks after the primary chancre, T. pallidum disseminates through the bloodstream (spirochetemia). This is the MOST CONTAGIOUS stage because lesions are teeming with active spirochetes. It’s called “The Great Imitator” because it mimics so many other diseases.

Differentials

  • Angular cheilitis — commissures only, NO other oral or systemic findings
  • Lichen planus — LP has Wickham striae (lace-like), not serpentine/snail-track
  • Leukoplakia — no serpentine pattern, no systemic findings

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The key is MULTIPLE findings. Angular cheilitis = commissures ONLY with nothing else. If commissure fissures come with mucous patches elsewhere + a palm/sole rash + systemic symptoms, it’s secondary syphilis. The serpentine/snail-track pattern of mucous patches is unique to syphilis.

4. Tertiary Syphilis

Clinical Appearance

(1) Gumma: destructive granuloma that creates a literal HOLE in the hard palate (perforation). (2) Interstitial glossitis: bald, lobulated tongue with deep furrows, loss of papillae.

What Causes It (Simply)

1-30 years after initial infection, the immune system mounts a granulomatous response that destroys tissue. This is immune-mediated damage, not high-burden active infection. The gumma eats through palatal bone.

Differentials

  • NK/T-cell lymphoma (midline lethal granuloma) — similar palatal destruction, but syphilis has positive serology
  • MRG (Central Papillary Atrophy) — MRG is a red smooth patch, not furrowed/lobulated across the entire tongue

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Hole in the palate = tertiary syphilis in this course. It’s the ONLY condition in these lectures that perforates palatal bone. If you see palatal perforation + history of STI years ago, that’s your answer.

5. Oral Tuberculosis

Clinical Appearance

Deep, non-healing ulcer on the posterior lateral tongue with papillary/irregular (“ragged”) borders and surrounding edema. PAINFUL. Patient has constitutional symptoms: night sweats, weight loss, persistent cough. Pano may show calcified cervical lymph nodes.

What Causes It (Simply)

Mycobacterium tuberculosis is an airborne pathogen. The oral ulcer is a secondary manifestation of pulmonary TB. The granulomatous immune response (caseating granulomas) creates the tissue destruction. It’s a mandatory reportable disease.

Differentials

  • SCC — clinically nearly IDENTICAL on posterior tongue. Biopsy required to differentiate, but do medical workup for TB FIRST
  • Primary syphilis — syphilis = PAINLESS + clean + indurated; TB = PAINFUL + papillary + irregular
  • Deep fungal infection — differentiated by stains (TB = AFB stain, fungal = PAS/GMS)

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Two distinguishers: (1) PAINFUL with ragged/papillary borders (syphilis is painless with clean borders), (2) SYSTEMIC symptoms (cough, night sweats, weight loss). Critical rule: do NOT biopsy before medical workup — aerosolizing TB endangers everyone. If a question adds cough + night sweats to a tongue ulcer, it’s TB and the answer is “refer for medical workup first.”

6. Pseudomembranous Candidiasis (Thrush)

Clinical Appearance

White curd-like/cottage cheese plaques on oral mucosa (palate, tongue, buccal) that CAN be wiped off, revealing a red/raw erythematous base underneath. Burning sensation.

What Causes It (Simply)

Candida albicans overgrowth. Normally kept in check by bacteria and immune system. Triggers: inhaler use without spacer/rinsing, antibiotics disrupting flora, immunosuppression (HIV), xerostomia. The white plaques are colonies of fungal hyphae mixed with debris.

Differentials

  • Hyperplastic candidiasis — does NOT wipe off
  • Leukoplakia — does NOT wipe off
  • Mucous patches (syphilis) — serpentine pattern, different clinical context

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The WIPE TEST is everything. If it wipes off = pseudomembranous candidiasis. Treat empirically with antifungals, NO biopsy needed. If it does NOT wipe off, you cannot distinguish hyperplastic candidiasis from leukoplakia and MUST biopsy.

7. Erythematous Candidiasis

Clinical Appearance

Red flat macules/patches (NOT white) on posterior hard palate, buccal mucosa, or dorsal tongue. Highest burning of all candidiasis types. Often under a denture or in HIV/immunosuppressed patients.

What Causes It (Simply)

Same Candida overgrowth as thrush, but the immune response produces erythema (inflammation) rather than white pseudomembranes. The redness = tissue inflammation from fungal invasion.

Differentials

  • Erythroplakia — erythroplakia is velvety, fixed, on high-risk sites, 75-90% severe at first biopsy. Candidiasis responds to antifungals
  • Thermal burn — burn has history of hot food, heals quickly
  • MRG — MRG is specifically midline posterior tongue, rhomboid shape

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Red patch + responds to antifungals = candidiasis. Red patch + fixed + high-risk site + doesn’t respond = erythroplakia (biopsy immediately). The antifungal trial is diagnostic — if it clears up, it was candidiasis.

8. Central Papillary Atrophy / Median Rhomboid Glossitis (MRG)

Clinical Appearance

Well-demarcated, red, depapillated (smooth) rhomboid-shaped patch at the exact midline of posterior dorsal tongue. Check the palate directly above for a “kissing lesion” (matching red patch).

What Causes It (Simply)

Chronic Candida infection at the junction of anterior 2/3 and posterior 1/3 of the tongue. The persistent fungal colonization causes papillary atrophy (loss of filiform papillae) in a fixed rhomboid pattern. The “kissing lesion” on the palate = fungal spread from tongue-to-palate contact.

Differentials

  • Geographic tongue — geographic tongue MIGRATES (changes location), has white keratotic borders. MRG is FIXED
  • Erythematous candidiasis — broader distribution, not specifically midline rhomboid
  • SCC — SCC on tongue is typically lateral, not midline

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Three keys: (1) MIDLINE (not lateral), (2) FIXED (doesn’t move like geographic tongue), (3) KISSING LESION on palate above. If the red patch on the tongue is NOT midline or is migrating, it’s not MRG.

9. Angular Cheilitis

Clinical Appearance

Red, fissured, cracked skin at BOTH commissures (corners of the mouth). May have slight white candidal component in the depths of the fissures.

What Causes It (Simply)

Candida albicans colonizes the commissures where saliva pools. Often caused by loss of vertical dimension (missing teeth, old dentures) creating deep skin folds at the corners where moisture collects. Also: B2/iron deficiency, drooling.

Differentials

  • Secondary syphilis (split papules) — syphilis has OTHER findings (mucous patches elsewhere, palm rash, systemic symptoms)
  • Impetigo — impetigo has golden honey crusts, not red fissures
  • Nutritional deficiency — B2/iron deficiency looks similar, check labs

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Angular cheilitis = commissures ONLY, nothing else. The moment there are OTHER oral or systemic findings (mucous patches, palm rash), upgrade your suspicion to secondary syphilis. Isolated commissure fissures + denture wearer = angular cheilitis.

10. Denture Stomatitis

Clinical Appearance

Red inflammation on the palate that EXACTLY conforms to the outline of the denture — sharp geometric cutoff at the denture margin. Ranges from smooth erythema (Type I) to papillary/granular (Type III).

What Causes It (Simply)

Candida colonizes the porous acrylic surface of the denture. The denture traps the fungus against the palatal tissue, creating a chronic infection. The denture itself is a fomite (contaminated object) that must be treated simultaneously.

Differentials

  • Erythematous candidiasis — more diffuse, NOT confined to denture outline
  • Nicotinic stomatitis — has white keratotic dots (inflamed minor salivary glands)
  • Erythroplakia — no denture correlation, velvety, concerning for malignancy

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The sharp geometric border matching the denture is pathognomonic. If the red area has an exact denture-shaped border, it’s denture stomatitis. Must soak denture in antifungal overnight — treating only the mucosa without treating the denture = guaranteed reinfection.

11. Hyperplastic Candidiasis

Clinical Appearance

White plaque that CANNOT be wiped/scraped off, typically on anterior buccal mucosa near the commissure (bilateral). Looks identical to leukoplakia clinically.

What Causes It (Simply)

Candida hyphae invade deeper into the epithelium, creating a keratotic plaque that is physically embedded in the tissue (unlike pseudomembranous which sits on top). The fungal invasion stimulates hyperkeratosis.

Differentials

  • Leukoplakia — clinically IDENTICAL. Must biopsy. Hyperplastic may respond to antifungals; leukoplakia won’t
  • Pseudomembranous candidiasis — pseudomembranous WIPES OFF

How to Not Get Mixed Up

You literally cannot tell it from leukoplakia without a biopsy. The rule: white plaque that doesn’t wipe off = MUST BIOPSY. Even if it responds to antifungals, still biopsy because Candida and dysplasia can coexist.

12. Primary Herpetic Gingivostomatitis (Primary HSV-1)

Clinical Appearance

Widespread oral ulcers covering ANY mucosal surface (keratinized AND non-keratinized) + fiery red, swollen gingiva. Child patient, febrile, drooling, refuses to eat, cervical LAD. Ulcers have yellow-gray base on red background.

What Causes It (Simply)

HSV-1 (HHV-1) first infection, typically in children 6 months - 5 years. The virus enters through mucosa, replicates massively (no prior immunity), causing widespread tissue destruction + systemic immune response (fever, LAD). After resolution, virus establishes latency in the trigeminal ganglion forever.

Differentials

  • Recurrent HSV — recurrent is LOCALIZED to keratinized tissue only, no systemic symptoms
  • Herpangina (Coxsackie) — posterior only (soft palate, tonsillar pillars)
  • Aphthous (RAS) — spares keratinized tissue, no gingival involvement, no fever
  • Erythema Multiforme — EM has target lesions on skin, bloody lip crusting

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The giveaway is GINGIVAL INVOLVEMENT + WIDESPREAD + CHILD + FEVER. Aphthous NEVER involves gingiva and has no fever. Recurrent HSV is localized, not widespread. If a child has ulcers everywhere including the gingiva plus systemic symptoms, it’s primary HSV.

13. Recurrent HSV-1 (Herpes Labialis / Intraoral)

Clinical Appearance

Cluster of tiny pinhead vesicles on KERATINIZED tissue (vermilion border, attached gingiva, hard palate) that coalesce on an erythematous base. Preceded by tingling/burning prodrome (6-24h). Vesicles rupture → ulcer → crust. Total course: 5 days to 2 weeks.

What Causes It (Simply)

HSV-1 reactivates from the trigeminal ganglion (where it’s been latent since primary infection). Triggers: stress, sun, trauma, illness. The virus travels down the nerve and replicates in epithelial cells at the nerve’s endpoint, which is always keratinized tissue.

Differentials

  • RAS (Aphthous) — aphthous = NON-keratinized tissue + no vesicles + no prodrome
  • Primary HSV — primary is widespread + systemic; recurrent is localized
  • Impetigo — honey crusts on perinasal skin, not vesicle clusters on lip

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Three rules: (1) KERATINIZED tissue only (hard palate, gingiva, lip), (2) VESICLE → ulcer sequence (aphthous has no vesicle stage), (3) CLUSTER pattern (grouped, not scattered). If ulcers are on buccal mucosa or soft palate, they’re NOT HSV — they’re aphthous.

14. Herpes Zoster Ophthalmicus / Hutchinson Sign (VZV)

Clinical Appearance

Vesicles in a UNILATERAL dermatomal band following V1 (ophthalmic division of trigeminal). DOES NOT CROSS MIDLINE. Hutchinson Sign = vesicles on the TIP of the nose (nasociliary branch involvement). Severe lancinating pain, often BEFORE lesions appear.

What Causes It (Simply)

VZV (HHV-3) reactivates from the trigeminal ganglion (same as chickenpox virus, dormant since childhood). Reactivation follows a single dermatome. V1 involvement threatens vision because the nasociliary branch also innervates the cornea.

Differentials

  • HSV-1 on nose — HSV doesn’t follow strict dermatomal pattern, less severe pain, recurs more often. But BOTH threaten vision if on the nose.

How to Not Get Mixed Up

VZV = UNILATERAL + DERMATOMAL + DOES NOT CROSS MIDLINE + SEVERE PAIN. HSV clusters don’t follow dermatomes this strictly. But the clinical takeaway: whether HSV or VZV, if vesicles are near the nose/eye = STAT ophthalmology referral. Don’t waste time distinguishing — ACT on the vision threat.

15. CMV (Cytomegalovirus / HHV-5)

Clinical Appearance

Necrotic ulcer(s) with punched-out or irregular borders and erythematous (red) halo, at multiple oral sites. ONLY in immunocompromised patients (HIV/AIDS, transplant). Histopath: “Owl eye” intranuclear inclusions.

What Causes It (Simply)

CMV (HHV-5) is carried by 83% of the population but only causes disease when the immune system is severely weakened (CD4 <100 in HIV). The virus infects endothelial cells and macrophages, causing tissue necrosis.

Differentials

  • EBV-MCU — EBV-MCU = immunoSUPPRESSED (drugs/age), typically vestibule/gingiva, self-limited
  • Major aphthous — aphthous patient is immunocompetent
  • HSV in immunocompromised — HSV ulcers are persistent but less necrotic

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Key diagnostic clue: Acyclovir failure. If you treat a presumed herpes ulcer with Acyclovir and it doesn’t respond in an immunocompromised patient, think CMV. CMV lacks the thymidine kinase Acyclovir needs. Treatment = Ganciclovir/Valganciclovir. Also: “owl eye” on biopsy is pathognomonic.

16. Measles (Rubeola) / Koplik Spots

Clinical Appearance

Koplik spots: blue-white pinpoint macules (“grains of salt”) on erythematous base on buccal/labial mucosa. Appear 1-2 days BEFORE the skin rash. Patient has the 3 C’s: Cough, Coryza, Conjunctivitis + high fever (103°F).

What Causes It (Simply)

Measles virus (paramyxovirus), one of the most contagious diseases known (R0 = 12-18). Airborne transmission. Virus remains viable in air for 2 hours. The Koplik spots represent viral replication in buccal mucosa epithelial cells — they’re pathognomonic for measles.

Differentials

  • Fordyce granules — Fordyce are yellow-white, larger, are normal sebaceous glands
  • Pseudomembranous candidiasis — confluent white plaques, not pinpoint dots

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Blue-white PINPOINT dots on buccal mucosa + sick child with 3 C’s = Koplik spots = measles. Nothing else presents as tiny blue-white dots. Critical action: DO NOT treat in dental clinic. Airborne isolation + report immediately.

17. Mumps

Clinical Appearance

BILATERAL symmetric parotid gland swelling — face looks swollen below the ears, below and anterior to the angle of the mandible. Lifts earlobes, obscures mandibular angle. Tender. Fever, malaise. Pain worsened by sour foods.

What Causes It (Simply)

Paramyxovirus infects the parotid glands via respiratory droplets. Targets salivary gland epithelial cells. Typically affects college-age young adults because MMR immunity wanes 15-20 years after vaccination. Complications: orchitis (3-10%), meningitis, hearing loss.

Differentials

  • Sjögren syndrome — also bilateral parotid, but chronic/gradual, dry eyes, middle-aged women, autoantibodies
  • Bacterial sialadenitis — usually UNILATERAL, purulent discharge from duct
  • Sialolithiasis — UNILATERAL, meal-related (pain peaks when eating)

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Bilateral + acute + young adult + fever + sour food exacerbation = mumps. Bilateral + chronic + dry eyes + older woman = Sjögren. Unilateral + meal-related = stone (sialolithiasis). Unilateral + pus from duct = bacterial.

18. EBV-Positive Mucocutaneous Ulcer (EBV-MCU)

Clinical Appearance

Large, sharply demarcated necrotic ulcer in buccal vestibule extending to gingiva in an immunoSUPPRESSED (iatrogenic) patient. Rolled/indurated borders. Usually solitary.

What Causes It (Simply)

Immunosuppressive drugs (transplant meds, methotrexate) or age-related immunosenescence suppress T-cell surveillance. Without T-cell control, EBV-infected B cells proliferate unchecked locally, creating a reactive ulcer. It is NOT cancer — it’s localized immune dysregulation. Reducing immunosuppression restores T-cell function and resolves it.

Differentials

  • CMV — CMV = immunoCOMPROMISED (HIV), multiple sites, owl-eye inclusions
  • SCC — similar appearance, biopsy required
  • Lymphoma (DLBCL) — EBV-MCU looks IDENTICAL to lymphoma on biopsy. Must check EBER ISH

How to Not Get Mixed Up

CMV vs EBV-MCU: CMV = immunoCOMPROMISED (HIV), EBV-MCU = immunoSUPPRESSED (drugs/age). The critical trap: biopsy shows atypical large B cells that look like lymphoma — do NOT start chemo. Check EBER ISH first. EBV-MCU has 0% disease-related deaths; giving chemo for a reactive process would be harmful.

LECTURE 3 — Immunologic / Allergic (19-36)

19. RAS — Minor Aphthous

Clinical Appearance

3-10mm shallow ulcer with yellow-white fibrinopurulent membrane surrounded by erythematous halo, on NONkeratinized mucosa (buccal, labial, ventral tongue, soft palate, FOM). Painful. Heals 7-14 days, no scarring. 80% of all RAS.

What Causes It (Simply)

Multifactorial immune dysregulation: triggers (stress, trauma, hormones, foods, SLS toothpaste) cause TNF-alpha to go up and IL-10 to go down, leading CD8+ T cells to attack epithelial cells, causing apoptosis and ulceration. NOT viral. Strong genetic component (both parents with RAS = 90% chance for child).

Differentials

  • Recurrent HSV — HSV = KERATINIZED tissue, starts as vesicle, may have systemic signs

How to Not Get Mixed Up

TISSUE TYPE is everything. RAS = nonkeratinized (buccal, labial, soft palate, FOM). HSV = keratinized (hard palate, gingiva, lip). “100 tiny ulcers on buccal mucosa” = herpetiform aphthae (NOT herpes) because the location is nonkeratinized.

20. RAS — Major Aphthous

Clinical Appearance

1-3 cm, DEEP ulcer on nonkeratinized mucosa. Heals 2-6 weeks, MAY SCAR. Very painful. 10-15% of RAS.

What Causes It (Simply)

Same mechanism as minor RAS but deeper immune-mediated tissue destruction. The ulcer penetrates deeper into the lamina propria, which is why it scars.

Differentials

  • SCC — both are large persistent ulcers. Key: major RAS has recurrent history since childhood, erythematous halo, nonkeratinized tissue. SCC has rolled/indurated borders, risk factors, older patient

How to Not Get Mixed Up

If a large deep ulcer is recurrent (patient has had them since childhood), on nonkeratinized tissue, with an erythematous halo = major aphthae. If it’s fixed, indurated, rolled borders, in an older smoker = biopsy for SCC. Any ulcer >2 weeks = biopsy to be safe.

21. RAS — Herpetiform

Clinical Appearance

Up to 100 tiny (1-3mm) pinpoint ulcers on nonkeratinized mucosa that coalesce into larger irregular ulcers. Female predominance. 5-10% of RAS.

What Causes It (Simply)

Same as other RAS subtypes. The name “herpetiform” refers only to the APPEARANCE (many tiny ulcers resembling herpes), NOT the etiology. This is NOT a herpes infection.

Differentials

  • Recurrent intraoral HSV — HSV = keratinized tissue + starts as vesicles. Herpetiform RAS = NONkeratinized + no vesicle stage

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Despite the name “herpetiform,” this is NOT herpes. The location tells you: nonkeratinized tissue (buccal, labial) = RAS. Keratinized tissue (hard palate, gingiva) = HSV. The name is the exam trap.

22. Geographic Tongue (Benign Migratory Glossitis)

Clinical Appearance

Depapillated red patches on the dorsal tongue surrounded by serpiginous (snake-like) white borders. The defining feature: the pattern MIGRATES — changes shape and location over days/weeks. The 3 D’s: Depapillated, Demarcated, Dynamic.

What Causes It (Simply)

Unknown etiology, likely immune-mediated. Associated with atopy (elevated IgE), psoriasis. The papillae are lost in patches due to localized inflammation that shifts location over time. Completely benign — no malignant potential.

Differentials

  • Erythroplakia — erythroplakia is FIXED (doesn’t migrate), no white borders, high-risk
  • MRG — MRG is fixed, midline, no white borders
  • Erythematous candidiasis — responds to antifungals, broader distribution

How to Not Get Mixed Up

MIGRATION is pathognomonic. No other oral lesion moves around. If the red patch was in a different spot last week = geographic tongue, benign, reassure. If the red patch is FIXED and doesn’t move = NOT geographic tongue → consider erythroplakia and biopsy.

23. Transient Lingual Papillitis

Clinical Appearance

Inflamed, swollen fungiform papillae on the tip/dorsum of the tongue. Painful little bumps. Resolves in 2-15 days (mean 7).

What Causes It (Simply)

Likely local irritation, viral, or immune-mediated. “Lie bumps” in common language. Self-limiting and benign.

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Short-lived, self-resolving bumps on the tongue tip. If persistent or growing, consider other pathology. These are “easy points” — unlikely to be confused with anything serious because they resolve so quickly.

24. Drug-Induced Gingival Hyperplasia

Clinical Appearance

Firm, fibrotic, lobulated gingival overgrowth starting at the interdental papillae, spreading laterally and coronally. Anterior/facial predominance. Spares edentulous areas. Mucosal-colored to pink (NOT red). Onset 1-3 months after starting drug.

What Causes It (Simply)

Three drug classes stimulate gingival fibroblasts to overproduce collagen: (1) Phenytoin (anticonvulsant, ~50%), (2) Cyclosporine (immunosuppressant, 25-70%), (3) Nifedipine/Amlodipine (CCBs, 6-25%). Poor oral hygiene is the #1 risk factor (plaque = inflammatory co-stimulus). Cyclosporine + Nifedipine together = synergistic severe hyperplasia.

Differentials

  • Plasma Cell Gingivitis (PCG) — PCG is “cherry red / fire engine red,” edematous, diffuse. Drug-induced is PINK, FIRM, starts at papillae
  • Leukemia infiltration — also gingival enlargement, but spongy, bleeds easily, patient is systemically ill

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Color + texture: drug-induced = PINK + FIRM. PCG = RED + GLOSSY. Starting point: drug-induced starts at papillae; PCG is diffuse. Classic trap: “Patient on Amlodipine with fire-engine red gingiva” = PCG (from cinnamon toothpaste), NOT drug-induced (which would be pink and firm). Also: Levetiracetam does NOT cause it (exam loves this swap with Phenytoin).

25. Oral Lichen Planus (OLP)

Clinical Appearance

Reticular: bilateral, symmetric, lacy white lines (Wickham striae) on posterior buccal mucosa and ventral tongue. Erosive: red, painful erosions + desquamative gingivitis. Waxes and wanes. Cutaneous LP has the 5 P’s: Purple, Polygonal, Pruritic, Planar, Papules.

What Causes It (Simply)

Autoimmune: CD8+ T cells target basal keratinocytes, creating a band-like lymphocytic infiltrate at the epithelial-connective tissue interface. Chronic, not curable — managed with topical steroids when symptomatic.

Differentials

  • Lichenoid contact reaction — UNILATERAL, adjacent to amalgam restoration
  • Dysplasia mimicking OLP — UNILATERAL, PROGRESSIVE, on high-risk site (lateral tongue, FOM)
  • Leukoplakia — no striae pattern, no bilateral symmetry

How to Not Get Mixed Up

BILATERAL + SYMMETRIC = OLP. UNILATERAL + next to amalgam = lichenoid reaction (remove restoration). UNILATERAL + lateral tongue + progressive = dysplasia (biopsy urgently). Dr. Woo paradigm: many “OLP transforming to cancer” cases were actually dysplasia from the start. “Check the other side of the mouth” = most important clinical maneuver.

26. Lichenoid Contact Reaction (OLL)

Clinical Appearance

White striae/patches that look like OLP but are UNILATERAL and directly adjacent to a restoration (typically amalgam). May have erythematous component.

What Causes It (Simply)

Type IV hypersensitivity (delayed T-cell reaction) to amalgam, composite, or other dental material in direct contact with mucosa. The immune reaction mimics OLP but is caused by a local trigger, not systemic autoimmunity.

Differentials

  • OLP — OLP is bilateral/symmetric; lichenoid is unilateral, next to the offending restoration
  • Dysplasia — dysplasia is progressive and on high-risk sites; lichenoid resolves after removing restoration

How to Not Get Mixed Up

UNILATERAL + ADJACENT TO RESTORATION = lichenoid. Remove the restoration, wait 2-3 weeks. If it resolves, it was lichenoid. If it persists after removing the trigger → mandatory biopsy (could be dysplasia).

27. Plasma Cell Gingivitis (PCG)

Clinical Appearance

“Cherry red / fire engine red” DIFFUSE gingival erythema. Edematous, glossy surface. Unlike drug-induced hyperplasia, it is red (not pink) and diffuse (not starting at papillae).

What Causes It (Simply)

Allergic/hypersensitivity reaction to cinnamaldehyde or benzoates found in toothpaste, gum, mouthwash, candy. Histology shows dense plasma cell infiltrate. Remove the allergen = resolves. Dexamethasone treats symptoms but doesn’t address the cause.

Differentials

  • Drug-induced gingival hyperplasia — firm, pink, lobulated, starts at papillae. PCG is red, diffuse, glossy
  • Desquamative gingivitis (MMP/PV/erosive OLP) — these are peeling/eroding, not uniformly red

How to Not Get Mixed Up

“Fire engine red” = PCG. The solution is allergen avoidance (cinnamon/benzoates), not steroids alone. Classic trap: patient on Amlodipine with fire-engine red gingiva — it’s PCG from cinnamon toothpaste, NOT drug-induced (which would be pink and firm).

28. Mucous Membrane Pemphigoid (MMP)

Clinical Appearance

Tense blisters on gingiva (intact, hard to rupture). Desquamative gingivitis (red, peeling gums). Nikolsky sign NEGATIVE. DIF: Linear IgG/IgA/C3 at BMZ. 61-70% ocular involvement (symblepharon → blindness).

What Causes It (Simply)

Autoantibodies (anti-BP180, BP230, Laminin 332) attack hemidesmosomes at the basement membrane zone (deep split). The deep split means the blister roof is thick epithelium → tense blisters. “PemphigoiD = D for Deep.”

Differentials

  • PV — PV = flaccid blisters, Nikolsky(+), chicken wire DIF, desmosomes (superficial split)
  • Erosive OLP — DIF shows shaggy fibrinogen, not linear band

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Tense blisters + Nikolsky(-) = MMP. Flaccid blisters + Nikolsky(+) = PV. Must do two-specimen biopsy (Formalin + Michel’s) for DIF. Special concerns: (1) ALWAYS refer to ophthalmology (61-70% ocular), (2) Anti-Laminin 332 → screen for adenocarcinoma (25% have underlying cancer).

29. Pemphigus Vulgaris (PV)

Clinical Appearance

Flaccid blisters that rupture almost instantly → you rarely see intact blisters, only ragged painful erosions. Nikolsky sign POSITIVE (gentle rubbing displaces epithelium). DIF: “Chicken wire” intercellular pattern. Oral lesions FIRST in 50% of cases.

What Causes It (Simply)

Autoantibodies (anti-Desmoglein 3 +/- Dsg1) attack desmosomes between epithelial cells (superficial/intraepithelial split = acantholysis). The thin blister roof ruptures immediately. Historically FATAL without treatment. “Pemphigus = high up (in the epithelium).”

Differentials

  • MMP — MMP = tense blisters, Nikolsky(-), linear DIF, deep split
  • PNP/PAMS — PNP doesn’t respond to steroids, associated with tumors
  • Erosive OLP — OLP has Wickham striae, different DIF pattern

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Flaccid + Nikolsky(+) + chicken wire DIF = PV. Critical rules: (1) Biopsy BEFORE starting steroids (steroids cause false-negative DIF), (2) Oral first in 50% — the dentist may be first to diagnose this potentially fatal disease. PV serology: Dsg3 only = oral only; Dsg3+Dsg1 = oral+skin; Dsg1 only = pemphigus foliaceus (skin only, NO oral).

30. Erythema Multiforme (EM) / RIME

Clinical Appearance

“Target” / “bullseye” lesions on extremities (acral distribution). “Jelly donut” hemorrhagic blood-crusted lips. Oral ulcers with erythematous base. Self-limiting (2-4 weeks). RIME = recurrent episodes.

What Causes It (Simply)

Infection-driven (~90%) — HSV is the #1 trigger, Mycoplasma pneumoniae #2. The infection triggers an immune complex reaction that causes vascular damage in the skin and mucosa, producing the characteristic target lesions. It is NOT a drug reaction (that’s SJS/TEN).

Differentials

  • SJS/TEN — SJS/TEN is DRUG-triggered (not infection), involves TRUNK (not extremities), has sheet-like sloughing (not target lesions), is a medical EMERGENCY
  • Primary HSV — no target lesions on skin

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Target lesions on EXTREMITIES + infection trigger = EM. Flat macules on TRUNK + drug trigger + skin sloughing = SJS/TEN. Both can have hemorrhagic lip crusting (“jelly donut”), but the skin lesion pattern and trigger are different. EM is self-limiting; SJS/TEN is potentially fatal. For recurrent EM (RIME): prophylactic Valacyclovir 500mg BID for 6+ months blocks the HSV trigger.

31. Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)

Clinical Appearance

Flat atypical macules on trunk → flaccid blisters → sheet-like skin sloughing. <10% BSA detachment. Hemorrhagic lip crusting. Mucosal erosions (oral, ocular, genital). Medical emergency.

What Causes It (Simply)

Drug-induced (1-3 weeks after starting the drug). High-risk drugs: Sulfonamides, Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Lamotrigine, Allopurinol (highest absolute risk), NSAIDs. Massive keratinocyte apoptosis mediated by cytotoxic T cells and Fas-FasL pathway. “Burn from inside out.”

How to Not Get Mixed Up

DRUG trigger + trunk + sloughing = SJS/TEN. INFECTION trigger + extremities + target lesions = EM. Action for SJS/TEN: STOP the offending drug immediately, transfer to ED/burn unit. SJS = <10% BSA, SJS/TEN overlap = 10-30%, TEN = >30% BSA.

32. Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)

Clinical Appearance

Same as SJS but >30% BSA detachment. Full-thickness epidermal necrosis. Mortality up to 90%. Patient looks like a burn victim.

What Causes It (Simply)

Same drug-induced mechanism as SJS, just more extensive. Same drug culprits. HLA-B*15:02 in Han Chinese patients dramatically increases risk with Carbamazepine — pharmacogenomic screening saves lives.

How to Not Get Mixed Up

SJS vs TEN is purely a matter of BSA percentage: <10% = SJS, 10-30% = overlap, >30% = TEN. Same disease spectrum, same drugs, same management (stop drug, ICU). TEN is just more severe and more lethal.

33. Paraneoplastic Pemphigus / PAMS (PNP)

Clinical Appearance

Severe, painful oral erosions with hemorrhagic lips. Looks like PV + LP + EM combined (polymorphous). Palmar/plantar bullae (rare in regular PV). Does NOT respond to standard steroid treatment (“rally pack failure”).

What Causes It (Simply)

An underlying malignancy produces autoantibodies against multiple epithelial antigens (envoplakin, periplakin, desmoplakin, Dsg3). NHL (~40%), CLL (~20%), Castleman disease (~18%). The tumor drives the immune dysregulation — treating the immune system without addressing the tumor is futile. Bronchiolitis obliterans (irreversible small airway destruction) is the primary cause of death.

Differentials

  • PV — PV responds to steroids; PNP does not. PV has no underlying malignancy

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Pemphigus that doesn’t respond to steroids = PNP = cancer workup. Other clues: palmar/plantar bullae (rare in regular PV), polymorphous appearance, progressive dyspnea (bronchiolitis obliterans). 60-90% mortality.

34. Type I Hypersensitivity (IgE-mediated)

Clinical Appearance

Immediate reaction (minutes to hours): urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm, anaphylaxis. In the oral setting: swelling of lips/tongue/throat after exposure to allergen.

What Causes It (Simply)

IgE-mediated immediate hypersensitivity. Prior sensitization creates IgE antibodies on mast cells. Re-exposure → mast cell degranulation → histamine release → immediate inflammation. Common dental triggers: latex, penicillins, chlorhexidine (CHX).

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Type I = IMMEDIATE (minutes-hours) + IgE. Type IV = DELAYED (24-72 hours) + T-cell. If reaction happens on the dental chair during the appointment = Type I. If it develops days later = Type IV.

35. Type IV Hypersensitivity (Delayed/Contact)

Clinical Appearance

Delayed reaction (24-72 hours): contact dermatitis/mucositis at the site of exposure. Erythema, vesicles, pruritus. Gold standard diagnosis: patch testing.

What Causes It (Simply)

T-cell mediated delayed hypersensitivity. Sensitized T cells encounter the allergen and mount an inflammatory response over 24-72 hours. Common triggers: nickel, chromium, cobalt (metals in restorations), acrylates (denture base), flavorings (cinnamon). Note: most “local anesthetic allergies” are actually reactions to bisulfite preservative, not the amide molecule itself.

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Type IV is DELAYED and LOCALIZED to the contact site. Important: positive patch test = sensitization exists, but sensitization ≠ active causation. Must correlate with actual exposure. Lichenoid contact reactions are a Type IV response to amalgam.

36. Allergic Contact Cheilitis

Clinical Appearance

Inflammation, scaling, deep fissuring of the entire lip or perioral area after exposure to an allergen (lip balm, lipstick, toothpaste, food).

What Causes It (Simply)

Type IV hypersensitivity to a topical allergen applied to the lips. Common culprits: fragrances, preservatives, dyes in cosmetics and oral care products.

Differentials

  • Actinic cheilitis — actinic is on LOWER lip only, chronic UV damage, scaly, pre-cancerous
  • Angular cheilitis — angular is at COMMISSURES only, Candida-driven

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Contact cheilitis = entire lip/perioral, related to a product. Actinic = lower lip only, outdoor worker, UV damage. Angular = commissures only, denture wearer. Contact cheilitis resolves when the offending product is identified (patch testing) and avoided.

LECTURE 4 — Cancer / OPMDs (37-50)

37. Squamous Papilloma (HPV 6, 11)

Clinical Appearance

Pedunculated (on a stalk), cauliflower/finger-like projections, solitary. White to pink. Most common benign oral neoplasm. Mean age 48, male predominance. Soft palate and lateral tongue.

What Causes It (Simply)

HPV strains 6 and 11 infect squamous epithelial cells and cause benign exophytic proliferation. These are LOW-risk HPV strains with ZERO malignant potential. Treatment = simple excision at the base. 2% recurrence.

Differentials

  • Condyloma acuminatum — sessile (broad base), MULTIPLE, sexually transmitted
  • Verruca vulgaris — sessile, rough keratotic surface like a finger wart
  • Verrucous carcinoma — MASSIVE, warty, buccal vestibule, needs deep biopsy

How to Not Get Mixed Up

PEDUNCULATED (stalk) + SOLITARY = squamous papilloma. This is the only one of the HPV quartet that grows on a stalk. Sessile + multiple + sexual = condyloma. Sessile + rough/keratotic = verruca. Sessile + soft/flat/cobblestone = Heck disease.

38. Condyloma Acuminatum (HPV 6, 11)

Clinical Appearance

Sessile (broad-based), MULTIPLE pink/white papillary nodules. Labial mucosa, palate, tongue. Sexually transmitted (75% HPV+).

What Causes It (Simply)

Same HPV 6/11 as papilloma, but transmitted sexually (oral-genital contact). No malignant potential. Critical: child with oral/genital condyloma = mandated reporter. Rule out autoinoculation and perinatal transmission before concluding abuse.

How to Not Get Mixed Up

SESSILE + MULTIPLE + SEXUAL TRANSMISSION = condyloma. Papilloma is pedunculated and solitary. The child abuse reporting requirement is a high-yield exam point.

39. Verruca Vulgaris (HPV 2, 4)

Clinical Appearance

Sessile, white, rough keratotic surface — looks exactly like a common finger wart. Found in children/adolescents.

What Causes It (Simply)

HPV strains 2 and 4 (the common wart strains). Oral lesions from autoinoculation — child bites finger warts and transfers virus to oral mucosa. No malignant potential.

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The rough, keratotic surface like a hand wart + child/adolescent + history of finger warts = verruca vulgaris. HPV 2,4 (not 6,11) = NOT covered by Gardasil.

40. Heck Disease / Focal Epithelial Hyperplasia (HPV 13, 32)

Clinical Appearance

Multiple soft, flat, cobblestone-pattern papules on lower lip, buccal mucosa, tongue. Children, especially Native American/Inuit populations. In older adults = think HIV/immunosuppression.

What Causes It (Simply)

HPV strains 13 and 32 cause benign epithelial hyperplasia with genetic susceptibility. Unique strains that are NOT covered by Gardasil 9 (which covers 6,11,16,18,31,33,45,52,58). Treatment: observation first (self-limiting in children), then imiquimod 5% cream 3x weekly (TLR7/8 agonist).

How to Not Get Mixed Up

SOFT + FLAT + COBBLESTONE + HPV 13,32 = Heck disease. It’s called “Heck disease” (NOT “Heck’s”). The key exam point: Gardasil does NOT prevent Heck disease because it doesn’t cover strains 13 and 32.

41. Leukoplakia (Homogeneous)

Clinical Appearance

Sharply demarcated uniform white plaque that CANNOT be wiped off. “Little continent on a map.” Variable location (lateral tongue, FOM, buccal mucosa).

What Causes It (Simply)

p53 gene mutation causes abnormal epithelial maturation (dysplasia). Leukoplakia is a clinical descriptive term (what you see). Dysplasia is the pathologic diagnosis (what the biopsy shows). It is a diagnosis of exclusion — rule out frictional keratosis, candidiasis, lichen planus first. Median time to cancer: 25 months.

Differentials

  • Hyperplastic candidiasis — clinically identical. Must biopsy both
  • Frictional keratosis — adjacent to chronic irritant, resolves after removing it
  • Lichen planus — LP has striae pattern, bilateral
  • White sponge nevus — bilateral, familial, present since birth

How to Not Get Mixed Up

White plaque + can’t wipe off + doesn’t fit any other category = leukoplakia by exclusion. Always biopsy to determine if dysplasia is present. Non-homogeneous (mixed red/white) = 4.2-6.5x higher transformation risk than homogeneous white.

42. Erythroleukoplakia (Non-homogeneous Leukoplakia)

Clinical Appearance

Mixed red and white patches/plaques. Speckled, nodular, or verrucous subtypes. The red areas represent loss of keratin production and more aggressive genetic change.

What Causes It (Simply)

Same p53 mutation-driven dysplasia as leukoplakia, but more advanced. The red component indicates areas where the epithelium has lost its ability to keratinize — a sign of higher-grade dysplasia. 4.2-6.5x higher transformation risk than white alone.

Differentials

  • Homogeneous leukoplakia — all white, lower risk
  • Erythroplakia — all red, highest risk (75-90% severe at first biopsy)

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The hierarchy: Red > Mixed > White in terms of cancer risk. In a mixed lesion, BIOPSY THE RED PART — it has the highest-grade dysplasia. White part may just be hyperkeratosis.

43. Erythroplakia

Clinical Appearance

Well-demarcated velvety red macule/plaque, soft, “step-down”/depressed below surrounding tissue. Located on FOM, lateral tongue, soft palate (“Horseshoe of Death” sites).

What Causes It (Simply)

The most advanced OPMD. 75-90% already have severe dysplasia or invasive cancer at FIRST biopsy. The red color = complete loss of keratinization + increased vascularity from dysplastic/neoplastic tissue. Median time to cancer: 3.7 months (vs 25 months for leukoplakia). THE GOLDEN RULE: dysplasia/cancer until proven otherwise.

Differentials

  • Erythematous candidiasis — responds to antifungals; erythroplakia doesn’t
  • Geographic tongue — geographic MIGRATES; erythroplakia is FIXED
  • Thermal burn — history of hot food, heals quickly

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Fixed velvety red patch on a high-risk site (FOM, lateral tongue, soft palate) = EMERGENCY BIOPSY. Do NOT observe, do NOT wait, do NOT trial antifungals. The 3.7-month median means you are already potentially late. Red + fixed + high-risk site = act NOW.

44. Proliferative Leukoplakia (PVL/PL)

Clinical Appearance

Multiple white patches at 2+ distinct sites, most commonly on GINGIVA (“ring around the collar” = circumferential white marginal gingival involvement). Lesions enlarge and new ones appear over time. Progressive, recurrent after treatment.

What Causes It (Simply)

Field cancerization: the entire oral mucosa has widespread genetic failure. Multiple independent lesions arise across different sites. No single external carcinogen to remove (often NON-SMOKERS, older females). “Whack-a-mole” — treat one lesion, another appears. 48-50% transformation rate (highest of ALL OPMDs).

Differentials

  • Regular leukoplakia — single site, not progressive
  • OLP — OLP has striae, waxes and wanes; PL is progressive, doesn’t have striae

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Non-smoking older woman + multiple white patches + gingiva + progressive = proliferative leukoplakia. A single biopsy cannot diagnose PL (looks like regular leukoplakia under microscope) — the diagnosis requires clinical context: multifocal, progressive spread over time. Must send clinical photos with biopsy.

45. Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC)

Clinical Appearance

Early: looks like erythroleukoplakia, often completely ASYMPTOMATIC. Advanced: ulcer with raised rolled borders OR exophytic fungating mass. 50% NOT ulcerated at biopsy. Red flags: induration (hard on palpation), fixation (bound down), unexplained bleeding, numbness. Oral tongue = #1 site (41.7%).

What Causes It (Simply)

Accumulation of genetic mutations (p53, p16, etc.) in squamous epithelial cells, driven by tobacco + alcohol (70-75%, SYNERGISTIC), betel quid, HPV (minor role in oral cavity, major in oropharynx), inherited syndromes (Fanconi anemia OR 40.6). >50% present with regional/distant metastases at diagnosis because early disease is painless.

Differentials

  • Primary syphilis — syphilis is painless + clean + self-heals; SCC never self-heals
  • Oral TB — clinically similar on posterior tongue; TB has systemic symptoms
  • Traumatic ulcer — heals in 2-3 weeks after removing irritant; SCC doesn’t
  • Major aphthae — recurrent history since childhood, nonkeratinized tissue

How to Not Get Mixed Up

The 2-3 Week Rule: any ulcer persisting >2 weeks after removing the identifiable cause = biopsy. Induration + fixation = highest suspicion for invasive carcinoma. Early OSCC is painless — waiting for pain = waiting until it’s advanced. Screen tongue, FOM, and back of throat EVERY visit.

46. Verrucous Carcinoma

Clinical Appearance

Slow-growing, massive exophytic, cauliflower/warty white mass on buccal mucosa/vestibule/alveolar ridge (where snuff is held). Low-grade SCC (2-3% of oral carcinomas).

What Causes It (Simply)

Chronic carcinogen contact from smokeless tobacco (snuff dipping) at the habitual placement site. It’s a well-differentiated SCC with pushing (not infiltrating) margins. 0% metastasis rate — locally destructive only.

Differentials

  • Squamous papilloma — papilloma is small (<1cm), pedunculated; verrucous carcinoma is MASSIVE
  • Thick leukoplakia — leukoplakia is flat plaque; verrucous carcinoma is exophytic and warty

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Must do a DEEP biopsy — the surface looks benign (well-differentiated), and a superficial biopsy will miss the invasive front at the base. Surgery ONLY (NO radiation — radiation can induce anaplastic transformation to conventional SCC). 0% metastasis = no neck dissection needed. “Cancer with 0% metastasis” = verrucous carcinoma.

47. Oral Melanoma

Clinical Appearance

Pigmented lesion on hard palate (~50%) or maxillary gingiva (~30%). Asymmetric, irregular borders, color variation (brown/black/blue/red), diameter >1cm, evolving (ABCDEs). 15% AMELANOTIC (flesh-colored/pink — easily misdiagnosed). 5-year survival: 20-34%.

What Causes It (Simply)

NOT UV-driven (unlike skin melanoma). Caused by c-KIT mutations (not BRAF). Resistant to BRAF-targeted therapies that work for skin melanoma. Deadly because: (1) hidden location = late detection, (2) rich vascularity of oral mucosa = early hematogenous metastasis.

Differentials

  • Melanotic macule — uniform, symmetric, stable, small
  • Amalgam tattoo — adjacent to restoration, blue-gray, stable, radiograph shows metal
  • Physiologic pigmentation — bilateral, symmetric, stable since youth
  • Pyogenic granuloma — amelanotic melanoma can mimic this

How to Not Get Mixed Up

New/changing pigmented lesion on palate or gingiva in older adult = SAME-DAY BIOPSY. Don’t forget the 15% that have NO pigment (amelanotic). Amalgam tattoo is stable + adjacent to restoration + radiopaque on film. Physiologic pigmentation is bilateral + present since youth. Any ASYMMETRIC, EVOLVING, or NEW pigment = biopsy immediately.

48. Oral Submucous Fibrosis (OSF)

Clinical Appearance

Trismus (limited mouth opening), burning with spicy foods, blanching/stiffening of mucosa, palpable fibrous bands in the buccal mucosa and soft palate.

What Causes It (Simply)

Areca nut/betel quid chewing (South/Southeast Asian populations). The areca nut alkaloids stimulate fibroblasts to overproduce collagen in the lamina propria, causing progressive fibrosis. THE PARADOX: it’s a disease of fibroblasts that causes EPITHELIAL cancer (SCC) via toxic microenvironment (hypoxia + oxidative stress from compressed blood vessels). Cancer risk: 5.1%, median 36 months to cancer.

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Trismus + fibrous bands + South/Southeast Asian + areca nut = OSF. This is the only pathology in these lectures with palpable fibrous bands and progressive trismus. Management: stop areca nut, physiotherapy for trismus, intralesional steroids.

49. Actinic Cheilitis

Clinical Appearance

Silvery/scaly, atrophic LOWER lip with blurred/indistinct vermilion border. Chronic. In outdoor workers with chronic sun exposure.

What Causes It (Simply)

Chronic UV damage to the lower lip (which gets 3x more UV than the upper lip). UV causes cumulative DNA damage to epithelial cells. Pre-cancerous: 10-30% transformation rate to lip SCC. “Lipstick hypothesis”: lipstick provides UV barrier, explaining male predilection for lip SCC.

Differentials

  • Angular cheilitis — at commissures only, Candida-driven
  • Allergic contact cheilitis — entire lip, related to product, resolves with avoidance

How to Not Get Mixed Up

Scaly LOWER lip + outdoor worker + blurred vermilion border = actinic cheilitis (pre-cancerous). Angular cheilitis = commissures only. Contact cheilitis = related to a product. Actinic is the ONLY lip condition in these lectures that is pre-cancerous. Counsel male patients: SPF 30+ lip balm, reapply every 2 hours.

50. HPV-Positive Oropharyngeal SCC

Clinical Appearance

Tiny primary tumor in the tonsil or base of tongue (often hard to see) + MASSIVE cystic neck lymph nodes. Patient presents with a neck mass, and workup reveals a small tonsillar primary. Younger males, non-smokers.

What Causes It (Simply)

HPV 16 infects the tonsillar crypt epithelium. The virus drives malignant transformation via E6/E7 oncoproteins. 60-70% of oropharyngeal SCCs are HPV-positive. Essentially a sexually transmitted cancer. HPV+ oropharyngeal SCC has BETTER prognosis (81% 5-year survival) than HPV-negative, and testing with p16 immunostaining changes treatment decisions (de-escalation protocols).

Differentials

  • Oral cavity SCC — HPV is minor role (2-6%) in oral cavity. Do NOT test for HPV in oral cavity SCC (no management change)

How to Not Get Mixed Up

OROPHARYNX = test for HPV (p16), HPV+ = better prognosis. ORAL CAVITY = do NOT test, HPV status doesn’t matter. Different anatomic sites = different biology = different testing logic. SCC of tonsil (“HPV+, younger non-smoker, better prognosis”) vs SCC of tongue (“HPV irrelevant, older smoker/drinker, worse prognosis”).

1. Impetigo — Diagnostic Reasoning

  • Why exfoliate before mupirocin: crust = physical barrier. Removing exposes infection bed
  • Escalation: topical → systemic when widespread, recurrent, or not responding

2. Syphilis — Diagnostic Reasoning

  • Serology FAILS early: antibodies take ~6 weeks. Negative RPR/VDRL does NOT rule out
  • Must biopsy to rule out SCC (painless ulcer with rolled borders is on differential)
  • Biopsy needs 3 tissue zones: normal margin, transition zone, lesion
  • Disease progression: Primary (local) → Secondary (disseminated, MOST contagious) → Tertiary (granulomatous destruction)
  • Chancre self-heals but disease PROGRESSES. Only Penicillin G cures

3. Oral TB — Critical Safety

  • Do NOT biopsy before medical workup: creating open wound aerosolizes bacteria
  • Correct sequence: suspect → refer (CXR, sputum, QuantiFERON) → THEN biopsy after patient on treatment
  • NO steroids for TB: immunosuppression causes dissemination
  • DOT rationale: non-adherence breeds MDR-TB/XDR-TB

4. Candidiasis — Clinical Reasoning

  • Trigger triad: Inhaler without spacer + no rinsing + recent antibiotics
  • Nystatin for diabetics (no sugar). Clotrimazole CONTRAINDICATED (has sugar)
  • “Azole = Liver”: check LFTs before systemic azoles
  • Denture = fomite: treat mucosa AND denture simultaneously
  • When to biopsy: Hyperplastic (doesn’t wipe off) = MUST biopsy. Pseudomembranous/erythematous = clinical dx

5. HSV — V1 Vision Threat

  • V1 dermatome → cornea risk. Hutchinson sign = nasociliary branch = HIGH risk
  • Facial biopsy creates unnecessary scarring. Use PCR from vesicle fluid
  • Primary vs Recurrent: Primary = widespread, any tissue. Recurrent = ONLY keratinized
  • Treatment window: Must start within 72h. After that, switch to supportive care

6. CMV Reasoning

  • Acyclovir failure = diagnostic clue. CMV lacks thymidine kinase. Needs Ganciclovir (UL97 kinase)
  • Don’t biopsy hard palate (friable, poor healing, bone exposure risk)

  • We were misdiagnosing early cancers AS OLP

PNP/PAMS

OPMDs — Key Concepts

  • Brush biopsy inadequate: captures cytology, misses architecture. Dysplasia is an architectural diagnosis
  • Pain paradox: Pre-cancers are painless. Pain = you’re already late
  • CIS = cancer: fully malignant cells, just haven’t broken through BM yet
  • Leukoplakia = clinical term. Dysplasia = pathologic diagnosis. Rule of exclusion

Erythroplakia Urgency

  • 75-90% already severe at first biopsy — by the time you see it, you’re late

Proliferative Leukoplakia

Cross-Cutting: When to Biopsy vs Not

ALWAYS Biopsy

  • Erythroplakia (75-90% severe)
  • Suspected melanoma (new pigmented on palate/gingiva)
  • Any ulcer >2 weeks
  • Leukoplakia (grade dysplasia)
  • Mixed red/white (biopsy RED part)
  • Desquamative gingivitis (2 specimens)
  • Suspected verrucous carcinoma (DEEP biopsy)

NEVER/RARELY Biopsy

  • Geographic tongue (dynamic, migrating)
  • Physiologic pigmentation (bilateral, stable)
  • RAS minor (classic, recurrent, nonkeratinized)
  • Amalgam tattoo with radiographic confirmation
