Key Terms Cheat Sheet

OFP — Orofacial pain: any pain in face/mouth/jaws
TMD — Temporomandibular disorders: jaw muscle/joint pain
DC/TMD — Gold-standard clinical TMD exam protocol (2014)
PIFP — Persistent idiopathic facial pain: chronic face pain, no cause found
AO — Atypical odontalgia: phantom tooth pain, subtype of PIFP
Sensitivity — Can the test catch people who HAVE it?
Specificity — Can it correctly clear people who DON'T?
AUROC — Overall accuracy: 0.5=coin flip, 0.7+=good, 0.9+=excellent
PPV / NPV — If +, chance you have it / If -, chance you're clear
3Q/TMD — 3 yes/no screening Qs (Swedish dental system)
TMD Pain Screener — 6-item questionnaire by Gonzalez (DC/TMD system)
RCT — Root canal treatment (not randomized controlled trial)

Sensitivity = smoke detector going off when there's fire. Specificity = staying quiet when you're just cooking. High Se + low Sp = beeps at everything including burnt toast.

Real numbers from our lit review. • • • Patients with PIFP and atypical odontalgia wait nearly 35 months — almost 3 years — before getting a correct diagnosis. • • • One hundred percent received at least one wrong diagnosis first. • • • And 73% of AO patients underwent unnecessary irreversible dental procedures — root canals, extractions — on teeth that were never the problem. • • • The core issue: orofacial pain is not one disease. Narrow exams cause years of misdiagnosis, unnecessary surgery, and suffering.

SLIDE 4 — CAN QUESTIONNAIRES CATCH TMD? ~1.5 min
SAY THIS
First question: can screening questionnaires identify TMD? Four studies. • • • Gerstner 1994 — 13-item questionnaire. Up to 98% sensitivity, 100% specificity for TMD vs healthy controls. But could NOT differentiate TMD from tension-type headache. Great at catching TMD, blind to non-TMD. • • • Macfarlane 2004 — 1,500+ people UK. Model sorted OFP into three buckets: musculoligamentous 7%, dentoalveolar 7%, neurological/vascular 6%. Useful for epi, needs validation. • • • Lovgren 2016 — 3Q/TMD — three yes/no questions — 7,800+ Swedish adults. 74% of positives had TMD vs 16% of negatives. Simple, cost-effective, valid for flagging. • • • Gonzalez 2011 — developed the TMD Pain Screener: 99% sensitivity, 97% specificity, AUC 0.98–0.99. Near perfect. But — excluded tooth pain patients. Odontogenic group tested separately: 26–29% false positive. • • • Key pattern: screening tools excel at detecting TMD — but struggle to differentiate it from non-TMD pain.
Screeners catch TMD well. But they can't tell TMD apart from tooth pain, headache, or PIFP.

All 4 Studies

Study 1 — Gerstner (1994)

13-item Q. n=307. TMD vs controls: excellent. TMD vs headache: failed.

MEDIUM — old, no gold-standard reference
Study 2 — Macfarlane (2004)

UK, n=1510. Multinomial logistic regression, 6 predictors. Sorted OFP into 3 types. Kappa=0.5.

MEDIUM — only study classifying ALL OFP types
Study 3 — Lovgren (2016)

7,831 adults, routine checkups. 300 examined with DC/TMD. NPV 0.92-0.99 in general practice.

HIGH — huge real-world sample, validated vs DC/TMD
Study 8 — Gonzalez (2011)

ORIGINAL TMD Pain Screener. n=504. From 49 items to 6. Se 99%, Sp 97%. Excluded tooth pain. 26-29% false+ in odontogenic group.

HIGH — the screener everyone uses; its limitation is our argument
3Q/TMD = (1) Weekly jaw/face/temple pain? (2) Pain on opening/chewing? (3) Jaw locking? Yes to any = positive.
TMD Pain Screener = 6 items: pain duration, morning stiffness, 4 jaw activities that change pain. Score 0-7. Cutoff 3+ = positive.
SLIDE 5 — THE SPECIFICITY PROBLEM — MOST IMPORTANT ~1.5 min
SAY THIS — NAIL THIS SLIDE
This is the most important table in our presentation. We're tracking the same 6-question TMD Pain Screener across three studies, three populations. • • • Gonzalez 2011 — clean research groups, pre-sorted, tooth pain excluded: 99% sensitivity, 97% specificity, AUROC 0.98–0.99. Beautiful. • • • Fonseca Alonso 2017 — TMD vs odontogenic head-to-head. Same screener. Sensitivity ~92%, but specificity crashes to ~59%. Symptom overlap is enormous. • • • Daline 2024 — real endo patients with toothaches. Sensitivity 85%, specificity 52%, AUROC 0.71. 46% had DUAL etiology. • • • The takeaway: as patient complexity increases, specificity collapses. Designed in clean populations, loses half its specificity in the real world. A positive screen means "could be TMD or tooth pain mimicking TMD." Only a full clinical assessment resolves that. A screener flags — it doesn't diagnose.
Same test + harder patients = specificity drops 97% → 52%. Clean research ≠ real practice.

The Three Populations

Gonzalez (2011) — "Clean Lab"

Pre-sorted. Tooth pain excluded. Se 99%, Sp 97%. Artificial perfection.

Alonso (2017) — "Mixed"

n=71. TMD vs odontogenic. Sp ~59%. Too much symptom overlap.

Daline (2024) — "Real World"

n=100 consecutive endo. Sp 52%. 46% dual. Dual-board examiner.

Why Sp drops: Tooth pain and TMD both cause jaw aching, pain on chewing, morning stiffness. Patients endorse same items for either condition.
AUROC grades: 0.98=A+, 0.71=C+, 0.81=B+. The A+ only happened in artificial settings.
SLIDE 6 — SCREENERS MEET THE REAL WORLD ~1.5 min
SAY THIS
Three more studies reinforce this. • • • Daline 2024 — 100 endo patients, 54% had painful TMD but came in for "toothache." Screener alone: AUROC 0.71. Adding one data point — current pain intensity — AUROC jumped to 0.81. Even small clinical info improves the screen. Takeaway: over half of "toothaches" in endo may be TMD. A screener can flag before irreversible treatment. • • • Lovgren 2018 — same 3Q/TMD, now in a specialist Amsterdam clinic, 449 patients. Sensitivity high, specificity dropped dramatically. Referred patients have neuropathic pain, AO, chronic dental pain — they say "yes" to pain questions but don't have TMD. But negative responses reliably ruled OUT TMD. • • • Alonso's thesis at UMN — tested dental pain questionnaire AND TMD screener. Neither had acceptable specificity. Overlap too great. • • • The Screening Paradox: screeners catch TMD well, but orofacial pain overlaps so much that a positive screen still can't tell you the exact diagnosis. Comprehensive assessment remains essential.
The Screening Paradox: screeners detect, but only a full workup diagnoses. Like a smoke detector that beeps but can't tell you what's burning.

If You Get Questions

  • "What's DC/TMD?" — Gold standard (2014): jaw pain last 30 days + modified by function + reproduced on opening/palpation.
  • "Why is specificity low?" — Tooth and jaw pain feel the same to patients. Both ache, both hurt on chewing, both cause morning stiffness.
  • "What to do clinically?" — 6Q screener + pain NRS as intake triage. Positive = DC/TMD exam BEFORE irreversible procedures.
  • "Myalgia vs arthralgia?" — Myalgia = muscle pain (masseter/temporalis). Arthralgia = TMJ pain. Both = whole system involved.
  • "Why does 8% matter?" — RCT on healthy tooth → still hurts → retreat → still hurts → extract → STILL hurts. All because jaw was never examined.
